## II. PROSE QUALITY

### Scene vs. Summary

The chapter is approximately 85% scene, 15% summary. The summary passages earn their place:

- The march north (¶11–13): compresses the journey into physical details — frozen ruts, visible breath, canteen ice. Each sentence is a scene in miniature.
- January cold deepening (¶109–111): compresses weeks of attrition into specific images — men freezing at their posts, the scarves of ice.
- The march south (¶151–153): compresses the recovery into the body's unclenching.

No passage fails the deletion test. Every paragraph earns its place.

**Verdict: PASS**

### Prose Distance

The chapter modulates effectively:

- **Extreme close-up:** The cold entering the body (¶19), Tomás's face against Martín's neck (¶117), Ibarra's frozen eyelashes (¶39), the boy soldier's chattering teeth (¶83).
- **Middle distance:** The trench sequences, the Christmas Eve battle, the schoolroom.
- **Compressed lyric:** "He did not sit." (¶51), "The snow covered them slowly — first the boots, then the legs, then the torsos, then the arms, then the faces." (¶89).

No five consecutive paragraphs at the same distance. The modulation is deliberate and effective.

**Verdict: PASS**

### Interiority

The chapter's interiority is strong. Key passages:

- The cold as occupation (¶19): "First the extremities... Then the hands and feet... Then the arms and legs... Then the chest... Then the mind." This is rendered consciousness — the body's experience articulated with clinical precision. Level 5.
- Padre Joaquín's fear of sitting (¶49): "I think he was afraid that if he sat down, he would not get up." Martín's inference, not omniscience. Specific to this character at this moment. Level 5.
- The body's argument (¶19): "the body's argument that lying still was easier than standing up, that sleeping was easier than staying awake." Physical, specific, not named emotion. Level 4.
- Tomás's warmth (¶115): "His body was beside mine in the trench, his shoulder against mine, his blanket over both of us, his warmth the only warmth I knew." Concrete, physical, specific. Level 4.

No named emotions. No generic interiority. All interiority is body-anchored or behavior-anchored.

**Verdict: PASS**

### Dialogue

- Tomás speaks in character: "Get in." / "Stay awake." / "Stay awake, cura." — Blunt, Register 2 (The Brother). Correct for extreme cold. Later: "Let's go home" — quiet, Register 1 (The Companion) returning.
- Vidal speaks in character: "Dig deep. The deeper the warmer." — Register 1 (The Officer). "Men will die... Keep them moving." — Register 3 (The Realist). No ideology. Pure competence.
- Padre Joaquín speaks in character: "God receives you." — Register 1 (The Pastor), but stripped of warmth. His silence when Tomás asks "Is it the same prayer?" is more powerful than any response.
- Etxeberria speaks once: "With what?" — Blunt, soldier's frustration. Effective.
- Dialogue ratio is low but appropriate for a chapter about extremity. The silences do more work than the words.

**Verdict: PASS**

### POV

Martín's POV holds throughout. No omniscient intrusions. Padre Joaquín's interiority is rendered through Martín's inference ("I think he was afraid"). Tomás's interiority is rendered through behavior (digging, wrapping, pressing his face against Martín's neck). Vidal's interiority is absent — correct.

The frame narrative (present-tense opening and closing) is Martín-as-older-man looking back. This is within the POV contract — it is still Martín's consciousness, just at a different time.

**Verdict: PASS**

### Sentence Rhythm

The chapter uses short, declarative sentences as its dominant rhythm — appropriate for extremity. Examples:

- "He was right. Men were dying. From the cold." (¶17)
- "He did not sit." (¶51)
- "We held." (¶89)

Longer sentences appear in the sensory renderings (¶19, ¶31, ¶89) and the Tomás-preservation passage (¶113). The variation is deliberate. No five consecutive sentences of similar length.

**Verdict: PASS**

### Literary Cliché

None of the banned phrases appear. The prose is plain, physical, specific. No false elevation. The closest to decoration is "the body's last argument against the cold" (¶117), which is earned through the chapter's sustained metaphor of the body as a negotiating entity.

**Verdict: PASS**

## III. ADVISORY FINDINGS (Non-Blocking)

### A. "White Plumes" Repetition

The phrase "white plumes" or "breath rising in white plumes" appears 4 times (¶13, ¶43, ¶45, ¶47). While the repetition mirrors the rote of the cold and the rote of the blessings, it approaches the threshold of verbatim cross-passage repetition. Consider varying the rendering in at least one instance — e.g., "his breath visible, rising, gone" or "the white of his breath."

### B. Foreshadowing Explicitness

The frame narrative contains explicit foreshadowing of Tomás's death: "That in four months, on the banks of the Ebro, I would hold Tomás in my arms and his blood would be on my hands and his last word would be an unfinished sentence to a mother in Aoiz." (¶159). This is a structural choice — the older Martín knows the future. It is effective as a frame device, but it does remove some of the shock of Chapter 12. The reader will arrive at the Ebro knowing Tomás will die. This is not a flaw — it shifts the reader's experience from surprise to dread, which is consistent with the novel's theme of inevitability. But it should be a conscious choice, not an accident.

### C. The Frame Narrative's Temporal Markers

The frame opens with "I am writing this in the back room of the hardware store" and references "thirty years" of the debt following him. This places the frame at approximately 1968–1969. The hardware store is Don Eusebio's. This is the first time we see Martín in the post-war world — running his father's store, writing. This is significant for the novel's ending architecture. If the frame is intended to recur, it should be noted in the state files.

### D. The Boy Soldier

The Christmas Eve passage includes a Republican boy — "seventeen, maybe" — whose bolt is frozen, whose fingers will not close. Martín shoots him. The snow covers the blood. This is rendered with the same clinical distance as the rest of the combat. It is effective. But it is also the second time Martín has killed at close range (the first was Chapter 3). The accumulation of these moments should be tracked — they are building toward the Ebro, toward Tomás, toward the silence.

## IV. QUALITATIVE ASSESSMENT

This is the strongest chapter in the novel so far. The prose is disciplined, the scenes are rendered with specificity, the characters are in voice, and the emotional palette is earned through physical detail rather than named emotion. The cold is not described — it is experienced. The reader's body responds to the prose because the prose is anchored in the body.

The chapter's greatest strength is its restraint. The temptation in a chapter about extremity is to swell — to add adjectives, to name the horror, to reach for metaphor. This chapter does none of that. "He was right. Men were dying. From the cold." Three sentences. The third fragment does more work than a paragraph of description.

The Tomás-Martín relationship is rendered with a physical intimacy that is neither sentimental nor sexual — it is the intimacy of survival, of two bodies pressed together because moving apart means death. The passage where Tomás presses his face against Martín's neck (¶117) is the novel's most tender moment so far, and it earns that tenderness through two hundred words of physical extremity.

Padre Joaquín's arc in this chapter — from blessing Ibarra to moving body to body without sitting to saying Mass without a sermon in a frozen schoolroom — is the clearest rendering of the faith-erosion theme in the novel. The skipped sermon (¶139) is the most powerful moment: a priest who has run out of words, in a war that has consumed the meaning of the words he had.

The only risk is the explicit foreshadowing of Tomás's death in the frame narrative. This is a structural choice that shifts Chapter 12 from surprise to dread. It is consistent with the novel's theme of inevitability, but it should be intentional.
