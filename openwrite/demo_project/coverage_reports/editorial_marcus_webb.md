# Development Notes — Marcus Webb — The Red Beret Outline

**Reviewer:** Marcus Webb, Development Executive (12 years)
**Date:** June 6, 2026
**Material:** Bible (concept, mythology, 7 character profiles) + 15-chapter outline

---

## Market Assessment

This is a prestige literary novel with a clearly defined but narrow audience. The Spanish Civil War occupies a specific shelf in Anglophone literary fiction. The protagonist fights for a faction within the fascist coalition — a commercially challenging pitch.

Where this novel has genuine commercial potential is in the European and Spanish-language markets. The Carlist experience is undertold even in Spanish fiction. Translation rights into Spanish, Basque, and potentially Catalan could be significant.

## Verdict: CONSIDER — with targeted revision

The novel is structurally sound, thematically rich, and the character architecture is strong. But the commercial risks are real, and the current outline has specific marketability gaps that could be addressed without compromising literary ambition.

## Marketability Concerns

1. No female perspective or significant female presence.
2. Single POV, linear structure, no temporal variety.
3. The protagonist fights for the "wrong" side.
4. The ending is deliberately anti-cathartic.
5. The midpoint pivot (Chapter 5) may arrive too late.
6. Limited contemporary resonance hooks.
7. The title is opaque to non-specialist readers.
8. No romance subplot.

## Market Strengths

1. The moral architecture is exceptional — prize potential.
2. The callback system is a structural masterpiece.
3. Tomás is a breakout character.
4. The Spanish Civil War is undertold in English-language fiction.
5. The Basque Country setting is commercially promising.
6. The novel is structurally tight — 80,000–100,000 words.
7. The Carlist feast day device is a marketing asset.
8. Prize potential — Booker International, Dublin Literary Award.

## Positioning Recommendations

**Category:** Literary fiction / Historical fiction. Not military fiction.

**Comp titles:** *The Sympathizer* (Nguyen), *The Narrow Road to the Deep North* (Flanagan), *A Gentleman in Moscow* (Towles).

**Audience:** (1) Literary fiction readers who prize moral complexity. (2) European historical fiction readers. (3) Catholic/religious-readership markets. (4) Spanish and Basque diaspora communities. (5) Book club market (conditional).

**Specific recommendations:**
- Consider a structural on-ramp in Act I.
- Consider expanding Amaia's presence beyond two chapters.
- The title needs a conversation.
- The flap copy must lead with the betrayal, not the war.
- Target the Spanish-language market from the outset.
