## II. PROSE QUALITY

### Scene vs. Summary

The chapter is approximately 75% scene, 25% summary. The summary passages earn their place:

- The train journey (¶13–15): compresses the ride into sensory accumulation — brown plain, olive trees, foothills, green. Each landscape marker is a scene in miniature. The green entering "like water entering a dry channel" does the work of a paragraph of description.
- "Days passed" section (¶149–155): compresses the middle days into catalogue — walking streets, eating bread, drinking wine, sitting at the window. The compression is earned because the outline specifies these as background days, not dramatized scenes.
- The departure walk (¶189): compresses the return to war into stones under boots, bell still ringing.

No passage fails the deletion test. Every paragraph earns its place.

**Verdict: PASS**

### Prose Distance

The chapter modulates effectively:

- **Extreme close-up:** Don Eusebio's hand reaching for Martín's temple (¶47–51), the crescent burn on the palm, the fingers finding only hair. The church kneeler — "Knees on wood. Hands on the rail. Eyes on the altar." The crack in the ceiling followed like a map of rivers.

- **Compressed lyric:** "Pamplona was Pamplona." (¶19). "He had stayed away." (¶83). "I did not pray." (¶129). "Wool remembers." (¶207).

No five consecutive paragraphs at the same distance. The modulation is deliberate.

### Interiority

The chapter's interiority is strong throughout. Key passages:

- The green arriving (¶13): "Green entered my eyes like water entering a dry channel. I pressed my forehead to the window and watched it come closer, field after field, and my chest ached with something I had believed was gone." Physical, sensory, specific to a man who has been in brown mountains for twenty months. Level 5.
- The store smell (¶29): "A space behind my eyes that had been hollow for twenty months filled with recognition so sharp it bordered on pain." Interiority as physical sensation. Level 4.
- The bread and wine (¶89): "Taste of my childhood. I chewed and behind it was every meal I had eaten at this table and I was a boy and my father was young and the crucifix was new and the king was coming." Rendered consciousness — taste as temporal portal. Level 5.
- The prayer silence (¶135–137): "Place where prayer had been was empty... A great emptiness between us... Place where prayer had been frozen solid." The frozen-water metaphor is specific, physical, earned. Level 5.
- The window (¶177): "His fingers opening and closing at his side, as if he were holding something that was slipping away." Martín reading Don Eusebio's body. Not omniscient — behavioral inference. Level 4.

No named emotions. No generic interiority. All interiority is body-anchored or behavior-anchored or perception-anchored.

**Verdict: PASS**

### Dialogue

Dialogue is minimal — correct for a chapter about two men who lack a language for what they feel. Key lines:

- "Martin." / "Father." — The naming. Two words that carry the weight of twenty months.
- "Fueros are being discussed. In Burgos." / "Being discussed badly." — Don Eusebio's Register 1 (The Patriarch): declarative, factual, muted. The pause before "badly" is earned.
- "Tomas?" / "Alive." — The question Don Eusebio needed to ask. The answer that is complete and incomplete.
- "You will eat." — Command, not question. Don Eusebio's register unchanged.
- "Go back. Finish it." — The Patriarch understanding that the war is something to be ended, not won.

Every line is in character. No adverbial tags. No hedging. The silence does more work than the words.

**Verdict: PASS**

### POV

Martín's POV holds throughout. No omniscient intrusions. Don Eusebio's interiority is rendered through behavior (the hand reaching and dropping, the fingers opening and closing, the silhouette at the window). The crucifix's meaning is Martín's reading, not the narrator's.

The frame narrative (present-tense opening and closing) is Martín-as-older-man looking back. The flash-forward to 1958 ("My father died in 1958. In this house.") is within the POV contract — it is Martín's knowledge at the time of writing. See Advisory Finding A.

**Verdict: PASS**

## III. ADVISORY FINDINGS (Non-Blocking)

### A. The Flash-Forward to 1958

The frame narrative's closing contains a flash-forward: "My father died in 1958. In this house. Crucifix on the wall. Beret on the hook. Store on Calle Mayor. Fueros abolished. King stayed away." (¶203). This is the first time the novel reveals Don Eusebio's death. It is emotionally devastating — five declarative sentences that compress twenty years into a list of facts.

However, this is a significant narrative choice. The outline's Chapter 15 ending is built on silence — Don Eusebio takes the crucifix from the wall, holds it, hangs it back, says nothing. If the reader already knows Don Eusebio will die in 1958, the Chapter 15 silence is recontextualized — it becomes the silence of a man who will live twenty more years in the world that devoured his cause. This is not a flaw. It may be the chapter's strongest structural contribution. But it should be a conscious choice, not an accident. The 1958 flash-forward changes what Chapter 15 can do.

### B. The "Crown" Metaphor

"A father's hand placing a crown on a son's head before sending him into a country that would devour them both." (¶205). The word "crown" is the most elevated language in the chapter. It sits inside the frame narrative, where Martín-as-older-man is allowed a more reflective register. It works. But it approaches the threshold of false elevation that `07_format_rules.md` guards against. If the frame narrative recurs in later chapters, the "crown" should not reappear — it is a one-time deployment.

### C. The Oak Seed

The Plaza del Castillo oak (¶159–169) is a new callback seed not in the outline's seed list. It extends the Ch9 motif (Basque oak cut by Republicans) into the domestic space — tradition being dismantled at home. The detail is specific ("Two hundred years old. My father sat under it. His father sat under it. Before the first war.") and earns its place. It should be added to the callback ledger for tracking through Chapter 15.

### D. Ignacio's Closed Door

"Ignacio's was closed. It had been closed since 1932. I did not open it." (¶107). Three sentences. The brother's absence is rendered through a door. This is the novel's most compressed piece of backstory — the reader does not know who Ignacio is or what happened in 1932. The compression is effective: it signals absence without explaining it. If Ignacio's story is never opened (structurally), this is perfect. If it is opened later, the door serves as seed. Either way, the three sentences are among the chapter's best.

## IV. QUALITATIVE ASSESSMENT

This is a compression chapter — shorter than the surrounding Teruel and Ebro chapters, deliberately so. The outline specifies it may be shorter. The compression works: ten days of leave are rendered in 2,700 words because the leave itself is compressed. Martín and Don Eusebio do not have conversations. They sit. They eat. They look out windows. The war is a room in the house and the door is closed.

The chapter's greatest strength is the father-son relationship. Don Eusebio is rendered with the same precision as the Teruel cold — every physical detail (the crescent burn, the frayed collar, the veins on the backs of his hands, the stoop, the slower steps) accumulates into a portrait of a man being aged by what he is watching. His silence is not passive. It is "labor of a man watching a century of faith being dismantled in front of him, piece by piece, decree by decree."

The arrival scene — hand reaching, pausing, dropping — is the chapter's most powerful moment. The hand that adjusted the beret twenty months ago reaches for it again and finds nothing to adjust. The reflex of ducking that Martín suppresses. The two men standing in a space where a gesture used to be possible and is no longer. This does the work of a chapter in four paragraphs.

The prayer scene is the chapter's theological contribution. Martín kneels. Body in position. Words do not come. He does not deny God. He does not rage. There is "a great emptiness between us." The frozen-water metaphor — "still present, still water, but immovable" — is the most precise rendering of the faith-erosion theme in the novel. It is not crisis. It is not apostasy. It is the body willing and the words absent.

The frame narrative's flash-forward to 1958 is the chapter's riskiest choice. It works — five declarative sentences that compress twenty years — but it changes what Chapter 15 can do. This should be intentional.

The prose discipline is exemplary. No named emotions. No AI tics. No false elevation. The dialogue is minimal and every word is earned. The POV holds. The sentence rhythm varies. The prose distance modulates. The chapter obeys every rule in `07_format_rules.md` without appearing to try.

Chapter 11 meets all structural requirements, advances all character arcs, and maintains the prose standard established in Chapter 10. The advisory findings are non-blocking. The chapter is ready for the next stage of the pipeline.
