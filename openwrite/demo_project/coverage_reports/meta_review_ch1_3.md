## Recurring Issues

### 1. Retrospective Narrator Interpretive Commentary
**Chapters affected:** 1, 2, 3
**Severity trend:** Stable (all three chapters)
**Revision addressed it?** No — pattern persists across all three chapters

The single most persistent issue across the first act. The retrospective narrator (older Martín at the table) consistently steps in to explain what rendered scenes already convey:

- **Ch1:** The narrator explains Don Eusebio's interiority ("the hands needed to do it again"), interprets gestures as symbolic equivalents ("The hands on the beret were the blessing"), and defends his own belief ("The belief was not naive"). 4 critical violations in show-don't-tell.
- **Ch2:** The narrator names emotions ("vertigo," "assessment," "admired"), delivers political thesis ("a coalition that does not share a name does not share a purpose"), and closes with a prophecy about the war's arc. 1 high + 2 moderate-high findings.
- **Ch3:** The narrator editorializes about military social dynamics ("the officer does not shake"), explains the psychology of habituation ("the place where bodies file the things they will need later"), and closes with a 5-line "I did not know" cascade that compresses the entire war's arc. 6 blocking findings.

**Pattern:** The narrator's impulse to interpret intensifies at chapter endings. All three chapters have their worst violations in the final 20% of the text. The closing passages consistently shift from rendered scene to retrospective thesis.

**Suggested focus:** Chapter endings are the primary battleground. Critics for Ch4–6 should treat the final 15% of each chapter as a high-risk zone and flag any interpretive commentary that explains what the preceding scene already showed.

### 3. "I Did Not Know" / "He Does Not Know" Refrain
**Chapters affected:** 1, 2, 3
**Severity trend:** Worsening
**Revision addressed it?** No — the refrain is escalating

| Chapter | Count | Worst Concentration |
|---------|-------|---------------------|
| Ch1 | 3 instances | Spread across chapter |
| Ch2 | 4 instances | 3 in closing passage |
| Ch3 | 5 instances | 5 in single paragraph (line 175) |

This refrain is the retrospective narrator's signature device — the older Martín naming what the boy did not know. In Ch1 it works as an occasional frame-marker. By Ch3 it has become a cascading pattern loop that the format rules flag at 3+ instances.

**The problem is structural, not rhetorical.** Each chapter uses this device to close, creating a recurring thesis sentence at the end of every chapter. By Ch3, the reader has been told "the boy did not know" three times per chapter for three chapters. The device has exhausted its capacity to generate emotional weight.

**Suggested focus:** Show-don't-tell and naturalism critics for Ch4–6 should flag any "I did not know" / "He does not know" construction beyond a single instance per chapter. The device should be reserved for its strongest possible deployment, not used as a default closing mechanism.

### 4. Polysyndeton Chain Saturation
**Chapters affected:** 1, 2, 3
**Severity trend:** Stable-high
**Revision addressed it?** No

The "X and Y and Z and W" construction is the prose's default accumulation device. Naturalism flagged it in all three chapters:
- Ch1: 24 triplet patterns (critical)
- Ch2: 60+ triplet/list instances (critical)
- Ch3: 33 polysyndeton lines out of 87 prose lines (38%, critical)

The construction is effective in isolation — the final sentence of Ch3 ("I am writing this and the pen moves and the boy moves with it...") earns its length. But at 38% density, every accumulation sounds the same. The reader's ear stops differentiating between listing what's in a pocket and describing the moment of first kill.

**Suggested focus:** Naturalism critics for Ch4–6 should count polysyndeton chains per paragraph and flag any chapter where 25%+ of prose lines contain 3+ "and" constructions. Reserve the chain for moments where accumulation IS the content.

## Critic Blind Spots

### 1. No critic tracks chapter-ending quality
Every chapter's worst violations cluster in the final 15–20% of the text. No critic specifically evaluates chapter endings as a category. The retrospective narrator's interpretive impulse is strongest at closings — this pattern should be explicitly tracked.

### 2. No critic evaluates prose distance modulation
The format rules require prose distance modulation every five paragraphs. The palette critic in Ch3 noted "lines 129–135 maintain the same middle distance for seven consecutive sentences" but this was an observation, not a systematic check. No critic systematically audits prose distance variation.

### 3. The voice critic does not check format-rule banned constructions
As noted above, the voice critic passed Ch3 despite two Tier 1 banned constructions. The voice critic's mandate is register coherence, not format compliance — but the gap means banned constructions can slip through if they don't affect voice quality.

### 4. No critic evaluates the frame-narrator breaks as a pattern
The "I am at the table. The inkwell is full." frame break appears in all three chapters with near-identical structure. Naturalism flagged it in Ch3 (moderate), but no critic evaluates the frame breaks as a cross-chapter pattern. The repetition risks becoming a stamped thesis sentence.

### 5. The continuity critic does not evaluate prose quality
Continuity catches factual errors, POV violations, and callback gaps — but does not evaluate whether the prose renders or tells. This is by design, but it means the continuity critic can pass a chapter that the show-don't-tell critic blocks.

## Refinement Notes for Ch4–6 Critics

### For Show-Don't-Tell Critics

**R1. Treat chapter endings as a high-risk zone.** All three chapters have their worst telling-not-rendering violations in the final 15% of the text. The retrospective narrator's impulse to interpret, summarize, and prophesize intensifies at closings. Flag any passage in the final 200 words that names emotions, explains what scenes already showed, or delivers political thesis statements.

**R2. Flag "I did not know" beyond a single instance per chapter.** The refrain has escalated from 3 instances (Ch1) to 5 in a single paragraph (Ch3). A single deployment per chapter is the maximum before the device exhausts its emotional capacity. Any instance beyond one should be flagged as moderate-high.

**R3. Check for banned constructions even when the prose is strong.** Ch3's show-don't-tell critic correctly flagged 2 Tier 1 banned constructions that the voice critic missed. The show-don't-tell critic should include a format-rule compliance pass as standard procedure, not as an afterthought.

**R4. Watch for the narrator explaining the body-as-machine motif.** Ch3's "the place where bodies file the things they will need later, the place that is adaptation, the body's own language" is the narrator explaining a conceit that the prose has already rendered through physical detail. The body-as-machine motif works when it's shown (shaking hands, mechanical learning, the cigarette cough); it fails when it's explained. Flag any passage that names the motif rather than rendering it.

### For Voice Critics

**R5. Add a Tier 1 banned construction check.** The voice critic passed Ch3 with 8.0/10 and zero blocking findings, while the show-don't-tell critic found 2 Tier 1 violations in the same chapter. The voice critic should explicitly check for: "Not X but Y" constructions, meta-address, "The particular X of Y," and hedge words. A chapter with excellent voice can still contain banned constructions.

**R6. Track "the way" density as a voice concern.** The naturalism critic flagged "the way" at 5.8/1k in Ch3 (8.3× Ch1's rate). This is not just a naturalism issue — it's a voice monotony issue. The comparison device is the narrator's default, and at this density it creates a rhythmic fingerprint that undermines the voice's authenticity. Flag any chapter where "the way" exceeds 3 per 1k words.

**R7. Monitor the "I am at the table" frame breaks.** Three chapters have used near-identical structure for the frame-narrator returns: "I am at the table. The inkwell is full." The voice critic should evaluate whether each frame break enters through a new sensory detail or repeats the established inventory. Repetition of the same opening structure is a voice monotony risk.

### For Palette Critics

**R8. Evaluate whether the target palette is undermined by narrator interpretation.** Ch3's palette critic made the batch's strongest finding: the trigger-pull and killing are rendered at narrative distance, failing the [Terror] target. The narrator's intellectual framing ("the space between one thing and another") substitutes observation for felt experience. Palette critics should specifically check whether the narrator's commentary enhances or dilutes the target emotions.

**R9. Check for rhetoric-vs-reality gap rendering.** Ch3's outline requires "the gap between the crusade's rhetoric and the physical reality of a man falling." The palette critic correctly identified that this gap is stated (line 147: "the gap between the body and the mind") rather than felt. When the outline specifies a gap between two states, the palette critic should evaluate whether the gap is rendered through physical contradiction (the failed prayer) or named by the narrator (the thesis sentence).

### For Continuity Critics

**R10. Verify prop locations against prior chapters, not just state files.** Ch3's scapular inconsistency (pocket vs. neck) was caught by cross-referencing Ch1's text, not the state file. The state file said "on Tomás's neck" but the Ch3 text said "in his pocket." Continuity critics should verify prop locations against the actual chapter text, not just the state file's recorded location.

**R11. Check directional/geographic references against the established march route.** Ch3's "north to the Madrid front" was caught because the meseta is south of Pamplona. As the novel moves through specific campaigns, geographic references become higher-risk. Continuity critics should maintain a running directional context.

### For Naturalism Critics

**R12. Track "the way" as the primary regression metric.** The density went from 0.7/1k (Ch1) to 5.8/1k (Ch3). This is the clearest generation fingerprint in the current batch. Target: ≤2 per 1k words. Any instance where "the way" appears 3+ times in a single paragraph should be flagged as critical.

**R13. Evaluate polysyndeton effectiveness, not just density.** Ch3 has 33 polysyndeton lines (38% of prose), but the final sentence earns its length. Naturalism critics should distinguish between polysyndeton that IS the content (accumulation as meaning) and polysyndeton that is default rhythm (accumulation as habit). Flag density above 25%, but note which instances earn their place.

**R14. Track "the body" as agent.** Ch3 uses "the body" as sentence subject 19 times. This is the chapter's central conceit, but at this density it becomes a pattern loop. The format rules flag pattern loops at 3+ instances. Naturalism critics should track "the body" density and flag triple-constructions ("The body would X. The body would Y. The body would Z.").

**R15. Watch for substitution patterns.** The em-dash density dropped from 3.27/250w (Ch2) to 0.25/250w (Ch3), but "the way" simultaneously rose from 1.8/1k to 5.8/1k. This suggests the prose-writer replaced one comparison device with another rather than varying the syntax. Naturalism critics should check whether a reduction in one pattern correlates with an increase in another — this indicates substitution, not improvement.

**CC1. When voice and show-don't-tell disagree, the more restrictive verdict prevails.** Ch3: voice = PASS (8.0/10), show-don't-tell = ADVANCE-WITH-FINDINGS (6 blocking). The show-don't-tell verdict is more restrictive and should be the operative one.

**CC2. Naturalism findings should be weighted by prose effectiveness, not just pattern count.** The naturalism critic flagged Ch3's final sentence (10 "and" constructions) as critical, but the sentence earns its length through the sensory flood it renders. Pattern density alone is insufficient — the critic should evaluate whether the pattern serves the content or overrides it.

**CC3. Continuity errors should block before prose revision begins.** Ch2's Magdalene feast date error and Ch3's scapular inconsistency are factual errors that should be fixed before any prose revision pass. Continuity blockers take priority over prose quality findings.

## Summary

The first three chapters establish strong voice work, consistent character registers, and effective scene-level prose. The primary risks are: (1) the retrospective narrator's increasing impulse to interpret at chapter endings, (2) the "I did not know" refrain escalating beyond its useful life, (3) the "the way" comparison device replacing em-dashes as the dominant generation fingerprint, and (4) the polysyndeton chain functioning as default rhythm rather than deliberate accumulation. The em-dash resolution (Ch1–2 → Ch3) shows the prose-writer responds to naturalism feedback, but the "the way" regression suggests the response may involve substitution rather than genuine variation. Critics for Ch4–6 should track these patterns quantitatively and flag density regressions immediately.
