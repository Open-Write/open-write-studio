## III. STRUCTURAL ISSUES

### Issue 1: Pre-emption of Ch15 Callback (MODERATE)

The outline assigns the Aoiz visit and scapular return to Ch15:

> *"Martín visits Tomás's family in Aoiz. He gives Tomás's mother the scapular she sewed. He says: 'He was brave. He was my friend.'"* — Outline, Ch15

Ch12 delivers this scene in full (lines 183–197): the bus from Pamplona, the mother at the door, the scapular handed over, the wheat field in stubble. This collapses the "Tell my mother" callback arc from a Ch12→Ch15 seed-payoff into a single chapter. The outline's callback seed table specifies:

> *"Tell my mother" (Ch12 → Ch15): Tomás's unfinished sentence, completed by Martín's visit to Aoiz.*

**Resolution options:**
1. **Move the Aoiz visit to Ch15** (preferred). End Ch12 at the river crossing. The scapular remains in the pocket. Ch15 delivers the payoff.
2. **Revise Ch15's outline** to account for the visit already happening. Ch15 could reference the visit rather than render it, focusing on the aftermath or the letter from Amaia.

### Issue 2: Scapular Continuity (MINOR)

Lines 147–148: "I put it in my pocket. The pocket with the crucifix. Wood and wool."
Lines 187–189: He gives the scapular to Tomás's mother.
Line 199: "The scapular is still in my pocket. Next to the crucifix. Wood and wool. The two things I carry."

The scapular cannot be both given away and "still in my pocket." If the Aoiz visit is retained in Ch12, line 199 needs revision. If the visit is moved to Ch15, the line is correct (the scapular is still in the pocket at the time of writing the war section).

### B. Prose Highlights

**The death scene (lines 111–143).** Masterful restraint. The mortar arrives without foreshadowing — "The third round landed in our hole." Three words. The physical rendering is precise: the shattered rifle stock, the dark blood pooling in the dust, the hands around nothing. The emotional weight arrives through the body, not through commentary: "My hands were red. His blood was warm. It was the warmest thing on the ridge." This is level 5 interiority — rendered consciousness, specific to this man in this moment.

**The months montage (lines 35–74).** Effective compression. The mortar walk routine ("One. Two. Three. Move.") establishes the daily machinery. The October rain and the drainage channel render Tomás through action. The blankets, the shoulder against shoulder, the half-smile — these details accumulate into a portrait of a man the reader has known for eleven chapters and will lose in this one.

**The Aoiz visit (lines 183–197).** Devastating. The wheat field in stubble. The mother's hand on the doorframe. Her eyes dropping to the shape of the scapular through the cloth. "Thank you." The door closing. The bus stop. The field that would grow again in spring.

**"Sixty-three walked out." (line 207).** The chapter's final line. Two words after the full stop. The count that has been building since the first mortar round. Perfect placement.

### C. Minor Prose Notes

**"His hands were on the rifle, or where the rifle had been"** — appears twice (lines 117 and 123). The repetition is close enough to read as accidental rather than deliberate. Consider varying the second instance.

**"I am looking. I am calling it."** — appears at lines 73 and 201. Within the memoir frame, this is the narrator-character speaking about his own act of writing, not novel-level meta-address. The repetition is structurally intentional — it brackets the death scene. Acceptable, though the second instance could be cut for tighter prose (the chapter's ending is stronger without it — "Sixty-three walked out." is the true ending).

**"The body knows"** — appears at lines 7 and 205. The first instance ("The body knows what the hand resists. The body knows what comes next.") is strong. The second ("The body does not forget. The body dumps its memories on the floor and says: sort it out.") is weaker — "dumps its memories on the floor" is more colloquial than the chapter's register. Consider revision.
