## CRITICAL FINDINGS (2) — Must fix before advancing

### C1. Omniscience Leak — Future Historical Knowledge (Lines 161)

> "The boy did not know, then, what his father knew. The Decree of Unification was a permanent measure — the boy thought it was temporary. The fueros would be abolished, formally, legally, by the regime the boy was building with his body and his bullets and his dead. The schools would be closed. The language would be banned. The autonomy would be erased. The king would not come. The red beret would hang on a hook in a hallway in Pamplona and gather dust and mean nothing."

**Type:** Omniscience leak / POV violation
**Location:** Lines 161 (within final 15% of chapter — most critical real estate)
**Problem:** The narrator shifts from retrospective first-person into omniscient historical register. The passage presents the Decree of Unification's consequences (fueros abolished, schools closed, language banned, autonomy erased) as authorial certainties rather than the older Martín's hindsight reflection. The phrase "the boy did not know, then, what his father knew" adopts an external narrator's vantage — seeing both the boy's ignorance and the father's knowledge simultaneously — which violates the POV contract (Section IX). Five consecutive declarative sentences about future political events read as historical exposition, not as a character's rendered consciousness.
**Fix:** Remove or drastically compress. If the older narrator must gesture at hindsight, render it through a single physical image — the beret on the hook, the empty desk — rather than a list of political outcomes. The letter already carries the father's warning; the reader does not need the narrator to confirm the father was right.

### C2. POV Shift — First Person to Third Person "The Boy" (Lines 161–163)

> "The boy did not know, then, what his father knew."
> "The boy did not know. The boy held the letter and the crucifix and he did not write back because he did not have the words."

**Type:** POV violation / voice break
**Location:** Lines 161, 163 (within final 15%)
**Problem:** The chapter's voice contract is first-person retrospective ("I was twenty-three," "I can hear it now"). The shift to "the boy" in third person breaks this contract. It introduces a narrator who stands outside Martín entirely, observing him from an external vantage. This is not free indirect — free indirect stays inside the character's head. This is an omniscient narrator who has stepped between the reader and the character.
**Fix:** Either stay in first person ("I did not know, then, what my father knew") or, if the retrospective frame demands distance, use a single transitional gesture and return to first person immediately. The sustained third-person passage (lines 161–163) must not stand.

### A7. Meta-Commentary on Language (Line 139)

> "He said it the way a man says a word that is not enough. A tree. As if naming it could hold what it was."

**Type:** Meta-literary commentary / authorial intrusion
**Location:** Lines 139
**Problem:** The narrator steps back to comment on the inadequacy of Tomás's word. This is meta-address — the narrator explaining the novel's own thematic concern with language and loss. Tomás saying "A tree" is powerful on its own. The narrator's gloss ("the way a man says a word that is not enough") tells the reader how to interpret the moment rather than letting the moment land.
**Fix:** Cut lines 139's first two sentences. Let Tomás's "A tree" stand without the narrator's editorial. The stump, the roots, the sap, the rings — the physical description that follows — already renders what the word cannot hold.

### A8. Interpretive Symbolism — "The Shape of the Thing the Fueros Were" (Line 139)

**Type:** Telling-not-rendering / interpretive symbolism
**Location:** Line 139
**Problem:** The narrator explicitly connects the oak tree to the fueros and explains the metaphor (deep-rooted = fueros, visible part = public rights, hidden part = cultural foundation). This is the narrator doing the reader's interpretive work. The physical description of the roots — "as deep as the tree was tall, the mirror image, the part that had been hidden now exposed" — already renders the duality without naming the fueros.
**Fix:** Cut the explicit fueros connection. The physical image of the root system — the hidden half now exposed — does the symbolic work without the narrator's gloss.
