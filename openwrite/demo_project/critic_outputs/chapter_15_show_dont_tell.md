# Show-Don't-Tell Critic — Chapter 15: The Altar

**Chapter:** 015 — The Altar
**Critic:** Show-Don't-Tell
**Date:** 2026-06-08
**Verdict:** CONDITIONAL PASS (7 findings — all concentrated in final 15%)
**Total findings:** 7
**Chapter word count:** ~4,800

---

## Summary

The first 85% of this chapter is among the strongest prose in the novel. The parade, the father on the sidewalk, the Aoiz kitchen, the scapular, the dining room — all rendered through physical detail, gesture, silence, and concrete imagery. The narrator stays inside Martín's perception. The crucifix scene with the father is flawless: hands, weight, hanging, silence. No emotion named. No interpretation offered.

The final 15% (lines 139–161) is where the chapter weakens. The narrator steps back, labels the symbolism, and explains what the reader has already understood. The restraint that makes the first 85% devastating collapses into a catalogue of thematic declarations. Every finding below falls in this zone.

---

## Findings

### Finding 1 — Authorial Intrusion (Symbolism Label)

**Location:** Line 133
**Type:** Authorial intrusion / meta-address

> Three objects. The sacred, the testimony, the tradition. In the same house. In the same silence.

**Problem:** The narrator steps outside Martín's consciousness to name the symbolic register of three objects. Martín would not think "the sacred, the testimony, the tradition." He would see the crucifix, the letter, the beret. The reader has already assembled the symbolism from 130 lines of rendered detail. Naming it deflates it.

**Fix:** Delete lines 133–134. The preceding paragraph (lines 131–132) already places the three objects. The following paragraph (line 135) already holds them in silence. The label adds nothing.

---

### Finding 2 — Telling-Not-Rendering (Peace Cost)

**Location:** Line 129
**Type:** Telling-not-rendering / authorial commentary

> The ordinary sounds of a city that has been at peace for a month and has not yet learned what the peace will cost.

**Problem:** The narrator interprets the city's condition rather than rendering it. "Has not yet learned what the peace will cost" is the narrator's judgment, not Martín's perception. Martín is sitting on a bed reading a letter. He hears a cart, a dog. He does not audit the city's relationship to its own peace.

**Fix:** Cut after "A dog barking." The ordinary sounds are enough. The reader understands the cost from the letter just read.

---

### Finding 3 — Authorial Intrusion (Naming the Silence)

**Location:** Line 151
**Type:** Authorial intrusion / telling-not-rendering

> The silence of a man who fought for three years and came home and stood in the dining room with his father and watched his father hold the crucifix and hang it back and say nothing.

**Problem:** The narrator summarizes and interprets the father's silence. The silence has already been rendered — in the dining room scene (lines 67–75), in the father's hands on the crucifix, in the footsteps on the stairs, in the closing door. Naming it "the silence of a man who..." is explanation, not art.

**Fix:** Delete the sentence. Line 150's catalogue of absences ("The king did not come. The fueros are gone...") already carries the weight. Line 152 ("My father's silence") can stand as a fragment anchor if needed, but the long interpretive clause should go.

---

### Finding 4 — Telling-Not-Rendering (The Reckoning)

**Location:** Line 155
**Type:** Telling-not-rendering

> He held the crucifix once, in the dining room, the night I came home. He took it from the wall and held it and hung it back. That was all. That was his reckoning. The hands. The weight. The hanging. The silence.

**Problem:** "That was his reckoning" is the narrator's word, not Martín's. It interprets the father's action as a deliberate moral accounting. Martín would see the hands, the weight, the hanging — and he has already rendered these. The word "reckoning" imposes meaning the scene earned without it.

**Fix:** Delete "That was his reckoning." Keep the fragments: "The hands. The weight. The hanging. The silence." — these repeat the physical detail of the dining room scene and work as litany without the interpretive label.

---

### Finding 5 — Authorial Intrusion (Self-Referential Meta-Address)

**Location:** Lines 157–159
**Type:** Meta-address (Tier 1 violation)

> I hold the pen. The pen holds the ink. The ink holds the words. The words hold what happened. What happened is this: the crucifix is on the wall. The beret is on the hook. The letter is on the table. The silence is in the house.
>
> I am looking. I am calling it.

**Problem:** "The words hold what happened" is the narrator commenting on the act of narration. "I am looking. I am calling it." is the narrator stepping outside the story to announce that he is writing the story. This violates the POV contract (Section IX) and the meta-address ban (Section IV, Tier 1). The pen-ink-words chain also reads as a literary device Martín would not construct — he is a hardware store owner, not a stylist.

**Fix:** Delete lines 157–159. Go from line 156 ("He held the crucifix once...") directly to line 161 ("The king did not come."). The catalogue on line 150 already lists the absences. The king line lands harder without the narrator announcing it is about to land.

---

### Finding 6 — Telling-Not-Rendering (Interiority Summary)

**Location:** Lines 45–46
**Type:** Telling-not-rendering (named knowledge)

> I knew that if I stayed in this kitchen for a hundred years I would not find the words that belonged in the space between us.

**Problem:** This is Martín naming his own inability, which is telling. The scene has already rendered the inability through physical detail — the mother not touching the scapular, the dog under the table, the stove ticking. The reader feels the gap. The sentence explains it.

**Fix:** Delete the sentence. The preceding detail ("She did not touch it. Her hands were in her lap.") and the following detail ("He was brave," I said again. Because I did not have anything else.") already render the failure of language without naming it.

---

### Finding 7 — Telling-Not-Rendering (Authorial Commentary)

**Location:** Line 129 (beginning)
**Type:** Authorial intrusion

> I looked at the room. The bed. The window. The alley outside. The sound of a cart on the street. A dog barking.

**Analysis:** This part is fine — rendered perception. The problem is what follows (covered in Finding 2). Including here for completeness of the line.

**No fix needed** for this portion. The fix is in Finding 2.

## Positive Notes (No Violations Found)

The following passages are exemplary and earn their place:

- **Lines 5–26:** The parade sequence. Dust, berets, drums, father on sidewalk. Zero named emotions. Physical detail only. The father's hands at his sides, his mouth not opening — devastating through restraint.
- **Lines 29–57:** Aoiz. The kitchen. The scapular. The stove ticking. The dog under the table. The mother not touching the scapular. All rendered through gesture and object. The best scene in the novel.
- **Lines 61–76:** The dining room. The father holds the crucifix, hangs it back, walks out. No words. No interpretation. The thumb moving across the wood — detail only this character would notice.
- **Lines 103–121:** Amaia's letter. Voice differentiation is precise. The teacher's clarity, the Basque woman's directness. "I hope the king you fought for was worth it." Subtext as blade.
- **Lines 147–150:** The catalogue of what remains. "The school is closed. The language is banned. The king did not come." Earned repetition. The refrain lands because the novel has built toward it for 15 chapters.

## Verdict

**CONDITIONAL PASS.** The chapter's first 85% is clean, rendered, and earned. The final 15% breaks the discipline the rest of the chapter follows. The narrator steps outside Martín's consciousness to label symbolism, interpret silence, and announce the act of writing. Every finding is in this zone.

**Action required:** Cut or rewrite lines 133–134, 151 (interpretive clause), 155 ("That was his reckoning"), and 157–159. Keep the physical catalogues. Keep the king line. Trust the reader.
