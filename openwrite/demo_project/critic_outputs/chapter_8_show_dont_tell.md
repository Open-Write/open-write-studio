## Verdict: ADVANCE WITH NOTES

**Findings: 14** (3 blocking, 5 advisory, 6 borderline)

The chapter is strong. The Amaia confrontation is rendered through precise physical detail — the broom marks, the overturned desks, the chewed pencil, the child's arithmetic. The Euskara sequence (lines 83–89) is the chapter's best writing: the language rendered through sound, texture, and the body's failure to respond. The soldier's death is clean, specific, unsentimental.

The primary weakness is **thematic meta-address** — the narrator stepping back twice to explain what the scene means (lines 103, 125–127). These passages tell the reader what to think about material that has already been rendered. The omniscience leak at line 145 violates the POV contract. Several passages label or interpret what the prose has already shown.

The chapter ending (final 15%, lines 168–197) is mostly clean. Tomás's dialogue about his mother is rendered through specific detail. The present-tense writing frame (lines 191–197) is borderline meta-narrative but earns its place as the chapter's signature closure.

## Blocking Findings (3)

### Finding 1 — Line 103: Thematic Meta-Address

> "She was teaching me the lesson I had come south to learn and had not known I needed: that the crusade had a hole in it, and the hole was shaped like a woman standing in a room where children used to learn to read, and the room was empty, and the desks were overturned, and the chart was on the floor, and the crucifix remained."

**Type:** Authorial intrusion / Meta-address
**Location:** Line 103
**Severity:** Blocking

The narrator steps back to explain the novel's thematic argument. This is the most significant violation in the chapter. "The lesson I had come south to learn" is meta-narrative — the narrator interpreting the scene's meaning for the reader. "The hole was shaped like a woman" is the narrator's symbolic gloss on material that has already been rendered through Amaia's physical presence, the overturned desks, and the chart in Martín's jacket.

**Fix:** Cut the entire passage. The scene's meaning is carried by the confrontation itself — Amaia's voice, the chart, the crucifix that stays while the alphabet is removed. The reader does not need the narrator to explain what the room means. End on "I had no answer for her. I said nothing." (line 105).

---

### Finding 2 — Lines 125–127: Thematic Interpretation of the Map

> "On the map, it was thin. On the ground, it was the difference between a school that was open and a school that was closed, between a language that was taught and a language that was banned, between a woman who stood in a doorway and a man who could not answer her."

**Type:** Authorial intrusion / Meta-address
**Location:** Lines 125–127
**Severity:** Blocking

The narrator explains the symbolic meaning of the map border. The physical details are already rendered — the ink line, the provinces, Pamplona vs. Tolosa. The triadic parallel ("between a school… between a language… between a woman…") is the narrator interpreting for the reader. This is the same violation as Finding 1: the scene's meaning is already carried by the map, the chart, and the woman who walked out.

**Fix:** Cut from "On the ground, it was the difference…" through the end of the paragraph. Keep the physical details: "My finger found Pamplona. I traced the road south…" The reader understands the distance without the narrator's gloss.

---

### Finding 3 — Line 145: Omniscience Leak

> "It moved on the soldier's forehead with the precision of a man who has made that sign ten thousand times and has never yet found it adequate."

**Type:** Omniscience leak / POV violation
**Location:** Line 145
**Severity:** Blocking

"Has never yet found it adequate" accesses Padre Joaquín's interiority — his private assessment of his own performance. Martín cannot know this. The physical precision is rendered ("the thumb was small, the nail clean, the skin darkened by sun"); the "never found it adequate" is the narrator's omniscient judgment.

**Fix:** Cut "and has never yet found it adequate." The physical precision already carries the weight. "It moved on the soldier's forehead with the precision of a man who has made that sign ten thousand times" is sufficient.

## Advisory Findings (5)

### Finding 4 — Line 65: Authorial Interpretation of Voice

> "She was stating. The way a teacher states a fact to a student who has not yet understood it."

**Type:** Authorial intrusion
**Location:** Line 65
**Severity:** Advisory

The narrator explains the function of Amaia's voice. The preceding dialogue and the voice description ("clear, measured") already render this. "The way a teacher states a fact to a student who has not yet understood it" is the narrator interpreting for the reader.

**Fix:** Cut "She was stating. The way a teacher states a fact to a student who has not yet understood it." The voice is already rendered.

### Finding 5 — Line 77: Telling What the Scene Shows

> "She was teaching me. She was telling me something I had not heard, or something I knew and had not yet been forced to hear from a mouth that was not my father's, in a room where twenty-three children had learned to read in a language that was now forbidden."

**Type:** Telling-not-rendering
**Location:** Line 77
**Severity:** Advisory

The confrontation is already rendered through the dialogue, the chart, the overturned desks. "She was teaching me" labels what the reader has already witnessed. The extended clause ("something I knew and had not yet been forced to hear…") is the narrator explaining the scene's impact rather than letting the impact land.

**Fix:** Cut the entire passage. The preceding dialogue ("We are Catholic. We are Basque. We had the fueros. You took them.") is sufficient. The silence that follows (line 78) does the work.

---

### Finding 6 — Line 91: Naming Perception

> "In them I saw recognition."

**Type:** Telling-not-rendering / Named emotion
**Location:** Line 91
**Severity:** Advisory

This names the emotion Martín perceives rather than rendering it through physical detail. What does recognition look like in her eyes? The format rules (Section V) place "named emotion" at Level 1 on the rendering spectrum — the worst quality.

**Fix:** Render the recognition through physical detail. Example: "Her eyes held mine. Something in them shifted — a softening, or a narrowing, or the particular stillness of a person who has just placed a face in a context they did not expect." Or simply: "Her eyes held mine." Let the reader infer.

### Finding 7 — Lines 87: Labeling What's Rendered

> "In Castilian she was a teacher. In Euskara she was something else. A woman speaking to a man who shared her language, or had shared it, or whose bones remembered it even if his mouth had forgotten."

**Type:** Telling-not-rendering
**Location:** Line 87
**Severity:** Advisory

The voice change has already been rendered through the physical description of the sounds — "warmer, closer, the consonants rounder, the vowels longer." "In Castilian she was a teacher. In Euskara she was something else" labels what the prose has already shown. The extended clause ("a woman speaking to a man who shared her language…") is the narrator interpreting.

**Fix:** Cut "In Castilian she was a teacher. In Euskara she was something else. A woman speaking to a man who shared her language, or had shared it, or whose bones remembered it even if his mouth had forgotten." The preceding physical description ("warmer, closer, the consonants rounder, the vowels longer") is sufficient. The next line — "I caught words" — moves the scene forward.

### Finding 8 — Line 149: Theological Interpretation

> "the same God who absolved the man who took the town and the man who lost it"

**Type:** Authorial intrusion
**Location:** Line 149
**Severity:** Advisory

The narrator makes a theological argument about the nature of absolution. This is the narrator's interpretation, not Martín's rendered perception. The physical detail — the thumb, the sign of the cross, the same words — already carries the weight.

**Fix:** Cut the clause. The preceding sentence ("The same sign he had made over the requeté who died at the bridge. The same words. The same absolution.") is sufficient. The repetition of "the same" does the work without the narrator's gloss.

## Borderline Findings (6)

### Finding 9 — Line 45: Interiority Attribution

> "She paused at the threshold, her eyes moving over the room like someone entering a house they once lived in — knowing where everything was, knowing everything had changed."

**Type:** Telling-not-rendering (borderline)
**Location:** Line 45
**Severity:** Borderline

The simile ("like someone entering a house they once lived in") works. The clause after the em-dash ("knowing where everything was, knowing everything had changed") attributes interior knowledge to Amaia that Martín cannot verify. However, the simile earns the inference — a person entering a house they once lived in *does* know where everything was. The em-dash clause is the narrator completing the simile's logic.

**Suggestion:** Cut "knowing where everything was, knowing everything had changed." The simile stands alone.

---

### Finding 10 — Line 81: Physical Tell Interpretation

> "His jaw worked slightly, the muscles moving under the skin, as it did before a man said something he had been holding back. He did not say it."

**Type:** Telling-not-rendering (borderline)
**Location:** Line 81
**Severity:** Borderline

The physical detail (jaw working, muscles moving) is good. "As it did before a man said something he had been holding back" explains what the physical tell means. However, this is Martín's inference from observed pattern — he has seen Tomás's jaw work before. The "He did not say it" that follows is rendering.

**Suggestion:** Cut "as it did before a man said something he had been holding back." Keep "His jaw worked slightly, the muscles moving under the skin. He did not say it."

### Finding 11 — Line 107: Interpretation of Silence

> "She was waiting for an answer I could not give."

**Type:** Telling-not-rendering (borderline)
**Location:** Line 107
**Severity:** Borderline

The preceding physical details (eyes held, hands at sides, breathing steady) render the moment. "She was waiting for an answer I could not give" interprets the silence. However, this is Martín's consciousness — he knows she is waiting, and he knows he cannot answer. It's close to rendered consciousness.

**Suggestion:** Keep. This is Martín's interiority, not the narrator's interpretation. The "I could not give" is his own assessment of his failure.

### Finding 12 — Line 127: Interpretation of Amaia's Departure

> "the woman who made it was walking through the streets of a town that was no longer hers"

**Type:** Authorial interpretation (borderline)
**Location:** Line 127
**Severity:** Borderline

"A town that was no longer hers" is the narrator's judgment. Martín doesn't know where Amaia is or what she's doing. However, the inference is earned — she has just walked out of her own school, which is locked, in a town under military occupation.

**Suggestion:** Keep. The inference is earned by the scene.

### Finding 13 — Lines 195–196: Meta-Narrative in Writing Frame

> "The pen moves. The boy moves with it."

**Type:** Meta-narrative (borderline)
**Location:** Lines 195–196
**Severity:** Borderline

This is the present-tense writing frame — Martín at the table, writing. "The pen moves" is rendered action in the writing frame. "The boy moves with it" is ambiguous — is it the boy character in the story, or young Martín? The shift to present tense is the chapter's signature closure, and the images that follow (the woman in the doorway, the overturned desks, the crucifix) are rendered.

**Suggestion:** Keep. The writing frame earns its place as the chapter's structural signature.

### Finding 14 — Line 197: Interpretation of Stance

> "She is waiting for the room to settle."

**Type:** Telling-not-rendering (borderline)
**Location:** Line 197
**Severity:** Borderline

This is the same image from line 31, now recontextualized. "Waiting for the room to settle" interprets her stance. The physical detail (hands at sides, fingers slightly spread) is rendered. The "waiting for the room to settle" is the narrator's interpretation of what a teacher's stance means.

**Suggestion:** Keep. The echo of line 31 is structural — the chapter opens and closes on the same image. The interpretation is earned by the repetition.

## Recommendation

The chapter advances. The three blocking findings are concentrated in two passages of thematic meta-address (lines 103 and 125–127) and one omniscience leak (line 145). All three are cuttable without affecting the scene's structure or emotional impact. The advisory findings are passages where the narrator labels or interprets what the prose has already shown — the pattern of a strong chapter that over-explains itself.

**Priority fixes:**
1. Cut line 103 (thematic meta-address)
2. Cut lines 125–127 from "On the ground, it was the difference…" (thematic interpretation)
3. Cut "and has never yet found it adequate" from line 145 (omniscience leak)
