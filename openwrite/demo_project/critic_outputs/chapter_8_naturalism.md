## Critical Finding: "the same" — 22 instances (limit: 12)

The phrase "the same" is the dominant structural crutch in this chapter. It appears in dense clusters that create a hammering, anaphoric rhythm that reads as AI-generated pattern lock.

### Cluster 1 — Line 21 (3 instances in one sentence)
> "The letters were **the same** letters my grandmother had on her wall... **The same** paper. **The same** black ink."

### Cluster 2 — Line 79 (6 instances in one paragraph)
> "**The same** crucifix. **The same** expression. **The same** God who watched over the classroom in Pamplona... **The same** wood. **The same** varnish. **The same** half-open eyes."

### Cluster 3 — Line 145 (4 instances in one sentence)
> "**The same** sign he had made over the requeté who died at the bridge. **The same** words. **The same** absolution. **The same** God who absolved the man who took the town and the man who lost it."

### Cluster 4 — Line 155 (3 instances in one sentence)
> "carrying **the same** words to **the same** kind of men in **the same** kind of ditch."

### Remaining instances
- Line 65: "It was the same clear, measured voice"
- Line 129: "with the same expression it had watched the full one"
- Line 161: "the same sound as the river in Pamplona, the same water moving south"
- Line 181: "She made the scapular the same year"
- Line 193: "shaping the same sounds, patient, repeating them"

**Diagnosis:** The anaphoric "the same" is doing thematic work — connecting the schoolroom to Pamplona, the crucifix to memory, the padre's absolution to futility. But at 22 instances it becomes a metronome. Lines 79 and 145 alone account for 10 instances. The prose is telling the reader "these things are the same" instead of letting the reader feel the sameness through the physical details, which are already strong enough to carry the meaning without the verbal underlining.

**Recommendation:** Cut to ≤12. Preserve the strongest instances (line 79 cluster can keep 2-3, line 145 can keep 2, line 155 can keep 2). Cut the standalone instances where context already conveys the connection (lines 65, 129, 193).

This phrase functions as a comparison crutch throughout the chapter, introducing similes and analogies at a rate that becomes a recognizable pattern.

| Line | Instance |

| 65 | "The way a teacher states a fact to a student" |
| 85 | "the way it moved forward and then paused and then moved forward again" |
| 179 | "the way a man moves his fingers on a rosary, bead by bead" |

**Diagnosis:** Four of eight instances use "the way a [role] [verbs]" — a teacher stands, holds chalk, states a fact; a man clears a field. This is a single syntactic template repeated. The comparisons themselves are good — specific, physical, character-grounded — but the framing device is identical each time.

**Recommendation:** Vary the comparison syntax. Convert 3-4 instances to direct metaphor or remove the framing entirely. "Her fingers found chalk between thumb and forefinger, the other fingers curled" loses nothing by cutting "the way a teacher holds chalk."
