## 4. Subtext

### The Shaking Sequence (lines 85–103)

> "The shaking started in the fingers. Both hands. The fingers curled and uncurled and curled again and I could not stop them." (line 85)

**Assessment:** The body's response to killing is rendered entirely through physical detail. No named emotion. No "Martín felt horror." The shaking is the subtext — the body processing what the mind cannot. The format rule's "rendered consciousness" level is achieved.

> "He sat. He took his canteen off his belt and uncapped it and held it out." (line 89)

**Assessment:** Tomás does not ask if Martín is all right. He does not say "it's okay." He sits and offers water. The subtext is complete: this is how a shepherd's son comforts a man — with presence, not words. The Companion register in action.

> "I took it. The paper was warm. The smoke was hot in my throat. I coughed. The coughing shook my whole body and the shaking in my hands was the same as the coughing and for a moment the whole body was doing the same thing, shaking, trembling, the machine coming apart at the joints." (line 93)

**Assessment:** "The machine coming apart at the joints" — the body-as-machine metaphor, consistent with the voice spec's body-language framework. The coughing and the shaking merge into one physical event. The subtext: the body is processing the kill. The cigarette is not comfort; it is a mechanism for the body to recalibrate.

### The Blue-Shirt Man (lines 67–73)

> "A man came out of a house. He was running. He ran from the door to the wall of the next house, crouched, his arms pumping. He wore a shirt. Blue, or gray." (line 67)

**Assessment:** "Blue, or gray." The narrator does not know the color with certainty. This is the voice spec's honesty: rendering what was seen, not what is convenient. The uncertainty is the subtext — the man was a target, not a person. The color does not matter because the bullet does not check the color.

> "The man's body changed direction. It was a small thing." (line 71)

**Assessment:** "It was a small thing." The subtext is devastating. A man dying is rendered as a physics event — trajectory, direction, ground. The Soldier register has taken over. The Catechism is not present here. The body is doing the work. The narrator does not tell us this is terrible. He shows us the mechanics and trusts the reader to feel it.

### The Prayer That Would Not Come (lines 145–151)

> "The words were not there. I reached for the words and the words were not there." (line 147)

**Assessment:** The Catechism's prayers — Our Father, Hail Mary, Glory Be — are absent. The body prays but the mind does not form the words. The subtext: the first crack in the framework. The crucifix is in the hand, the body is in the posture of prayer, but the words — the catechism's words — are gone. This is not yet the Fracture register (that comes in later acts), but it is the Catechism beginning to hollow.

> "I prayed without words. I prayed with the hands and the breath and the dark." (line 151)

**Assessment:** The prayer has become physical — hands, breath, dark. The Catechism's language (words, doctrine, declaration) has been replaced by the Soldier's language (body, sensation, presence). The subtext: faith is moving from the head to the hands. The direction of travel is toward the numbness.

**Assessment:** The numbness insight is delivered directly, in the narrator's retrospective voice.
