# Show-Don't-Tell Critic — Chapter 12: The River

**Critic mode:** Show-don't-tell enforcement
**Source:** `manuscript/chapters/012_the_river.md` (209 lines, ~4,500 words)
**Discipline reference:** `bible/07_format_rules.md`
**Focus:** Emotional state names, telling-not-rendering, authorial intrusion, omniscience leaks. Special attention to chapter ending (final 15%).

---

## Verdict: CONDITIONAL PASS — 18 violations found

The chapter is strong in its rendered consciousness — the mortar walk, the water organizing, the physical act of holding Tomás's body are all rendered at Level 4–5. But a persistent pattern of omniscience leaks (7) undermines the POV contract. Martín repeatedly attributes internal states, decisions, and philosophies to other characters (Vidal, Tomás, Tomás's mother) rather than rendering what he can observe. Four meta-address moments break the fictional frame. Three Tier 1 banned constructions ("Not X but Y" / "Not X. Not Y. Z.") survive in the text. The chapter ending (final 15%) is cleaner than the middle — two violations only.

## Violation Register

### 1 — Omniscience Leak (Vidal)
**Line 21:**
> "a quick count, an assessment of condition, a calculation of how many would be here when the position was finally taken or lost"

**Type:** Omniscience. Martín attributes an internal calculation to Vidal — "a calculation of how many would be here" is not observable.

**Fix:** Render only what Martín sees. "He looked at us — a quick count, eyes moving from face to face — and turned back to his map."

---

### 2 — Meta-Address
**Line 29:**
> "I am going to describe this because it must be described."

**Type:** Meta-address. The narrator justifies a structural choice — why he is including a description. Violates Tier 1: "The narrator must never step back to explain the novel's own structural choices."

**Fix:** Cut the justification. Begin directly: "The Ebro valley in the summer of 1938 was a landscape stripped of everything that makes a landscape livable."

---

### 3 — Telling (Attributed Understanding)
**Line 39:**
> "Tomás understood this better than I did."

**Type:** Telling. Martín declares Tomás's understanding rather than rendering it through observable behavior. The format rules require: "Don Eusebio's silence is rendered through what Martín sees: the face, the hands, the window."

**Fix:** Show through behavior. "Tomás did not pray. He touched the scapular and said nothing." The behavioral contrast already conveys the difference in understanding without stating it.

---

### 4 — Omniscience Leak (Tomás)
**Line 39:**
> "The body remembered what the mind had stopped believing."

**Type:** Omniscience. Martín attributes a mental state — "the mind had stopped believing" — to Tomás. Martín cannot access Tomás's interiority.

**Fix:** Render from Martín's own POV. "The body remembered what words could not hold." Keeps the rendered consciousness within Martín's perception.

### 5 — Omniscience Leak (Vidal)

### 6 — Omniscience Leak (Tomás)

### 7 — Meta-Address

**Type:** Meta-address. "In this paragraph" explicitly references the text as text — the narrator acknowledging he is writing a paragraph of prose. Violates Tier 1.

### 9 — Meta-Address

**Fix:** Cut entirely. End the paragraph at "I will not describe it further." The restraint is already shown by not describing; explaining why is the narrator showing off about not showing off.

**Ending verdict:** Clean. The two violations (17, 18) are in the middle of the ending sequence, not in the final beats. The closing lines are well-rendered.

## Pattern Analysis

**Dominant failure mode: Omniscience leaks (7 of 18).** Martín consistently attributes internal states to other characters — Vidal's calculations, Tomás's beliefs, Tomás's mother's feelings. The frame narrative (Martín writing from memory) does not grant access to other characters' interiority. Every instance follows the same pattern: "the voice/face/hands of a man who had [internal state]." The fix is consistent: render the observable behavior and let the reader infer.

**Secondary failure mode: Meta-address (4 of 18).** The narrator steps back four times to comment on his own act of writing — justifying descriptions, referencing paragraphs, explaining chapter structure. These break the fictional frame. Three of four occur at section breaks (lines 29, 71, 169), suggesting a pattern where the transition between sections triggers narrator self-consciousness.

**Cluster concern — Lines 119–127:** Five violations in nine lines (9, 10, 11, 12, 13). This passage — the moment of Tomás's death — has the highest violation density in the chapter. The emotional intensity of the scene appears to have pulled the prose toward explanation and attribution rather than rendering. This is the section most in need of revision.

## What the Chapter Does Well

- The mortar walk (lines 37–38) is rendered with mechanical precision — counting, moving, repeating. No telling.
- Tomás's water organizing (lines 45–47) is shown through specific observable actions: the schedule, the canteen, the afternoon without drinking.
- The physical act of holding Tomás's body (lines 121, 135–139) is Level 5 rendered consciousness — weight, warmth, blood, dust.
- The scapular sequence (lines 143–147) is rendered through hands, wool, cord, pocket. No emotional naming.
- The mother's visit (lines 183–195) is mostly strong — the doorframe, the fingers closing around the scapular, the closed door. Only the attributed knowledge (line 185) and attributed love (line 191) break the rendering.
- The chapter ending (lines 197–207) is clean and earns its emotional weight through specificity.
