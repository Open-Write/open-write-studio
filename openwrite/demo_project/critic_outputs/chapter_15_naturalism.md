## Assessment

This chapter uses a cataloguing, ritualistic voice — noun phrases, triplets, polysyndeton, negation — to render a man reckoning with what he fought for and what was lost. The voice is deliberate. The patterns are chosen, not accidental.

But the density exceeds what the voice requires. When every section uses the same devices at the same intensity, the devices lose their power. The em-dashes in the Aoiz kitchen should feel different from the em-dashes in the parade. The triplets in the green valley passage should land differently from the triplets in the final inventory. They don't — because the pattern load is uniform.

The hard failure is "the same" at 17 (limit 12). The advisory failures are universal. The chapter would benefit from:

1. Reducing "the same" to ≤12 (cut 5 instances, prioritizing L133 and L19)
2. Breaking the "The" opener pattern in at least 3 sections (swap to subject-first or verb-first sentences)
3. Reducing em-dashes in the first half (L5–L29) where they cluster heaviest
4. Varying polysyndeton intensity — some sections should use fewer "and" chains, not more

The narrator's voice is strong. The chapter earns its closing weight. But the naturalism scaffolding is visible through the prose.
