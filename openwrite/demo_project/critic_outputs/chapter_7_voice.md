## 1. Voice Spec Compliance (V1-A — Immediate Reckoning)

**POV:** First-person retrospective. PASS. Martín narrates throughout. No omniscient leakage. No other character's interiority accessed directly.

**Tense:** Past for remembered events, present for writing frame. PASS. The chapter opens and closes in present tense ("I am at the table. The inkwell is full."), with the remembered events in past. Clean frame.

**Register:** Confessional, intimate, weight of hindsight. PASS. The narrator's tone is measured, retrospective, the voice of a man who now knows what the crusade became — but does not editorialize. The closing paragraph ("He has yet to understand what it means that he recognizes it. He will learn.") is the narrator looking at the boy with full knowledge, not pity.

**Immediacy Collapse:** PRESENT but restrained. The sensory bridges work — "My eyes could find no difference" collapses past perception and present knowledge. The writing frame anchors the act of reckoning. The body-recognition of the bridge ("my chest did something I had no word for") is the immediacy technique operating through the body rather than through explicit "I can feel it now" phrasing.

**Numbness Insight:** Not foregrounded in this chapter. The chapter's moral center is recognition, not numbness — appropriate for Act II, where the cracks are forming but have not yet opened.

**Rhythm:** PASS. Long reflective sentences broken by short declaratives: "I looked away. I looked at the church." / "Nobody cheered." / "He smiled. I did not." The fragments are earned: "Green mountains. A crucifix I knew. A language my grandmother spoke. A flag in the mud." This is the Fracture register beginning to surface.

**Vocabulary:** PASS. Concrete nouns dominate: the beret, the crucifix, the altar rail, the chart, the flag, the river, the stone. No false elevation. No ornament.

### "There Was" Expletive — 3 Instances

1. **Line 71:** "between us there was a silence full of the prayers" — The expletive delays the subject. Rewrite: "between us, a silence full of the prayers"
2. **Line 161:** "There was no mark." — Strong, declarative, final. The expletive serves the rhythm. Borderline.
3. **Line 243:** "because there is no line. There never was." — Present-tense writing frame. "There is no line" could be "no line exists" but the rhythm of "There is no line. There never was." is powerful. Borderline.

**Assessment:** Line 71 is a clear Tier 1 violation. Lines 161 and 243 are borderline — the expletive serves the declarative rhythm. Flag 71 for revision; leave 161 and 243.

### Triadic Parallel — Advisory (Non-Blocking)

The chapter uses polysyndeton chains extensively. Notable instances:

- **Line 35:** "a church and a plaza and a school and streets of stone and a river" (4+ items)
- **Line 43:** "at windows and at windows and along the edges" (rhythmic, intentional)
- **Line 55:** "the smell of stone and candle wax and incense" (3 items — sensory inventory)
- **Line 155:** "woodsmoke and wet grass and the river" (3 items — sensory)

The format rule permits triadic parallels "when the moment specifically calls for three beats." Many of these are sensory inventories — the narrator cataloguing what he perceives — which is consistent with the Soldier register's concrete attention. However, the density (11 instances across 4,668 words) risks becoming the default rhythm rather than a deliberate choice. **Advisory: reduce to 6-7 instances, breaking some chains into separate sentences.**

## 4. Additional Findings

### Repetition of "the same" — 58 instances
This is a thematic refrain — the narrator's inability to distinguish "their" church from "his" church, "their" crucifix from "his" crucifix. The repetition is architecturally intentional and works as a structural device. However, 58 instances in 4,668 words (1 per ~80 words) risks numbing the reader to the phrase. **Advisory: prune to ~35-40 instances, keeping the strongest repetitions in the church scene and the closing.**

### "My eyes" as sentence opener — 9 instances
A pattern emerges: "My eyes adjusted." / "My eyes found it and stayed." / "My eyes moved across the room." / "My eyes went to it." / "My eyes held the village." / "My eyes moved over everything." / "My eyes could find no difference." This is the Soldier register — concrete, physical, body-anchored — but the repetition of the opener creates an unintentional tic. **Advisory: vary 3-4 of these to break the pattern.**
