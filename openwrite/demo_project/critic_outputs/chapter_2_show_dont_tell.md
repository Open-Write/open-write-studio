## Summary

Chapter 2 is predominantly strong on the show-don't-tell axis. The sensory rendering of the meseta, the physical characterization of Tomás and Vidal, the dialogue-as-subtext, and the landscape-as-consciousness work are all at a high level. The chapter earns its length through specificity. However, there are recurring violations concentrated in two areas: (1) the retrospective narrator naming emotions directly, and (2) the retrospective narrator stepping outside the boy's moment to provide interpretive commentary on what the boy did not know. These are not catastrophic — the novel's frame is first-person retrospective, so some interpretive distance is structural — but the naming and telling patterns can be rendered instead.

## Findings

### Finding 1: Named emotion — "vertigo"

**Location:** Line 57
**Text:** "I looked at it and felt the vertigo of a man who has always lived under a ceiling and is suddenly standing under nothing."
**Violation type:** Named emotion
**Analysis:** "Vertigo" is an emotion label. The clause that follows — "a man who has always lived under a ceiling and is suddenly standing under nothing" — is a strong rendering of the *experience*, but the word "vertigo" names it first and the elaboration explains it. The prose would be stronger if it rendered the physical sensation without the label.
**Suggested fix:** "I looked at it and the sky went all the way down and my chest opened and my eyes had nothing to hold — the buildings and the church towers and the river were five hundred kilometers behind me and above me there was nothing. Only the stars pressing through."
**Severity:** Moderate

### Finding 2: Named emotion — "assessment"

**Location:** Line 39
**Text:** "his face moved through something I could not read. Not concern. Not displeasure. Assessment."
**Violation type:** Named emotion
**Analysis:** The narrator labels Tomás's expression as "Assessment." This is the narrator interpreting the face rather than rendering what it looked like. The preceding negations ("Not concern. Not displeasure") are a step toward specificity but then collapse into a category label.
**Suggested fix:** Render the face: "his face moved through something I could not read. His eyes narrowed. His mouth pressed flat. The face of a man counting distances."
**Severity:** Low-moderate

### Finding 3: Named emotion — "I admired this"

**Location:** Line 147
**Text:** "I admired this. I want to say I did not, because in the years since Vidal has become something else [...] But I admired it."
**Violation type:** Named emotion (with meta-awareness)
**Analysis:** The narrator names "admired" three times. The surrounding passage is otherwise strong — the craft comparison (father's iron, Vidal's men) is rendered consciousness. But the emotion is labeled, not shown. The narrator's own hedging ("I want to say I did not") acknowledges the naming but does not replace it with rendering.
**Suggested fix:** Cut the first "I admired this" and the final "I admired the competence." Let the craft comparison stand as the rendered perception. The reader will understand the admiration from the specificity of the observation: "The precision was the same. The economy was the same. The refusal to waste."
**Severity:** Moderate

### Finding 4: Telling-not-rendering — faith passage

**Location:** Lines 93-95
**Text:** "It was not easy. It was not natural. It was a choice I made every morning [...] The faith was work. I did not know this then. I thought faith was a thing you carried, like the rifle or the canteen. I did not know it was a thing you built, like a wall, stone by stone, and that the wall could be knocked down."
**Violation type:** Telling-not-rendering; named interiority
**Analysis:** This passage *tells* us what faith was ("work," "a thing you built") rather than rendering the experience of building it. The sentence "I did not know this then" is a retrospective narrator's thesis statement, not a rendered moment. The preceding paragraph (line 91-92) — the crucifix, the groove, the thumb, the three generations — is excellent rendered consciousness. The telling passage that follows explains what the rendered passage already conveyed.
**Suggested fix:** Cut lines 93-95 entirely. The preceding paragraph (touching the crucifix, the groove, the wood warm from the body) already renders the faith as something held and built. The next line ("But I am ahead of myself. That morning, in the church, on the feast of Santiago, the wall was intact.") provides the retrospective frame without the thesis statement.
**Severity:** Moderate-high

### Finding 5: Telling-not-rendering — "Recognition"

**Location:** Line 135
**Text:** "But something in his eyes shifted — not warmth, exactly. Recognition. The recognition of one professional for another, even if the other's profession was rabbits."
**Violation type:** Named emotion; telling-not-rendering
**Analysis:** "Recognition" is an emotion label. The elaboration ("one professional for another") is the narrator interpreting Vidal's interiority — what Vidal was feeling — which the POV character cannot access. The line "even if the other's profession was rabbits" is a good writerly touch, but the interiority leak is real.
**Suggested fix:** Render what Martín sees: "But something in his eyes shifted. He looked at Tomás the way my father looked at a man who knew iron — a nod, brief, the kind that says: you know your work." This keeps the observation in Martín's perceptual range.
**Severity:** Moderate

### Finding 6: Omniscience leak — "what the boy did not know" (first instance)

**Location:** Lines 199-205
**Text:** "I did not know that the breadth was the danger. I did not know that a coalition that does not share a name does not share a purpose [...] That the fueros would be abolished. That the red beret would bear a new symbol. That the king would not come."
**Violation type:** Authorial intrusion; invisible information
**Analysis:** The retrospective narrator provides a political thesis (coalition without shared name → purpose redefined by the powerful) and specific historical outcomes (fueros abolished, beret repurposed, king absent). This is the narrator at the table, thirty years later, explaining what the scene *means*. The boy on the bench did not know these things, and the prose tells us he did not know rather than rendering the gap between his perception and reality through scene.
**Suggested fix:** Reduce to a single line of retrospective frame and let the boy's actions speak: "The boy on the bench did not know." Then cut directly to the next section. The reader already has enough from the fueros conversation to understand the gap. The specifics (abolished, repurposed, king) belong in later chapters where they happen, not here as prophecy.
**Severity:** Moderate-high

### Finding 7: Omniscience leak — "what the boy did not know" (second instance)

**Location:** Lines 237-239
**Text:** "He does not know that the meseta will become the landscape of the war [...] and that each crossing will strip something from him: the green, the hills, the river, the certainty. He does not know that the man on the bench was the first of many [...] men who would keep their names and their lands and their churches while the fueros disappeared into the archive of a dictatorship that used the requeté's blood as mortar and discarded the bricks."
**Violation type:** Authorial intrusion; invisible information; pure summary
**Analysis:** This is the chapter's closing passage and it is entirely retrospective prophecy. The narrator tells us what the war will do, what the meseta will mean, what will happen to the fueros. None of this is rendered through the boy's experience. The final metaphor ("blood as mortar and discarded the bricks") is the narrator's interpretive flourish. The line immediately before this passage — "He knows the road. He knows the beret." — is rendered and strong. The prophecy that follows undermines it.
**Suggested fix:** End the chapter at "He knew the road. He knew the beret. He knew the crucifix and the blessing and the stocky man beside him who had said we fight, we win, we go home and who believed it the way the boy believed the bells." This is a complete ending. The prophecy is unnecessary — the reader has the scene, the fueros conversation, the landscape, the contrast between Martín and Tomás. Let the scene do the work.
**Severity:** High

### Finding 8: Telling-not-rendering — "I realized"

**Location:** Line 181
**Text:** "I realized that I did not have an answer that would fit in his world."
**Violation type:** Named interiority process
**Analysis:** "I realized" is a telling hinge word (flagged in format rules: "She felt that," "he thought that," "they realized that"). The content that follows is rendered — the man's face, the crucifix, the waiting — but the "realized" names the cognitive process rather than rendering it.
**Suggested fix:** Cut "I realized that": "I opened my mouth. I closed it. I looked at the man and his face was open and waiting and I did not have an answer that would fit in his world."
**Severity:** Low

## Overall Assessment

**Strengths:**
- Landscape rendering is excellent. The meseta passage (lines 11-13) is among the best in the chapter — sky-as-floor, the eye reaching for something to hold.
- Tomás is characterized entirely through behavior: sleeping, eating, writing, speaking in facts. No emotion is ever named for him.
- Dialogue is clean. No character names their own emotional state. Tomás's "We fight. We win. We go home." is pure subtext.
- The sensory layering is strong: heat stored under the skin, the vibration of the truck putting the narrator on the train, the groove in the crucifix.
- The fueros conversation (lines 159-195) is rendered consciousness at its best — the man's face, the parallel to land, the gap between what the word means and what the man needs it to mean.

**Patterns to address:**
1. The retrospective narrator's tendency to name emotions (vertigo, assessment, admiration) rather than let the rendered detail carry the weight.
2. The "what the boy did not know" refrain — used twice (lines 199-205, 237-239) — functions as authorial thesis rather than rendered scene. Both instances would be stronger if cut or reduced to a single line.
3. The faith passage (lines 93-95) tells what the preceding paragraph already showed. Trust the crucifix scene; cut the thesis.

**Findings by severity:**
- High: 1 (Finding 7 — closing prophecy)
- Moderate-high: 2 (Findings 4, 6 — faith passage, first omniscience leak)
- Moderate: 3 (Findings 1, 3, 5 — vertigo, admiration, recognition)
- Low-moderate: 1 (Finding 2 — assessment)
- Low: 1 (Finding 8 — "I realized")

**Verdict: PASS with 8 findings.** The chapter's prose is strong and earns its length. The violations are concentrated in the retrospective narrator's interpretive commentary, not in the scene work itself. The scene-to-summary ratio is healthy. No dialogue tags with adverbs. No banned literary clichés detected. No AI tic patterns (no "Not X but Y," no "The particular X of Y," no hedge words).
