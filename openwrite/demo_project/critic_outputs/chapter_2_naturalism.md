## CRITICAL Findings

### C1. Em-Dash Overuse — 3.27 per 250 words (threshold: 2/250w)

81 em-dashes in 6,192 words. This is 63.5% above the threshold. The em-dash is used as the default parenthetical, appositive, and aside device, creating a rhythmic sameness across paragraphs.

**Finding C1a — Em-dash used as appositive chain:**

> "I saw a man walking across the square with a map case over his shoulder and binoculars around his neck and the officer's cap on his head — not a beret, the cap, the peaked thing with the insignia, the thing that said Army, not requeté. He was tall. Lean. Angular. His boots were polished. His uniform was clean — not new, but maintained, the creases sharp, the buttons bright."

— Lines 101, em-dash density in this paragraph: 3x in 130 words (6.9/250w, triple threshold). **Severity:** Critical. **Fix:** Convert at least one em-dash to a period or colon. "His uniform was clean. Not new, but maintained."

**Finding C1b — Em-dash as aside/explanation device:**

> "He slept the way he ate — without ceremony, without transition, as if sleep were a task he had been assigned and intended to complete."

— Line 9. **Severity:** Critical. **Fix:** "He slept the way he ate. No ceremony, no transition."

**Finding C1c — Triple em-dash paragraph:**

> "Padre Joaquín was three cars ahead. I did not see him on the train. I heard about it later — that he had said Mass at dawn in the Pamplona station before we boarded, using a wooden crate as an altar, his blanket over his shoulders despite the heat, his voice carrying across the platform to the men who were already forming lines."

— Line 19, 3 em-dashes in one paragraph. **Severity:** Critical. **Fix:** Break the aside into its own sentence. "I heard about it later. He had said Mass at dawn."

**Finding C1d — Em-dash in dialogue attribution:**

> "He said it the way he said most things — as a fact, not a complaint."

— Line 41. **Severity:** Critical. **Fix:** "He said it the way he said most things. A fact, not a complaint."

## Priority Recommendations

1. **Reduce em-dashes from 81 to ~50** (target: 2/250w). Convert appositive em-dashes to periods or commas. Reserve em-dashes for genuine interruption or surprise.
2. **Cut negative constructions by 30%** (target: <15/1k). The Vidal section (30.6/1k) and fueros section (29.1/1k) need the most work. Replace "He did not X, he did not Y" with positive assertions.
3. **Replace 10+ of 21 "the way" instances.** Vary with "as," "like," direct comparison, or restructure the sentence entirely.
4. **Break 20+ triplet lists.** Use single strong details instead of three-item catalogs. The closing paragraphs (L229, L237) are the worst offenders.
5. **Vary frame-narrator breaks.** The "I am at the table / the inkwell is full" structure should change on each return.
