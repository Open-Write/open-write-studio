# Chapter 4 Plan — "The Siege"

## 1. Chapter Number and Title
Chapter 4 — "The Siege"

## 2. Characters Present
- **Martín Javier Echeverría** (POV) — active registers: The Soldier (primary), The Catechism (cracking)
- **Tomás Eguía** — active registers: The Companion, The Brother
- **Padre Joaquín** — active register: The Pastor (blessing the dead)
- **Teniente Rodrigo Vidal** — active registers: The Officer, The Realist (background)

## 3. POV Character
Martín Javier Echeverría. First-person retrospective with immediacy technique. The narrator is Martín ~30 years later. The remembered Martín is 22, at the outskirts of Madrid, watching the dream of a quick victory die.

## 4. Narrative Distance
First-person retrospective with immediacy collapse. This chapter demands middle distance — the scale of Madrid requires the prose to pull back from the extreme close-up of Ch3. The body is still present but the eye is wider: the city, the sounds, the Army of Africa, the dead. Modulate between close-up (specific moments — a dead man, a Regulares soldier) and middle distance (the siege as a whole).

## 5. Consciousness Markers
The remembered Martín notices:
- **Scale.** Madrid is not a village. The sounds are different — artillery, machine guns, the scale of organized death. The meseta was wide; Madrid is dense.
- **The Army of Africa.** Moorish Regulares, Legionnaires. Men who fight differently. Professional, brutal, efficient. Martín recognizes competence in a register he has not seen — not Vidal's clipped professionalism but something harder, more violent, colonial.
- **The dead.** Padre Joaquín blessing corpses. The bodies from the failed assault. The physical fact of death at scale.
- **What he does NOT notice:** That the assault's failure means the war will be long. He reads the failure as a setback, not as the end of the quick-victory narrative.

## 6. POV Constraints
- The narrator knows the siege of Madrid will last years. The boy does not.
- The Army of Africa is rendered only through what Martín can see — their bearing, their weapons, their faces, the way the requeté look at them.
- The sounds of battle are heard from the reserve line, not experienced in combat.

## 7. Outline Summary
November 1936. The battalion reaches the outskirts of Madrid. The Nationalist assault is underway — Army of Africa troops, Regulares, Legionnaires. The requeté are held in reserve. Martín hears the sounds of the battle — artillery, machine guns, the International Brigades entering the city. The assault fails. The Nationalists are thrown back. Martín watches from the reserve line as the dream of a quick victory dies. He sees the Army of Africa for the first time — Moorish Regulares, Legionnaires. Men who fight differently than the Carlist volunteers. Professional, brutal, efficient. Martín is awed and disturbed. Tomás is pragmatic: "At least they're on our side." Padre Joaquín blesses the dead from the assault. The requeté are not yet in the worst of it. But the war has changed scale. This is not a village. This is a capital.

## 8. State Changes

- **After:** The assault has failed. The war will be long. The Army of Africa is visible — men who fight differently than the requeté. The scale has changed.
- **Padre Joaquín:** Has blessed the dead from a failed assault. The blessing is still certain, but it has been used for the dead, not just the living.

## 11. Reader-Belief Phase
**Misdirection active, first crack.** The reader still believes this is a war story, but the failed assault introduces the first hint that the crusade will not be quick. The crack is subtle — the reader reads it as a setback, not as the end of the narrative. The misdirection is still intact.

## 12. Reader-State Guidance
- **Must convey:** The scale of Madrid. The sounds. The Army of Africa. The dead. The failed assault.
- **Must NOT convey:** That the war will be long. That the crusade's narrative is broken. The reader should feel the awe and dread without understanding their full implications.
- **Can plant (subtly):** The first crack — the quick victory is not coming. But this is a seed, not a signal.

## 13. Emotional Palette

Dread is secondary (the failed assault, the dead, the hint that this will be longer than anyone promised).

## Notes for the Prose Writer

1. **The sounds of Madrid are the chapter's anchor.** This is the first time Martín hears war at scale. Render the soundscape: artillery as vibration, machine guns as rhythm, the city as a body under bombardment. Use ears as the primary body anchor — new for this chapter.
2. **The Army of Africa must be rendered with awe and dread simultaneously.** Their competence is real. Their brutality is real. Martín is both impressed and disturbed. Do not resolve the tension.
3. **The failed assault is the chapter's thematic hinge.** The dream of quick victory dies. But the boy does not know this yet. The narrator knows. The gap is the chapter's dramatic irony.
4. **Padre Joaquín blessing the dead is the chapter's spiritual beat.** The same hands, the same voice, the same blessing — but the bodies are different from the living. Render the physical difference.
5. **Tomás's "At least they're on our side" must land as pragmatism, not as foreshadowing.** The narrator knows; the boy does not.
6. **Watch for meta-critic patterns:** Chapter endings (no interpretive commentary), "I did not know" (max 1), "the way" (≤2/1k), polysyndeton (≤25% of lines).
