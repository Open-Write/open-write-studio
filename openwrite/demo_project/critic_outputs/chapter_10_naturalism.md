## Findings

### 1. "There was" existential expletive — CRITICAL (Tier 1 ban)

Three instances of the banned "There was" construction.

- **L119:** "There was only the cold, and Tomás's body against mine, and the sound of his breathing"
- **L133:** "There was only the body's need, and the body accepts no argument."
- **L137:** "He said Mass. There was no altar. He used a desk."

**Fixes:**
- L119: "Only the cold remained, and Tomás's body against mine" — or restructure: "The cold was all that existed between us."
- L133: "Only the body's need remained, and the body accepts no argument."
- L137: "He said Mass. No altar stood. He used a desk." — or: "He said Mass off a desk. He put the prayer book on the wood."

### 2. "Not X but Y" construction — CRITICAL (threshold: 0)

One instance of the banned contrastive negation pattern.

- **L25:** "The snow was fine — not the soft flakes of Navarre but hard, dry, driven sideways by the wind."

**Fix:** Convert to positive assertion: "The snow was fine — hard, dry, driven sideways by the wind. Nothing like the soft flakes of Navarre." Or: "The snow was fine. Hard. Dry. Driven sideways by the wind." Let the physical detail do the work without the contrastive frame.

### 3. Triplet closings as default rhythm — MODERATE

38 lines contain 3+ parallel comma-separated items. The triplet list is the chapter's primary descriptive device, used for cold sensations, body parts, burial rites, and combat sequences alike.

Worst offenders:
- **L5:** "the fingers, the toes, the nose, the ears" — body enumeration
- **L7:** "like water soaking into cloth. Then the arms and legs, the muscles stiffening, the joints locking" — cold progression
- **L43:** "the same words he had said in the barns and the fields and the plazas and the ditches" — 4 items
- **L47:** "the same words for every body, the same breath, the same hand on the blanket" — triple "the same"
- **L55:** "The officers and the NCOs and the requeté section leaders" — 3 items
- **L89:** "the boots, then the legs, then the torsos, then the arms, then the faces" — 5 items
- **L109:** "the scarves and the scarves became masks of ice" — repetitive structure
- **L127:** "the streets, the houses, the buildings" / "from the bombardment, from the cold, from the two months of fighting" — double triplet in one line
- **L151:** "the muscles softening, the joints loosening, the skin losing the tight, burning feeling" — thawing body

The L89 burial passage ("first the boots, then the legs, then the torsos, then the arms, then the faces") earns its list — the incremental covering is the point. But the L5 body-part enumeration, L7 cold progression, L55 officer list, and L127 building list use the same device for inventory rather than emotional effect.

**Recommendation:** Break 50%+ of these into varied structures. L5: "First the extremities — fingers, toes, nose, ears. Then the hands and feet." L55: "The officers gathered. The NCOs. The requeté section leaders." L127: "Into the streets. Into the houses. The buildings." Use fragments and asyndeton for inventory. Reserve the full triplet for moments that accumulate toward an emotional peak.

### 4. Polysyndeton chains — MODERATE

15 sentences contain 3+ "and" conjunctions. The polysyndeton is the chapter's secondary rhythmic device, often compounding the triplet problem.

Worst offenders:
- **L5:** "and the cold is in my hands, and my hands are shaking, and I cannot make them stop" — 3 "and" (frame-breaking: effective here)
- **L13:** "and the stars were hard and bright and they gave no warmth" — 3 "and"
- **L43:** "in the barns and the fields and the plazas and the ditches" — 4 "and"
- **L49:** "the habit would break and the cold would win and he would freeze" — 3 "and"
- **L83:** "he was trying to work the bolt and the bolt was frozen and his fingers would not close" — 3 "and" (effective: escalating failure)
- **L85:** "the snow was white around him and then it was red and then it was white again" — 3 "and" (effective: color cycle)
- **L119:** "There was only the cold, and Tomás's body against mine, and the sound of his breathing, and the sound of the wind" — 4 "and"
- **L127:** "we fired up and the bullets punched through the frozen walls and the plaster fell in chunks" — 3 "and"
- **L155:** "his body and his blankets and his bread and his voice in the dark" — 4 "and"
- **L165:** "the road ahead and the mountains behind and the sun on the frozen ground and the steam rising" — 4 "and"

L83 (the boy's frozen bolt) and L85 (the snow color cycle) earn their polysyndeton — the "and" enacts the futility and the burial. L5 (the frame-breaking opening) works as a deliberate entry into the body's memory. But L43, L49, L119, L155, L165 use polysyndeton as default rhythm rather than earned effect.

**Recommendation:** Cut polysyndeton in L43 ("the barns, the fields, the plazas, the ditches" — asyndeton), L49 ("the habit would break. The cold would win." — period), L119 (remove one "and"), L155 ("his body, his blankets, his bread, his voice in the dark" — comma list), L165 (break into two sentences). Keep L5, L83, L85.

### 5. Em-dash density — FLAG (at threshold)

58 em-dashes in ~18 pages = **3.2/page**. The threshold flags >2/page; critical is 5+/page. This is moderate.

Many em-dashes introduce elaboration or apposition:

- **L19:** "I can feel it now — the way the cold entered the body. An occupation."
- **L39:** "His face was white. His lips were blue. His eyes were open. The frost had collected on his eyelashes, on his eyebrows, on the stubble of his jaw."
- **L47:** "His breath rising, his cassock visible under the blanket, his limp heavy but his pace steady."
- **L99:** "A voice stripped of warmth, of pastoral care. A voice of a man who had said the same words so many times they had worn smooth, like a stone in a river, the edges gone, the meaning compressed into the shape of the sounds themselves."

L117 uses the em-dash pair effectively — the parenthetical interruption mirrors the fleeting warmth. L19's dash works as a pivot into the body-memory frame. But many dashes (L7, L39, L47, L99, L133) introduce elaboration that could be integrated or set as separate sentences.

**Recommendation:** Reduce to ~35 em-dashes (~2/page). Convert elaboration dashes at L7, L39, L47, L99 to periods or commas. Reserve dashes for genuine interruptions (L117) and frame breaks (L5, L19).

### 6. "the way" repetition — FLAG

4 instances of the comparative "the way" construction:
- **L5:** "the way my father taught me"
- **L19:** "the way the cold entered the body"
- **L31:** "the way the bread came out of the oven"
- **L49:** "the way Ibarra had frozen, the way the others had frozen"

L5 and L19 are the strongest — L5 anchors the frame, L19 pivots into the body-memory. L31 is Tomás's remembered warmth, character-appropriate. But L49 doubles up ("the way Ibarra had frozen, the way the others had frozen") in a single sentence, which makes the pattern visible.

**Recommendation:** Cut L49's second "the way" — "the way Ibarra had frozen, the others too, their bodies finding the position of least resistance" — or restructure entirely. Keep L5, L19, L31.

### 7. Metric monotony (prose audit) — FLAG

The prose audit flagged 26 instances of "metric monotony" — 5+ consecutive sentences within 2 words of the same length. The chapter uses many short declarative sentences (3-6 words) in sequence:

- **L39-L41:** "His face was white. His lips were blue. His eyes were open." / "He looked like a statue." / "Someone tried to close his eyes."
- **L45-L47:** "He stood. He moved to the next trench." / "Padre Joaquín knelt again. Said the same words. Stood. Moved on." / "He stood, he knelt, he spoke, he rose."
- **L131:** "Tomás fired. The man fell. Another appeared. Tomás fired. The man fell."
- **L141:** "Slow. Deliberate. One at a time."
- **L167:** "Tomás watched the road. His breath was steady. His steps were steady. The pulse in his neck was steady."

The L39 frozen-body passage and L131 combat sequence earn their staccato — the short sentences enact the stillness and the mechanical repetition of killing. L141 ("Slow. Deliberate.") works as a rhythmic landing after Padre Joaquín's descent. But L45-L47 (the blessing round) and L167 (the final road) use the same staccato for very different emotional registers, flattening the contrast.

**Recommendation:** Vary L45-L47 — let one sentence breathe longer ("He moved to the next trench, and the next, his breath rising in white plumes, his cassock visible under the military blanket"). Keep L131 and L141 as-is. For L167, the triple "steady" is the point — keep but consider whether the preceding staccato lines need it.

## Clean Passages (evidence of critical reading)

### > L113-115: "Tomás kept me alive. I will say it plainly because there is no other way to say it. He kept me alive."
Passes because the repetition of "He kept me alive" is earned — the narrator is forcing himself to state the fact without decoration. The "I will say it plainly" is a metacommentary that justifies the directness. The second "He kept me alive" lands as weight, not as AI loop.

### > L83-85: "He had a rifle in his hands and he was trying to work the bolt and the bolt was frozen and his fingers would not close. / I shot him. He fell. He lay in the snow and the snow was white around him and then it was red and then it was white again as the falling snow covered the red."
Passes because the polysyndeton in L83 enacts the boy's fumbling failure — each "and" adds another layer of inability. The L85 color cycle (white → red → white) is the chapter's most precise image, and the polysyndeton ("and then... and then...") mirrors the snow's incremental erasure. This is earned rhythm, not default.

### > L117: "I felt Tomás press his face against the back of my neck. His nose was cold. His lips were cracked. His breath was warm for a moment — a small, desperate warmth, the body's last argument against the cold — and then it cooled and became the cold again."
Passes because the em-dash pair does real work — the parenthetical is a held breath, a moment of warmth interrupting the cold narrative, and the closing dash releases it back into the cold. The sentence length varies (short physical details, then the longer parenthetical, then the short release). This is the chapter's best sentence.
