## Violations

### 1. Named emotional state — "recognition"

> "His face had a look I had seen before, a look of recognition, the eyes soft, the mouth loose"

**Location:** Line 15
**Type:** Named emotional state
**Problem:** "A look of recognition" names the emotion. The physical details that follow (eyes soft, mouth loose) do the rendering work, but the label preempts them.
**Fix:** Cut "a look of recognition." Let the physical details carry it: "His face had a look I had seen before — the eyes soft, the mouth loose, the way a man looks when he hears a song from childhood."

### 2. Hedged physical symptom — "something I had no word for"

> "my chest did something I had no word for"

**Location:** Line 23
**Type:** Hedged physical symptom
**Problem:** Format rules Level 3 (physical symptom) should be specific. "Did something I had no word for" is a hedge — it gestures at a sensation without rendering it. The rules say: *Better, but generic* for physical symptoms; *Specific physical detail* is the target.
**Fix:** Render the specific sensation. Example: "my chest pulled tight, the breath shallow, the way it pulls before a cry that does not come." Or cut the sentence entirely — the bridge description that precedes it is already strong.

### 3. Telling — "the small courtesies that towns offer to liberating armies"

> "They did not offer bread or wine or the small courtesies that towns offer to liberating armies."

**Location:** Line 45
**Type:** Telling-not-rendering
**Problem:** "The small courtesies that towns offer to liberating armies" is abstract summary. It tells the reader what typically happens instead of rendering what this specific town did not do. The first half of the sentence (bread, wine) is concrete; the second half generalizes.
**Fix:** Cut the abstraction. Let the specific absences stand: "They did not offer bread or wine. They did not shake our hands. They watched."

### 10. Omniscience leak — "the face of a man who has decided to endure"

> "in them I saw something I recognized from the mirror, from the mornings in the trench before an attack, from the face of a man who has decided to endure."

**Location:** Line 171
**Type:** Omniscience leak
**Problem:** Martín cannot know what the old man has decided. "The face of a man who has decided to endure" attributes interiority — a decision, a resolve — to a character whose mind Martín cannot access. The format rules are explicit: *The narrator does not have access to other characters' interiority.*
**Fix:** Render what Martín sees, not what it means: "from the face of a man who has been here before, who has seen men in uniform come and go, who sits on the bench and does not move." Or use the mirror comparison alone: "in them I saw something I recognized from the mirror, from the mornings in the trench before an attack."

## Ending Analysis (Lines 207–243)

The final 15% of the chapter contains **8 of the 20 violations** (#14–#20 plus #13's second clause). The pattern is clear: the rendered scenes end at line 221 (Padre Joaquín walking away), and the remaining 22 lines shift into a mode the chapter has not earned — the narrator interpreting, summarizing, and explaining.

The final paragraph (lines 241–243) is the worst offender. It contains:
- A shift to third-person ("the boy") that creates narrative distance
- Prophetic summary ("He will learn. The war will teach him.")
- Direct thematic statement ("there is no line. There never was.")
- Repetition of "the same" five times, which is a triadic-parallel escalation that the format rules flag

The rendered chapter — the arrival, the silence, the church, the school, the flag, the old man, the Mass — is strong. It does not need the narrator to explain what it means. The ending should trust the reader.

**Recommendation:** Cut lines 231 ("His eyes said what his mouth did not" through "And you have taken my town"), 233, 235 (the "I think of" catalog and future-knowledge clause), 237, and 241–243. End the chapter at line 229 ("her voice behind me, shaping the sounds, patient, repeating them until my mouth could hold them") or at line 230 ("A, B, D, E. My grandmother's letters."). Let the alphabet chart be the final image. Let the silence do the work.
