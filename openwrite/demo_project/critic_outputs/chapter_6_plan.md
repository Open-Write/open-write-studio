# Chapter 6 Plan — "The Field of Jarama" (END OF ACT TWO)

## 1. Chapter Number and Title
Chapter 6 — "The Field of Jarama"

## 2. Characters Present
- **Martín Javier Echeverría** (POV) — active registers: The Soldier (primary), The Catechism (fading)
- **Tomás Eguía** — active registers: The Companion, The Brother
- **Padre Joaquín** — active register: The Pastor (hearing confessions, the doubt leaking)
- **Teniente Rodrigo Vidal** — active registers: The Officer, The Realist

## 3. POV Character
Martín Javier Echeverría. First-person retrospective with immediacy technique. The remembered Martín is 22, in a trench at Jarama, fighting the International Brigades.

## 4. Narrative Distance
First-person retrospective with immediacy collapse. This chapter demands close distance — the trench, the snipers, the mortar fire, the constant noise. The body is in the ground. The ears are full. The prose must be tight, physical, claustrophobic.

## 5. Consciousness Markers
The remembered Martín notices:
- **The trench.** The dirt, the sandbags, the corrugated tin, the smell of men and earth and cordite. The physical fact of living in a hole.
- **The enemy.** The International Brigades — German volunteers, British volunteers, men from fifty countries. Not the godless Reds of propaganda. Some are Catholic. Some are socialist. Some just came to fight fascism. The gap between rhetoric and reality widens.
- **The sound.** Snipers, mortar fire, the constant noise. The soundscape of a war that does not pause.
- **Padre Joaquín.** Hearing confessions at night. The confessions grow longer. The priest's voice is changing.

## 6. POV Constraints
- The narrator knows the Jarama front will stalemate. The boy does not.
- The International Brigades are rendered only through what Martín can see and hear — voices, uniforms, the sound of different languages.
- Padre Joaquín's doubt is visible only through behavior — shorter homilies, longer pauses, the voice changing.

## 7. Outline Summary
February 1937. The battalion is moved to the Jarama front — south of Madrid, where the Nationalists are trying to cut the Madrid-Valencia road. The fighting is intense. The requeté hold a section of the line. Martín and Tomás share a trench. The International Brigades are across the line — German volunteers, British volunteers, men from fifty countries. The requeté are fighting not Spaniards but foreigners. The propaganda said the war was against godless Reds. The men across the line are not godless — some are Catholic, some are socialist, some are just volunteers who came to fight fascism. The trench warfare is brutal — snipers, mortar fire, the constant noise. Padre Joaquín hears confessions at night. The confessions grow longer. The chapter ends with a stalemate — neither side advances. The requeté hold. The cost is seventeen dead.

- **Padre Joaquín:** The confessions are growing longer. The doubt is leaking into the pastoral register.

## 11. Reader-Belief Phase
**Misdirection cracking, deepening.** The reader now knows the crusade is being absorbed (Ch5). This chapter deepens the crack: the enemy is not who the propaganda said. The International Brigades are not godless. The rhetoric is failing. The reader should feel the grind — and the gap between what the war was supposed to be and what it is.

## 12. Reader-State Guidance
- **Must convey:** The trench's physical reality. The International Brigades as real people, not propaganda targets. The cost (seventeen dead). Padre Joaquín's confessions growing longer.
- **Must NOT convey:** That the crusade is over. The reader should feel the grind without yet understanding its full implications.
- **Can plant:** The gap between rhetoric and reality. The enemy is not godless. The confessions are growing longer.

## 13. Emotional Palette
**Weariness** and **Doubt.** The reader should feel the war's grind — and the gap between the crusade's rhetoric and the reality of men in trenches fighting men they do not know. Weariness is primary (the body in the dirt, the noise, the constant threat). Doubt is secondary (the enemy is not who they said, the confessions are growing longer).

## 19-20. Knowledge Deltas & Character Architecture

### Martín
- **Motivation:** To hold the line. To survive. To be a good soldier.
- **Blind spot:** He reads the International Brigades as individuals, not as a sign that the war's rhetoric is failing.
- **Contradiction:** He fights men who are not what the propaganda said they were.

### Tomás
- **Motivation:** To survive. To eat. To sleep. To be beside Martín.
- **Blind spot:** He does not see the ideological implications of fighting foreigners.

### Padre Joaquín
- **Motivation:** To hear confessions. To bless. To do his duty.
- **Blind spot:** He does not yet see that the confessions are growing longer because the men are losing certainty.
- **Interiority access:** Through Martín's observation — the voice, the pauses, the longer confessions.
