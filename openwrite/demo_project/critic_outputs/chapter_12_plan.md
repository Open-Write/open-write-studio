## 3. Outline Summary
July–November 1938. The Battle of the Ebro. The war's longest and bloodiest battle. The requeté are in the line for weeks — the blasted rock of the Ebro valley, the hills that look like the surface of the moon after bombardment. The fighting is attrition — point and counterpoint, hill and valley, attack and counterattack. Martín and Tomás share a position on a ridge. The air war is constant — German and Italian aircraft, Republican fighters, the sound of engines and bombs that never stops. Tomás is killed by a mortar round. Not heroic. Not meaningful. A round that lands in the wrong place at the wrong time. Martín holds him. Tomás says: "Tell my mother." He does not finish the sentence. Martín wraps him in a blanket and marks the position. The requeté hold the ridge. They are pulled from the line in October. Of the hundred and twenty men who entered the line in July, sixty-three walk out.

## 4. Emotional Palette
**Grief** and **Desolation.** The reader should feel the death — not as drama but as waste. Tomás is not a symbol. He is a man who wanted to go home.
