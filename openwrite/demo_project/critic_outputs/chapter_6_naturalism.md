## BLOCKING — Em-Dash Overuse: 58

One em-dash every 4.8 lines. The chapter averages more than one per paragraph. Many are structural — the narrator's voice leans on em-dashes as a cataloging device ("the ears full of the artillery — the ears full of the sniper cracks"). This is the single most pervasive stylistic tic.

**Worst offenders (lines with 2+ em-dashes):**
- L5: `the ears full of the artillery — the ears full of the sniper cracks... — Before the names, before the count`
- L37: `It was everywhere — under the fingernails... — a cold that settled in... — finding the shape, filling it`
- L109: `The thought stayed in my ears — the sound of foreign voices in the dark — where thoughts go`
- L123: `a man three positions down — a volunteer from Estella, young, maybe nineteen — stood to adjust`
- L199: `Dead accumulated slowly — one here, one there — the way a field accumulates`
- L229: `Blessing was the same — same words, same God, same sacrament`
- L249: `Pin caught the candle — a small flash, quick, gone`
- L277: `Sticks marked them — pale wood, names carved with a knife`

**Fix:** Replace ~30 em-dashes with periods, commas, or sentence restructures. The cataloging device works in the opening frame (L3, L273) and in the combat sequence (L215-221). Elsewhere, the dashes are padding. Target: ~25 total.

All five are simile constructions. The pattern is the same: concrete noun + "the way" + concrete comparison. It works the first time. By the fifth, it's a template.

**Fix:** Keep L37 (strongest image) and L199 (chapter-closing resonance). Cut or rephrase L15, L55, L57.
