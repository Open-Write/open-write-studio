## 1. Locked Voice Compliance (V1-A: The Immediate Reckoning)

### POV and Tense

The locked spec requires first-person retrospective with present-tense writing frame and past-tense remembered events. The chapter executes this correctly throughout:

- **Present-tense frame** appears in three locations: the opening (lines 5-7, "I can hear it now. Here, at this table"), the mid-chapter return (lines 173-177, "I am at the table. The inkwell is full"), and the closing (lines 179-183, "The pen moves. The boy moves with it.").
- **Past-tense narration** carries all remembered events — the rain campaign, the losses, the letter, the oak tree.
- **No POV breaks.** The chapter stays inside Martín's perception. Other characters' interiorities are rendered through observed behavior, not omniscient access.

### The Immediacy Collapse

The core technique — the body bridging thirty years between remembering and remembered self — is deployed with variation:

- **Sound bridge (opening):** "the pen moves and the ear remembers what the hand cannot hold — the sound of rain on stone" (line 6). Ear-as-anchor, not the repeated hand/parapet pattern from the voice tournament.
- **Touch bridge (mid-chapter):** "The paper is thin. The wood is hard. I felt them both against my chest" (line 75) — sensory overlap between the remembered pocket and the present reading.
- **Visual bridge (closing):** "The pen moves. The boy moves with it. He stands in the plaza." (line 181). The pen becomes the vehicle that re-enters the scene.

Each bridge uses a different sensory modality (sound, touch, sight). The spec warns: "Never the same bridge twice in a chapter." This rule is honored.

### The Grounding Location

The hardware store table appears twice — opening and mid-chapter — with consistent physical detail: the inkwell, the crack in the table, the hardware store's darkness. The spec's requirement for physical anchors of the act of writing is met. The detail "the same crack that was there when I was a boy" (line 177) earns the grounding location by connecting the table to generational memory.

### The Writing-as-Reckoning Frame

The closing section (lines 179-183) delivers the reckoning frame without self-pity or editorializing:

> "The boy stands in the plaza and looks at the place where the tree was and he does not speak and the silence is the answer and the answer is not enough."

This fulfills the spec's directive: "The narrator does not tell the reader what to feel. He renders what happened." The rendering is the reckoning. The silence is the conclusion. No editorializing.

### Risk Check: Self-Pity, Indulgence, Editorializing, Monotony

- **Self-pity:** Not present. The narrator observes the boy's suffering without commenting on its injustice.
- **Indulgence:** The immediacy technique serves the scenes — the rain, the letter, the stump — rather than displaying itself.
- **Editorializing:** Absent. "The boy did not know" (line 161) is factual narration from the older self, not moral instruction.
- **Monotony of technique:** The three bridges use different senses. The present-tense returns are spaced (opening, mid-chapter, closing) with no clustering. No repetition of identical bridge phrasing.

## 2. Character Voice Consistency

### Martín (Remembered Self)

The remembered Martín is in Act III — the Soldier register beginning to fracture. The chapter shows this correctly:

- **Register 3 (The Soldier)** dominates the narration: concrete, physical, sensory. "The leather had been good when I left Pamplona" (line 9). "The stitching at the left heel had split in June" (line 9). Short observations. No abstraction.
- **Register 2 (The Son)** surfaces in the letter scenes — deferential, slightly formal in the remembered exchanges with his father's words.
- **Register 4 (The Fracture)** begins to show: "I did not write back" repeated three times (lines 95, 97-99). The fragments near the end — "The beret. The crucifix. The king who did not come" style — appear as "The stump. The roots. The sap pooling in the center" (line 167). The syntax of a man whose framework is cracking, but still holding.

**No violations.** Martín does not speak in Register 1 (The Catechism) anywhere in this chapter — correct, as the crusade certainty has eroded by Act III.

### Tomás

Tomás's voice is consistent with his profile:

- **Register 1 (The Companion)** in the early scenes: "The feet will dry when the rain stops" (line 11). Practical. Deflecting discomfort with pragmatism.
- **Register 2 (The Brother)** in the combat sections: "Watch the ridge" / "The path turns ahead. There's a draw on the left" (lines 111). Flat, direct, stripped. Fear strips the dialect — correct per profile.
- **Register 1 (The Companion)** returns in the letter scene: "From your father" / "He's well?" / "Mothers and fathers. They worry" (lines 79-91). Simple. Not a man who says more than the thing requires.

**One note:** Tomás does not use dialect markers ("tío," dropped consonants) in any register here. The profile specifies "Navarrese dialect — Basque-inflected Spanish with dropped consonants and local idioms" for Register 1. His dialogue in this chapter is clean, unaccented Spanish. This is a minor gap — the combat sections (Register 2) correctly strip the dialect, but the camp/march scenes (Register 1) could use a dialect marker or two to maintain distinctiveness from Martín's speech. This does not rise to a failure — the chapter is predominantly interior narration with sparse dialogue, and Tomás's brevity itself serves as a voice marker — but subsequent chapters should reintroduce dialect flavor in non-combat scenes.

**No violations.** Tomás does not use blasphemy (correct — he uses soldier's profanity, not Carlist blasphemy, and none appears here). He does not talk about the cause (correct — he talks about food, sleep, home).

### Padre Joaquín

Padre Joaquín's voice is rendered primarily through observed behavior, consistent with the POV contract (Martín cannot access his interiority):

- **Register 1 (The Pastor)** appears in the Mass reference: "I said Mass this morning. For the men. In the rain. Under a tarpaulin" (line 47). Measured, pastoral.
- **Register 2 (The Confessor)** in the church scene: "The town is empty" / "They left before we arrived" (lines 37-43). Quiet, close. The questions that are not really questions.
- **The erosion of Register 1** is shown through behavior: "The homilies had been getting shorter since June" (line 53). "He stood before the men and he said: 'God is with you. Go with God.' And he stepped back" (lines 53-54). The pastoral fluency eroding — correct per profile.

**No violations.** Padre Joaquín's doubt is not made explicit in dialogue (correct — it leaks through behavior, not confession). The narrator renders doubt through observation: the shorter homilies, the hand on the stump, the lips moving without sound. The POV contract is honored.

### Don Eusebio (via Letter)

Don Eusebio's voice in the letter is consistent with his profile:

- **Register 1 (The Patriarch)** in the first two paragraphs: measured, declarative. The store. The town.
- **Register 2 (The Father)** in the third paragraph: "Your grandfather fought for the fueros. Your father wore the beret for the fueros. You are fighting for the men who will abolish them" (line 73). This is the Father register — softer, indirect, carrying the weight of what is not said. The subjunctive is absent here, but the indirection is present: Don Eusebio does not say "I am disappointed" or "you are wrong." He states three generations of fact and lets the gap between them speak.

**No violations.** The second letter's brevity — "two sentences... no third paragraph" (line 97) — correctly deploys Register 3 (The Silence) as an absence. "The absence of the third paragraph was louder than the paragraph itself" (line 97) is narrator observation, not omniscient access to Don Eusebio's mind.

The chapter's rhythm matches the spec's directive: "Longer sentences that carry the freight of reflection, broken by short declaratives when memory sharpens into image."

- **Long freight sentences:** "He walked with his head down, his limp more pronounced, his prayer book in the inner pocket of his cassock where the leather cover darkened and softened with each day of rain" (line 17).

The spec notes: "Fragments when the emotion is too much for complete sentences: 'The beret. The crucifix. The king who did not come.'" This chapter delivers that pattern in the closing section.

## 8. Advisory Notes (Non-Blocking)

1. **Tomás dialect:** In non-combat scenes (camp, march, conversation), Tomás's dialogue could benefit from occasional dialect markers — "tío," dropped consonants — to maintain voice distinctiveness from Martín. The profile specifies "Navarrese dialect" for Register 1. This is a refinement, not a failure.

2. **Polysyndeton density:** Two extended polysyndeton chains (lines 99 and 165) serve the chapter's rhythmic intent but should be monitored across the manuscript to avoid becoming a pattern. The spec flags polysyndeton chains as default rhythm — these are not default, but subsequent chapters should vary the technique.

3. **"The boy did not know" frame shift:** Lines 161-163 shift to a "the boy" third-person reference for the remembered self. This is effective — it creates distance between the narrator and his younger self — but it appears only in this passage. If used in other chapters, it should be consistent. If unique to this chapter, it works as a one-time technique for the letter's impact.
