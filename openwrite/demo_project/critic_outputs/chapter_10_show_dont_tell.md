# Show-Don't-Tell Critic — Chapter 10: The Cold

**Reader:** Show-Don't-Tell Critic

**Manuscript:** `manuscript/chapters/010_the_cold.md` (169 lines)
**Format Rules:** `bible/07_format_rules.md`
**Special focus:** Chapter endings (final 15%, lines 144–169), emotional state names, telling-not-rendering, authorial intrusion, omniscience leaks

---

## Method

Line-by-line scan against format rules §V (interiority must do work), §IX (POV contract — no omniscient), and §IV (AI tics — "There was," named emotions, meta-address). Special attention to the final 15% of the chapter (lines 144–169) where the narrator returns to the present-tense framing device.

**Note on framing:** This chapter uses a first-person retrospective frame (older Martín writing at a table). The frame permits some interpretive narration — a memoirist may reflect on what they experienced. The violations below are passages where even within that frame, the prose names what it should render, or crosses from Martín's perception into knowledge he cannot have.

## Findings

### 1. Line 7 — Authorial Intrusion (Meta-Explanation)

> "The body remembers what the mind would rather file away."

**Type:** Authorial intrusion — the narrator steps back to explain the mechanism of memory rather than rendering it. The sensory trigger (cold table → trench memory) is already doing this work in the surrounding lines. This sentence tells the reader what the scene has already shown.

**Fix:** Cut the sentence. The transition from the table to the trench (lines 5–6) already renders the associative leap through physical detail: cold forearms → cold hands → shaking → trench. The reader arrives there without being told how memory works.

---

### 2. Line 13 — Authorial Intrusion (Editorializing)

> "The cold was the real enemy — the only one that cared nothing for the color of your uniform."

**Type:** Authorial intrusion — the narrator steps back to make a thematic observation. This is the kind of interpretive generalization that belongs to an essay, not a scene. The prose has already rendered the cold as universal through physical detail (mules struggling, men stumbling, canteens freezing). The editorial line adds nothing the reader doesn't already feel.

**Fix:** Cut the sentence after "The cold was the real enemy." Or cut entirely — the preceding physical catalogue of the cold's progression (breath visible → water frozen → mud hardened) already proves the point without stating it.

---

### 3. Line 19 — Telling-Not-Rendering (Clinical Summary of Experience)

> "Then the mind — the thoughts slowing, the will to move fading, the body's argument that lying still was easier than standing up, that sleeping was easier than staying awake. That argument was the killer."

**Type:** Telling-not-rendering. The passage shifts from physical rendering (fingers, toes, hands, feet, arms, legs, chest, lungs — all excellent) into psychological interpretation. "The body's argument" and "That argument was the killer" are the narrator explaining why men died rather than showing the moment of surrender. Compare to the rendered version later in the chapter: Ibarra "sat down, pulled his blanket around his shoulders, and closed his eyes" (line 39). That is the argument made flesh. This passage pre-explains what the Ibarra scene will render.

**Fix:** End the catalogue at "the lungs catching on air that was so cold it felt solid going down." The Ibarra scene (lines 37–39) already demonstrates the lethal logic without naming it. If the narrator wants to keep the progression to the mind, render it as physical symptom: thoughts becoming indistinct, the eyelids heavy, the body settling — not as abstract argument.

---

### 4. Line 49 — Omniscience Leak

> "I think he was afraid that if he sat down, he would not get up. I think the standing was the only thing keeping him vertical."

**Type:** Omniscience leak (borderline). The "I think" hedge softens this into speculation, which the POV contract permits — Martín can guess. But the second sentence ("the standing was the only thing keeping him vertical") moves from speculation into diagnosis. It names the precise mechanism of Padre Joaquín's endurance with a certainty that exceeds what Martín can observe. The physical rendering — the limp, the deliberate steps, the cassock under the blanket, the rote of blessing from body to body — already conveys this without the narrator's clinical interpretation.

**Fix:** Keep the first sentence (speculation is permitted). Cut the second sentence and everything after it through line 51 ("He did not sit."). The physical sequence of Joaquín moving trench to trench (lines 43–47) already renders the man who cannot stop. "He did not sit" (line 51) works as a closing beat only if the interpretive scaffold is removed — without the explanation, the plain observation carries weight.

### 5. Line 113 — Telling-Not-Rendering (Explicit Statement of Fact)

> "Tomás kept me alive. I will say it plainly because there is no other way to say it. He kept me alive."

**Type:** Telling-not-rendering. The narrator declares the fact and then meta-addresses his own declaration. Everything that follows in lines 113–115 (shared blanket, digging, waking, bread, foot-wrapping) renders the claim with full physical specificity. The opening declaration pre-empts the rendering — it tells the reader what the next two paragraphs will show, then the paragraphs show it. The redundancy is the problem: the rendered detail is strong enough to carry the meaning without the thesis statement.

**Fix:** Cut lines "Tomás kept me alive. I will say it plainly because there is no other way to say it. He kept me alive." Begin the section with the physical actions: "He shared his blanket. He dug the trench deeper when my hands were too frozen to grip a shovel." The reader arrives at "kept me alive" through the catalogue of acts. The narrator does not need to say it first.

---

### 6. Line 119 — Authorial Intrusion (Declarative Abstraction)

> "The crusade was far away. The king was far away. God was far away."

**Type:** Authorial intrusion — the narrator steps back to name the ideological frame that has dissolved. This is telling-not-rendering of the highest order: the chapter has spent 118 lines rendering the dissolution of ideology through frozen bodies, empty sermons, and the raw physics of survival. The triadic declaration ("far away × 3") compresses what the scene has already earned into a slogan. The format rules flag triadic parallel as a default rhythm (§IV, Tier 1): "Three-element lists as background music — permitted only when the moment specifically calls for three beats."

**Fix:** Cut the three sentences. The scene is already doing this work: two men pressed together under blankets, the only warmth the body beside them, the wind screaming overhead. The absence of crusade, king, and God is rendered by the absence of any mention of them in the scene's action. The reader does not need the narrator to name what is not there.

---

### 7. Line 145 — Named Emotion (Emotional State Name)

> "Emptiness. A hollow space where pride or relief or sorrow might have lived."

**Type:** Named emotion. "Emptiness" is a direct emotional state label — the exact violation described in format rules §V: "Named emotion: *Martín felt grief.* Bad. Cut or replace." The follow-up sentence ("A hollow space where pride or relief or sorrow might have lived") compounds the problem by naming three emotions that are absent, which is telling the reader what the narrator does not feel rather than rendering the absence through behavior or physical detail.

**Fix:** Render the hollowness through the body. The narrator is standing in the street looking at a flag after two months of freezing warfare. What does the body do when it feels nothing? The hands hang. The eyes focus on nothing. The breath comes and goes and the chest does not care. Replace the named emotion with a physical beat: "I stood in the street and looked at the flag. My hands were at my sides. The men around me were cheering. My mouth was closed."

### 8. Line 155 — Telling-Not-Rendering (Narrator States What Reader Already Knows)

> "He had given them to me. He had kept me alive with his body and his blankets and his bread and his voice in the dark, talking about Aoiz, about the sheep, about the dog with two different eyes, about the river in spring."

**Type:** Telling-not-rendering. This restates what lines 113–115 already stated and what the intervening scene has already rendered. The reader has watched Tomás share blankets, dig trenches, press bread into frozen hands, and talk through the night. The narrator's summary adds no new information — it is a thesis statement appended to evidence that has already been presented.

**Fix:** Cut the sentence. The preceding catalogue of Tomás's deterioration (blanket torn to strips, fingers cracked to bone, thinner than the rest) already renders the cost of his generosity. The reader understands what was given without being told.

---

### 9. Line 157 — Telling-Not-Rendering (Naming the Unnamed)

> "The thing between us — whatever it was, whatever name it had — was a thing that required no looking at."

**Type:** Telling-not-rendering. The narrator names the relationship by declaring it unnameable — a paradox that still counts as naming. The physical details that follow ("It was in the blanket, the bread, the shoulder pressed against shoulder in the dark") render the bond through action. The opening clause pre-explains what the details demonstrate. The "whatever name it had" construction is also a variant of the banned "Something between X and Y" pattern (§IV, Tier 1): it avoids specificity by gesturing at the space between things rather than occupying it.

**Fix:** Cut the opening clause. Begin with the rendered details: "It was in the blanket, the bread, the shoulder pressed against shoulder in the dark." The reader does not need the narrator to flag that there is a thing between them. The blanket, the bread, and the shoulder say it.

---

### 10. Lines 159–160 — Omniscience Leak (Prolepsis / Flash-Forward)

> "I did not know what I would do with that debt. That in four months, on the banks of the Ebro, I would hold Tomás in my arms and his blood would be on my hands and his last word would be an unfinished sentence to a mother in Aoiz. That the debt would come due and I would fail to pay it and it would follow me for thirty years to this table, to this inkwell, to this pen moving across the page."

**Type:** Omniscience leak (prolepsis). The narrator-as-writer knows the future of the story he is telling. This is a structural choice (flash-forward) rather than a POV violation — the framing device permits the older Martín to know what happened next. However, the passage names Tomás's death in explicit terms ("his blood would be on my hands," "his last word would be an unfinished sentence to a mother in Aoiz") and then names the emotional consequence ("the debt would come due and I would fail to pay it"). This is telling the reader what will happen and what it will mean, rather than letting the story arrive there on its own.

**Note:** This is a structural/craft concern more than a pure show-don't-tell violation. The framing device technically permits the narrator to know the future. But the passage renders Tomás's death as summary — "his blood would be on my hands" — rather than leaving it for the scene where it happens. The reader is told the emotional payload of a future chapter in a single sentence.

**Fix:** Soften the prolepsis. The narrator can gesture at the debt without rendering the death scene in summary: "I did not know what I would do with that debt. I did not know that it would follow me for thirty years to this table, to this inkwell, to this pen moving across the page." Cut the middle sentence about the Ebro entirely — let the death scene earn its own weight when it arrives.

### 11. Lines 165–167 — Telling-Not-Rendering (Interpretive Catalogue)

> "I looked at that pulse. I looked at the skin of his neck. I looked at the road ahead and the mountains behind and the sun on the frozen ground and the steam rising from the earth as the ice melted and the world softened and the cold released its grip."

**Type:** Telling-not-rendering (borderline). The catalogue of looking is rendered, but the final clause ("the world softened and the cold released its grip") shifts from Martín's perception to authorial interpretation. The world did not soften — the temperature rose. The cold did not release its grip — the ice melted. The narrator is decorating a physical observation with metaphorical language that belongs to the author, not the character.

**Fix:** End the catalogue at "the steam rising from the earth as the ice melted." The physical detail is sufficient. "The world softened and the cold released its grip" is the narrator interpreting what the steam already shows.
