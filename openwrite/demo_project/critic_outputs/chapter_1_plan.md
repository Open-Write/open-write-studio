# Chapter 1 Plan — "The Red Beret"

---

## 1. Chapter Number and Title
Chapter 1 — "The Red Beret"

## 2. Characters Present
- **Martín Javier Echeverría** (POV) — active registers: The Catechism (primary), The Son (secondary)
- **Don Eusebio Echeverría** — active registers: The Patriarch (public), The Father (private with Martín)
- **Tomás Eguía** — active register: The Companion
- **Padre Joaquín** — active register: The Pastor

## 3. POV Character
Martín Javier Echeverría. First-person retrospective — the narrator is Martín ~30 years after the war, writing at his father's table in the hardware store in Pamplona. The remembered Martín is 22, certain, devout.

## 4. Narrative Distance
First-person retrospective with immediacy technique. The narrator oscillates between:
- The remembering self (at the table, inkwell, pen, the crack in the wood)
- The remembered self (July 18, 1936 — the hardware store, the plaza, the road south)
The gap between the two is where the tragedy lives. The immediacy collapse bridges the gap through sensory memory — smell, touch, the weight of objects.

## 5. Consciousness Markers
The narrator/remembered Martín notices:
- **Objects first.** The crucifix, the beret, the hardware store inventory, the stone of the streets. His vocabulary is concrete — hardware-store nouns (iron, wood, wool, stone, brass).
- **Hands.** His father's hands, his own hands, Tomás's hands. Hands carry, hold, adjust, release.
- **The physical weight of tradition.** The beret on the skull. The crucifix in the hand. The wool. The wood. These are not symbols to him — they are objects with mass and texture.
- **Religious cadence.** Prayer surfaces naturally — invocations, feast days, the rhythm of the breviary. Not displayed, but breathed.
- **What he does NOT notice:** political subtext, Franco's machinery, the factional dynamics within the Nationalist coalition. He is inside the crusade; he cannot see its frame.

## 6. POV Constraints
- The narrator knows how the story ends (king did not come, fueros abolished, Church blessed Franco). This knowledge surfaces in the immediacy technique — "the boy does not know that the rightness is the thing that will break."
- The remembered Martín knows only what a 22-year-old Carlist from Pamplona knows: the faith, the fueros, the tradition, the hardware store, the streets of his city.
- Other characters' interiorities are visible only through behavior, voice, and what Martín can read in their faces.
- The narrator does NOT editorialize. He renders. The reader must do the interpretive work.

## 7. Outline Summary
July 18, 1936. Pamplona. The mobilization order comes. Martín is in the hardware store with his father, Don Eusebio. The Carlist Communion's runners bring the word: Mola has risen. The Requeté assembles at the cuartel. Don Eusebio gives Martín the wooden crucifix — the one from his grandfather who fought in the Third Carlist War. "Carry this. It has been in three wars." Martín puts on the red beret. The chapter is set in the hours before departure — the streets of Pamplona filling with men in red berets, the church bells, the Plaza del Castillo. Padre Joaquín blesses the assembled requeté. Don Eusebio watches from the edge of the plaza. He does not embrace Martín. He adjusts the beret on his son's head and steps back. Tomás Eguía appears — a stocky man from Aoiz, already carrying more than his share. "I'm Tomás. I'll be beside you." They march south.

## 8. State Changes
- **Before:** Martín is a civilian hardware-store worker, Carlist by heritage but not yet a soldier.
- **After:** Martín is a mobilized requeté, marching south, carrying his grandfather's crucifix and wearing the red beret.
- **Don Eusebio:** From patriarch-in-repose to father who has sent his last living son to war.
- **Tomás:** From stranger to companion.
- **Padre Joaquín:** From parish priest to military chaplain.

## 9. Callbacks Landing
None — this is the first chapter. Seeds only.

## 10. New Callbacks Seeded
1. **The wooden crucifix** — Don Eusebio to Martín: "Carry this. It has been in three wars." (Payoff: Ch6, Ch9, Ch13, Ch15)
2. **The red beret** — Martín puts it on for the first time as a mobilized requeté. (Payoff: Ch5, Ch9, Ch14, Ch15)
3. **Don Eusebio's non-embrace** — He adjusts the beret, steps back. No embrace. (Payoff: Ch11, Ch15)
4. **Padre Joaquín's blessing** — Pastoral certainty, full conviction. (Payoff: Ch6, Ch9, Ch13)
5. **Tomás's arrival** — "I'm Tomás. I'll be beside you." (Payoff: Ch12, Ch15)
6. **Tomás's physicality** — stocky, carrying more than his share, hands. Establish the body that will die at the Ebro. (Payoff: Ch12)
7. **The hardware store** — The narrator's grounding location. The table, the inkwell, the crack. (Payoff: recurring framing device)

## 11. Reader-Belief Phase
**Misdirection active.** The reader believes this is a war story about a young Carlist's crusade. The Carlist cause is specific and beautiful. No cracks have appeared. The reader should be swept up in the conviction — the church bells, the red berets, the father's blessing, the camaraderie. The reader should feel the weight of the tradition being carried forward.

## 12. Reader-State Guidance
- **Must convey:** The beauty and specificity of the Carlist cause. These men believe in something real — God, king, the fueros. The tradition is a century old. The red beret is not a costume.
- **Must NOT convey:** Any irony, authorial distance, or suggestion that the crusade will fail. The narrator knows, but the immediacy technique keeps the reader inside the remembered Martín's conviction.
- **Can plant (subtly):** The faintest edge of cost — Don Eusebio's non-embrace, the narrator's present-tense bridge ("the boy does not know"). These are seeds, not signals.

## 13. Emotional Palette
**Conviction** and **Farewell.** The reader should feel the weight of the tradition being carried forward — and the first, faint edge of what it will cost. The conviction is primary; the farewell is secondary, felt in the father's hands, the church bells, the streets that are being left behind.

## 14. Chapter Objectives
1. Establish Martín's faith as specific, beautiful, and deeply held — not naive, but certain.
2. Establish the father-son chain through objects (crucifix, beret) and gesture (the non-embrace).
3. Introduce Tomás as the companion — physical, practical, loyal.
4. Set the narrator's voice: the Immediate Reckoning — looking back from the hardware store at the boy who believed.
5. Seed the callback objects that will carry the novel's thematic weight.

## 15. Key Beats
1. **The smell** (opening) — The hardware store. The inkwell. The narrator establishes the grounding location and the act of writing. The smell bridges to the memory of July 18.
2. **The runners arrive** — The mobilization order. Mola has risen. The hardware store door opens. The world changes.
3. **The crucifix** — Don Eusebio takes it from above the dining table, holds it, gives it to Martín. "Carry this. It has been in three wars." The weight of the wood in the hand.
4. **The beret** — Martín puts it on. The wool on the skull. The moment the civilian becomes the soldier.
5. **The plaza** — The requeté assembling. Church bells. Red berets filling the square. Padre Joaquín's blessing. The scale of the mobilization — not an abstraction, but men, hundreds of them, in the same wool.
6. **The non-embrace** — Don Eusebio at the edge of the plaza. He does not embrace Martín. He adjusts the beret on his son's head and steps back. The restraint is the love.
7. **Tomás** — "I'm Tomás. I'll be beside you." The stocky man from Aoiz. The hands. The ease. The companion.
8. **The march south** — Leaving Pamplona. The streets, the last look, the road. The narrator's bridge: "the boy is marching. He does not know."

## 16. Timeline Entry
- **Date:** July 18, 1936 (Saturday)
- **Events:** Mobilization order arrives. Martín receives crucifix and beret. Requeté assembles at cuartel. Padre Joaquín blesses the assembled men. Departure from Pamplona.
- **Historical context:** Day of the military uprising. Mola has risen in Pamplona. The Carlist Communion mobilizes the Requeté.
- **Time of day:** Morning (runners arrive) → midday (plaza assembly) → afternoon (departure).

## 17. Word Count Target
5,000–6,000 words. This is the novel's opening chapter — it must earn its length through scene density, not summary. The hardware store, the crucifix handoff, the plaza, the blessing, the non-embrace, Tomás's introduction — all rendered as scenes, not summarized.

## 18. Convention Ledger Notes
- **New conventions seeded:**
  - The smell-bridge (narrator's present sensory experience triggers the memory)
  - The object-as-anchor (crucifix, beret, inkwell — objects carry thematic weight through physical specificity)
  - The non-embrace as emotional register (Don Eusebio's restraint)
  - The narrator's "the boy does not know" technique (dramatic irony through the immediacy collapse)
- **Body anchors used:** hands (primary — Don Eusebio's, Martín's, Tomás's), smell (hardware store → road), wool (beret on skull), wood (crucifix in hand)

## 19. Knowledge Deltas

### Martín
- **Knew before:** Carlist faith, fueros, hardware store work, Pamplona streets, the red beret tradition, the crucifix's history.
- **Learns:** Mola has risen. The mobilization is real. The war is here.
- **Still doesn't know:** Franco's consolidation plans. The unification decree. The fate of the fueros. That the king will not come.

### Don Eusebio
- **Knew before:** Everything Martín knows, plus: the Carlist Communion's negotiations with Mola, the political dynamics, the weight of three wars.
- **Learns:** The mobilization order (though he may have anticipated it).
- **Still doesn't know:** That Franco will absorb the movement.

### Tomás
- **Knew before:** His family's tradition of fighting. Practical soldiering (limited).
- **Learns:** The mobilization order.
- **Still doesn't know:** Anything beyond "we fight, we win, we go home."

### Padre Joaquín
- **Knew before:** Carlist theology, parish ministry, the spiritual framework of the crusade.
- **Learns:** The mobilization order.
- **Still doesn't know:** That his blessings will become rote, that his doubt will grow.

## 20. Character Architecture Depth

### Martín (POV)
- **Motivation (scene-level):** To answer the call. To be worthy of the tradition. To carry the crucifix and the beret as his father and grandfather carried them.
- **Need (arc-level):** To discover whether faith can survive the corruption of the cause that claims to serve it.
- **Blind spot:** He cannot distinguish between the Carlist tradition and the institutional machinery that will consume it. He believes the crusade and the cause are the same thing.
- **Contradiction:** He is a man of faith marching toward a war that will destroy the faith he carries. His virtues — loyalty, piety, willingness to sacrifice — are the instruments of his subordination.
- **Interiority access:** First-person retrospective with immediacy collapse. The narrator's present-tense awareness ("I am at the table") bridges to the remembered self's sensory experience. Interiority arrives through the body (hands, breath, the weight of objects) and through the narrator's dramatic irony ("the boy does not know").

### Don Eusebio
- **Motivation:** To send his son to war with the objects that carry the tradition — the crucifix, the beret, the words.
- **Need:** To believe that this war will deliver what the previous three did not — the Carlist king.
- **Blind spot:** He cannot see that the movement he has spent his life serving will be consumed by the man it helps install.
- **Contradiction:** He is the tradition's most devoted servant and its most vulnerable casualty. His love for Martín is expressed through objects, not embraces — the restraint is both strength and wound.
- **Interiority access:** Through Martín's observation only — the face, the hands, the voice, the non-embrace. No direct access.

### Tomás
- **Motivation:** To fight because his family fights. To be beside Martín. To go home.
- **Need:** To survive the war with his humanity intact.
- **Blind spot:** He does not think about why they fight. The "why" is not his question. This is both his strength (he endures) and his vulnerability (he has no framework for what the war will become).
- **Contradiction:** He fights without ideology and dies without meaning — but he is the novel's most meaningful character.
- **Interiority access:** Through Martín's observation — the body, the voice, the humor, the hands.

### Padre Joaquín
- **Motivation:** To bless the requeté. To give the war its spiritual meaning. To be the Church's presence in the battalion.
- **Need:** To maintain the theological framework that makes the crusade just.
- **Blind spot:** He cannot yet see that blessing bullets and blessing men may not be the same sacrament.
- **Contradiction:** He is the Church's voice and will become its conscience — the man who discovers that the Church's endorsement of Franco costs more than the Church knows.
- **Interiority access:** Through Martín's observation — the voice, the blessing, the hands, the presence.

## Notes for the Prose Writer

1. **Voice:** First-person retrospective, V1-A Immediate Reckoning. See `bible/LOCKED_VOICE_SPEC.md`.
2. **The opening must establish the grounding location** — the hardware store, the table, the inkwell. This is the narrator's anchor throughout the novel.
3. **The smell-bridge** from the hardware store to the memory of July 18 is the chapter's first immediacy collapse. Make it sensory, not explanatory.
4. **Don Eusebio's non-embrace is the chapter's emotional center.** The restraint is the love. Do not explain it. Render the hands, the beret, the step back.
5. **Tomás must be immediately physical and immediately likable.** The reader must love him by the end of the chapter, because his death at the Ebro is the novel's wound.
6. **The Carlist cause must be rendered with full conviction.** No irony. No authorial distance. The reader must believe in the crusade before the crusade is hollowed out.
