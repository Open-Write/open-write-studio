# Chapter 11 Plan — "The Homecoming"

---

## 1. Chapter Number and Title
Chapter 11 — "The Homecoming"

## 2. Characters Present
- **Martín Javier Echeverría** (POV) — The Soldier, The Fracture
- **Don Eusebio** — The Patriarch (older, stooped, quieter), The Father — **IN PERSON for first time since Ch1**
- **Tomás** — mentioned, not present (Martín on leave alone)

## 3. Outline Summary
March 1938. Martín receives leave — ten days. He returns to Pamplona. The city is unchanged — the same stone streets, the same church towers, the same Plaza del Castillo. But Martín is changed. He walks through the streets and sees the red berets and the yoke-and-arrows and the flags of the unified party. He visits his father. Don Eusebio is older. Stooped. Quieter. They sit at the dining table. The crucifix is above them. Don Eusebio does not ask about the war. He says: "The fueros are being discussed. In Burgos." A pause. "They are not being discussed well." Martín sleeps in his childhood room. The red beret is on the hook in the hallway. He cannot sleep. He goes to the church. He kneels. He does not pray. He kneels. The leave ends. He returns to the front. Don Eusebio stands at the window and watches him go. He does not adjust the beret this time.

## 4. Emotional Palette
**Alienation** and **Tenderness.** The reader should feel the distance that has opened between Martín and the world he left — and the love between father and son that persists in the absence of language.

## 5. Key Beats
1. The return to Pamplona — the city unchanged, Martín changed.
2. Don Eusebio — older, stooped, quieter. The dining table. The crucifix above them.
3. "The fueros are being discussed. In Burgos." "They are not being discussed well."
4. The childhood room. The beret on the hook. He cannot sleep.
5. The church. He kneels. He does not pray.
6. The departure. Don Eusebio at the window. He does not adjust the beret.

## 6. Word Count Target
4,000–5,000 words.

## 7. Body Anchors
Eyes (the city, the father's face, the crucifix), hands (the beret on the hook, the crucifix), spine (Don Eusebio's stoop).

---

## Notes for the Prose Writer

1. **Don Eusebio is the chapter's emotional center.** The father-son reunion must be rendered through objects and silence — the dining table, the crucifix, the beret on the hook, the window.
2. **"The fueros are being discussed. In Burgos." "They are not being discussed well."** Fixed dialogue. Must land as fact.
3. **The beret on the hook** in the hallway — the same hook where his father's beret hung. The tradition.
4. **The church.** He kneels. He does not pray. The body in the pew. The silence.
5. **The departure.** Don Eusebio at the window. He does not adjust the beret this time. The restraint that was love in Ch1 is now distance.
6. **All meta-critic constraints apply.**
