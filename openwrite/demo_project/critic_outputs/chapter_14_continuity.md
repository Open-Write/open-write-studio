## I. BLOCKING FINDINGS

### B1. Yoke-and-Arrows: "Sewn" vs. "Pin" — State File Contradiction

**Location:** Line 105 vs. `state/callback_ledger.json` + `state/project_state.json`

Ch14 describes the yoke-and-arrows badge as **sewn onto the beret**:
> "The yoke-and-arrows badge was sewn on the left side, where the Carlist star had been. The stitching was rough — done in a hurry, in a field, with whatever thread the quartermaster had. I could feel the stitches under my thumb. The thread was thick. The knot was uneven."

The state files consistently describe it as a **pin**:
- `callback_ledger.json`: "The Falangist pin on the red beret"
- `project_state.json`: "yoke_and_arrows_pin: on berets"

This is not a trivial word-choice difference. The physical method of attachment changes what the "ghost star" detail means. If the badge is a pin, the darker wool underneath the star's former position is physically plausible — the pin covers a small area, the sun fades around it. If the badge is sewn on with rough stitching that "pulled at the wool underneath, puckering it," the sun-fading logic is different: sewn fabric covers more area and the stitching itself would damage/distort the wool beneath. The detailed tactile description of stitches, thread, and knots in Ch14 is incompatible with a pin.

Additionally, line 95 describes a **cap** with a yoke-and-arrows badge: "A cap with a badge — the yoke-and-arrows." This is consistent with the chapter's logic (caps replace berets), but creates a second object bearing the symbol that the state files don't track.

**Resolution:** Either the state files must be updated from "pin" to "badge/sewn" to match Ch14, or Ch14 must be revised to use "pin" language. The choice has downstream implications for Ch5 (where the unification is first imposed) and Ch15 (where the beret returns).

## II. ADVISORY FINDINGS

### A1. Retrospective Narrator Interpretive Commentary

**Location:** Lines 33, 99, 125

Three passages name the narrator's understanding rather than rendering it through action or image:

- Line 33: "I understood for the first time that those were the same thing and that the understanding changed nothing."
- Line 99: "I understood — not in words, not in thought, but in the body, in the chest, in the hands — that the crusade was over and the thing that replaced it was not what we had fought for and that this was the end."
- Line 125: "The same hands. Separated by thirty-two years and a war and a peace and the thing that came after the peace, which was not peace at all but the slow settling of dust after a building falls."

These align with meta-critic pattern P1 (retrospective narrator interpretive commentary). Line 99 is the most problematic — it is a thesis sentence that tells the reader exactly what the chapter's images have already shown. The berets going into packs, the caps staying on heads, the red wool disappearing — these images already communicate "the crusade was over." The explicit naming weakens the rendering.

Line 33 ("those were the same thing") is vague enough to function as interiority rather than thesis. Line 125 is frame-narrator reflection, consistent with the frame convention, but the "slow settling of dust after a building falls" verges on literary cliché (format rule VII).

## IV. NOTES (Non-Blocking)

### N1. "The Same" Anaphora

Ch14 uses "the same" 7 times (lines 7, 15, 27, 37, 41, 71, 107). Below the meta-critic's maximum of 12 per chapter. Several are structural callbacks ("the same stars over Pamplona," "the same crucifix"), not reflexive filler. Acceptable density.

### N2. Negative Construction Density

Ch14 has significant negative constructions: "I did not cheer," "I did not weep," "Not praying," "I did not pack," "Not an order," etc. These serve the chapter's emotional palette (emptiness, absence), but the density warrants monitoring against the meta-critic threshold of 15/1k words.

### N3. Polysyndeton / Triplet Density

Ch14 has moderate triplet usage (e.g., "smooth from three wars — my grandfather's, my father's, mine"; "God, king, fueros"). Below the meta-critic threshold of 10 per chapter for non-scenic passages. The incantatory register is reserved for the crucifix and beret passages, which is appropriate.

### N4. Frame-Narrator Chapter-Ending

The final lines (129–133) end with "Nothing else. / Nothing else." — a refrain ending. The meta-critic notes that chapter endings are "high-risk zones" (2 of 3 Ch7-9 chapters have worst writing in endings). This ending is restrained and earns its repetition through the prior images. No thesis sentence. No emotional naming. Clean.
