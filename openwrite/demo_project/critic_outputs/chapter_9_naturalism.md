## Findings

### 1. Triadic parallelism as default rhythm (FAIL — Tier 1 ban)

The format rules state: *"Triadic parallel as default rhythm. Three-element lists as background music — permitted only when the moment specifically calls for three beats."*

This chapter uses triplet lists as its **primary rhythmic device**. There are 41 lines containing 3+ parallel items. The pattern is so pervasive it constitutes the chapter's background music. Examples:

- L7: "on stone, on canvas, on the metal of a helmet"
- L13: "the creak of leather, the clank of a canteen against a rifle stock, and the rain"
- L25: "from the wet and the cold and the exhaustion"
- L31: "the smell of damp stone and candle wax and the residue of incense"
- L99: "I walked. I carried the rifle. I listened to the rain. I slept in barns and in ditches and under trees"
- L107: "they knew the terrain, they knew the passes, they knew where the tracks led"
- L121: "green and gold and gray"
- L127: "red clay and stones and the dark of the underworld"
- L143: "kneeling, his hand on the stump, his head bowed, his lips moving"
- L159: "the Third Carlist War, the mobilizations of the Restoration, and this one"
- L161: "his body and his bullets and his dead"
- L183: "He does not speak. The stump does not speak. The roots do not speak."

The L183 triplet ("He does not speak. The stump does not speak. The roots do not speak.") is a **triplet closing AND a polysyndeton chain AND a negative density cluster** — three violations in one sentence. The same pattern repeats at L113 ("I watched the ridge. I watched the draw. I watched the path.") and L135 ("He looked at the stump. He looked at the roots. He looked at the trunk.").

**Recommendation:** Break at least 60% of the triplet patterns. Vary list lengths (2 items, 4 items, 5 items). Let specific moments earn the triplet — the oak tree scene, the letter scene, the final barn passage. Do not use it for rain descriptions, boot descriptions, or terrain descriptions.

### 2. Polysyndeton chains as default rhythm (FAIL — Tier 2 flag)

32 of 183 lines (17.5%) contain 3 or more "and" conjunctions. The format rules flag "Polysyndeton chains used as default rhythm." This chapter uses polysyndeton as its secondary rhythmic device, often in combination with triplets.

Worst offenders:

The combination of polysyndeton + triplets creates a **monotone incantatory rhythm** that reads as AI-default rather than authorial choice. The incantatory quality works for specific moments (the oak stump, the final passage) but not for boot descriptions or rain descriptions.

**Recommendation:** Reserve polysyndeton for 3-4 key passages where the incantatory rhythm serves the emotional register (the stump scene, the final barn scene, the letter reading). Break the rest into varied sentence structures — short declaratives, fragments, asyndeton.

### 3. Negative density (FAIL — at threshold)

78 negative constructions in 5,189 words = **15.0 per 1k words**. The threshold is >15 per 1k words. This is right at the boundary.

The negatives cluster in specific passages:
- L161-163: "The boy did not know... did not know... did not write back... did not have the words... did not know"
- L95-99: "I did not write back" repeated 3 times with "I did not look at them" and "did not change them"
- L143: "I did not hear what he said. I do not know if he said anything. I do not know if the movement..."

The density is driven by the narrator's voice — Martín renders doubt and loss through negation. This is character-appropriate but mechanically it creates a loop. The L161-163 passage alone contains 8 negatives in 150 words (53 per 1k — triple the threshold).

**Recommendation:** The "The boy did not know" refrain (3 instances in L161-163) should be reduced to 1-2 instances. The "I did not write back" refrain (L95, 97, 99) works but the surrounding negations in L99 ("did not write back... did not take it out... did not look at it") should be restructured — convert some to positive assertions. Target: <12 per 1k words.

### 4. Em-dash elaboration (FLAG — at threshold)

### 5. "the way" repetition (FLAG)
