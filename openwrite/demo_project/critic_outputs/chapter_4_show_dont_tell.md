# Show-Don't-Tell Critic — Chapter 4: "The Siege"

**Critic type:** Show-don't-tell enforcement
**Chapter:** 004_the_siege.md (177 lines)
**Format rules reference:** bible/07_format_rules.md
**Verdict:** CONDITIONAL PASS
**Findings count:** 14 (3 Tier-1, 8 Tier-2, 3 Tier-3)

---

## Summary

The chapter's first two-thirds demonstrate strong rendered prose — physical detail, body-anchored perception, specific sensory work. The assault sequence (lines 69-98), the Army of Africa passage (lines 47-65), and the dead-blessing scene (lines 101-147) are largely earned through scene. The problem is concentrated in the final 30% and in several key interiority moments where the narrator explains what the rendered scene already communicates. The chapter ends with a long retrospective coda that tips into over-explanation.

---

## Findings

### F1 — Named Emotion / Telling Interiority

**Location:** Line 65
**Type:** Named emotional state (Tier 1 violation)
**Text:**
> "I saw the same things and I felt something else."

**Problem:** "I felt something else" is a named-emotion construction. The format rules (§V) ban generic interiority: "Martín felt sad is worse than nothing." The narrator tells the reader an emotion exists without rendering it. The subsequent sentences (Regulares like water, Legion like fire) do the rendering work — but the lead-in sentence undercuts them by pre-announcing that a feeling is coming.

**Fix:** Delete "and I felt something else." Begin directly: "The Regulares moved like water — fluid, silent, the eyes always moving." The contrast with Tomás's pragmatism is already established by the preceding paragraph. The reader will register the narrator's different perception without being told to look for it.

### F2 — Telling / Over-Explained Subtext

**Location:** Lines 63-64
**Type:** Pure summary / telling-not-rendering (Tier 2)
**Text:**
> "It was pragmatism. The pragmatism of a man who has walked from Pamplona to Madrid and has seen what a rifle does to a body and has decided that the men who are better at rifles are men he wants near him. Tomás did not see the Army of Africa as a sign. He saw them as tools. Good tools. The kind of tools a man who has worked with his hands respects — not for what they mean but for what they do."

**Problem:** The narrator names the quality ("It was pragmatism"), then explains it twice — once in general terms ("a man who has walked from Pamplona to Madrid"), then in interpretive terms ("He saw them as tools"). Tomás's dialogue ("At least they're on our side") already communicates pragmatic assessment. The paragraph of explanation that follows is authorial commentary on a line that works on its own.

**Fix:** Cut everything after Tomás's dialogue except perhaps one concrete detail: "He watched them the way a man watches good weather approach — without reverence, only readiness." Or cut entirely and let the dialogue stand against the narrator's contrasting perception in the next paragraph.

---

### F3 — Authorial Intrusion / Meta-Commentary

**Location:** Line 65 (final sentence)
**Type:** Authorial intrusion (Tier 1)
**Text:**
> "The distinction was small and it was everything."

**Problem:** This is the narrator stepping back to editorialize. The format rules (§IV, Tier 1) ban meta-address: "The narrator must never step back to explain the novel's own structural choices." This sentence tells the reader the thematic significance of the distinction between conviction and craft, rather than trusting the rendered contrast to carry it.

**Fix:** Cut the sentence. The preceding render (conviction vs. craft, God and king vs. fighting as profession) does the work. The reader is not stupid.

### F4 — Over-Explained Interiority / Telling

**Location:** Lines 93-96
**Type:** Telling-not-rendering (Tier 2)
**Text:**
> "He did not see the door close. He saw a tactical situation. The assault failed. We hold here. We dig in. We wait for the next order.
>
> I saw the door close. I did not know it was a door. I did not know that what had closed was the narrative I had carried from Pamplona — the crusade, the quick victory, the king returning, the fueros restored, the Church triumphant. The narrative that had made the marching and the killing and the shaking of the hands into a story with an end. The assault failed. Madrid held. The end was not coming. The war was not a march to a destination. The war was a siege, and a siege has no end — it has only duration."

**Problem:** The narrator names the metaphor ("the door"), then names the entire narrative that has closed ("the crusade, the quick victory, the king returning, the fueros restored, the Church triumphant"), then restates the meaning three times: "The end was not coming," "The war was not a march to a destination," "a siege has no end — it has only duration." This is the format rules' "Not X but Y" construction (§IV, Tier 1) embedded in a cascade of telling. The scene has already rendered this: the assault failed, Madrid held, Vidal's flat delivery. The narrator trusts nothing and explains everything.

**Fix:** Keep Tomás's paragraph (the tactical reading works). For the narrator's response, render through physical detail: the body's reaction, a specific detail in the room, the sound changing. Something like: "The sound changed after Vidal left. Not the artillery. Something else. The walls felt closer. The rifle was heavier in my hands than it had been that morning." Trust the concrete to carry the abstract.

### F5 — Omniscience Leak / POV Breach

**Location:** Lines 59, 93
**Type:** Omniscience leak (Tier 2)
**Text (line 59):**
> "He saw what I saw — the bearing, the equipment, the faces. He saw the competence. He saw the hardness."

**Text (line 93):**
> "He saw a tactical situation. The assault failed. We hold here. We dig in. We wait for the next order."

**Problem:** The format rules (§IX) state: "The narrator does not have access to other characters' interiority." Martín cannot know what Tomás saw or how Tomás categorized the information. "He saw the competence" and "He saw a tactical situation" are assertions about Tomás's interior perception. Martín can observe Tomás's face, posture, and words — not his cognitive processing.

**Fix:** Render through observable behavior. "His eyes moved over the column the same way they moved over a field — measuring, counting, forgetting nothing." For line 93: "He nodded. His face was the same face from the olive grove — flat, practical, the face of a man who had heard a fact and placed it."

### F6 — Named Emotion / Physical Telling

**Location:** Line 123
**Type:** Named emotional state via physical symptom (Tier 2)
**Text:**
> "His eyes were brown and deep and the skin around them was tight from sun and from something else, something the sun did not do."

**Problem:** "Something else, something the sun did not do" is a hedge — it gestures at an emotion without rendering it. The format rules (§V) rank physical symptoms as "Better, but generic" and specific physical detail as "Good." This construction falls between generic symptom and hedge, telling the reader an unnamed emotional state exists without specifying it.

**Fix:** Make it specific. "His eyes were brown and the skin around them was tight and the tightness was not from the sun." Or better: describe what Martín actually sees — the bloodshot whites, the heaviness of the lids, the way the priest's gaze does not focus on Martín's face but passes through it.

### F7 — Over-Explained Subtext

**Location:** Lines 128-129
**Type:** Telling-not-rendering (Tier 2)
**Text:**
> "I did not say anything else. He did not say anything else. The word was enough. Hijo. Son. The word he used for all of us. The word that was a blessing in itself, the priest's hand on the head of the boy, the older man's acknowledgment that the boy was young and the boy was here and the boy was looking at the dead and the dead were not what he expected."

**Problem:** The dialogue exchange ("Father." / "Hijo.") is already rendered and potent. The narrator then explains what "Hijo" means — "Son. The word he used for all of us. The word that was a blessing in itself" — and then narrates the entire emotional situation ("the older man's acknowledgment that the boy was young and the boy was here"). This is the format rules' named-emotion-with-qualifier pattern: the scene works, the explanation weakens it.

**Fix:** "Hijo." is enough. Cut everything after "The word was enough." The silence between the two men is the scene. The explanation fills the silence with noise.

### F8 — Pure Summary / Telling

**Location:** Lines 143-144
**Type:** Pure summary (Tier 2)
**Text:**
> "Tomás did not mean disrespect. He meant scale. The village had been a small dead — one body, two, the man against the wall, the woman in the doorway. The blessing had been a small blessing, the priest's hand on the brow of a man he had known. The row of bodies beside the school was a large dead. The blessing was the same. The scale was not. Tomás was noting the scale. He was noting it the way a shepherd notes the weather: without judgment, without theology, with the pragmatism of a man who has to walk in it."

**Problem:** The narrator interprets Tomás's dialogue at length. "Tomás did not mean disrespect" is the narrator telling us what Tomás meant — an omniscience leak and a telling violation. The shepherd simile is earned, but the three sentences before it ("He meant scale... The blessing was the same. The scale was not.") explain what the dialogue already communicated. Tomás said "The big priest. Blessing the big dead." The word "big" does the work. The narrator doesn't trust it.

**Fix:** Keep the shepherd simile — it's good. Cut "Tomás did not mean disrespect. He meant scale." and the summary of village vs. city. The contrast is already established by the scene.

### F9 — Verbatim Refrain / Repetition

**Location:** Lines 113, 115, 116 (and echoing through 119-121)
**Type:** Intra-chapter refrain — borderline structural (Tier 3)
**Text:**
> "The same words. The same blessing he had said over the rifles in the village" (line 109)
> "The same cross. The same words. The same voice." (line 113)
> "He blessed the Nationalist dead and the enemy dead and the Moroccan dead and the Legion dead. He blessed the man in uniform and the man in civilian clothes and the man in the blue-gray of the International Brigades. He blessed them all with the same hands and the same voice and the same God." (lines 115-116)

**Problem:** The repetition is clearly intentional — the ritual repetition of blessing mirrors the priest's physical repetition. This is the chapter's strongest structural device. However, by the third iteration the pattern has communicated its point and the prose continues explaining: "He blessed them all with the same hands and the same voice and the same God." The final sentence restates what the two preceding sentences already rendered.

**Fix:** Trim the final summary sentence. "He blessed them all with the same hands and the same voice and the same God" is the narrator underlining. The enumeration (Nationalist, enemy, Moroccan, Legion, uniform, civilian, blue-gray) already does the work. Cut the final sentence or shorten to: "He blessed them all."

### F10 — Adverb in Dialogue Tag

**Location:** Line 145
**Type:** Adverb in dialogue tag (Tier 2)
**Text:**
> "'The blessing does not change,' Padre Joaquin said. His voice was quiet. Not the pastoral voice. Not the homily cadence. A smaller voice. The voice of the confessional."

**Problem:** While "said" is the correct tag (no adverb in the tag itself), "His voice was quiet" is a telling construction that names a quality. The format rules (§VIII) say: "Never use adverbial dialogue tags." The subsequent lines ("Not the pastoral voice. Not the homily cadence. A smaller voice. The voice of the confessional") are a banned "Not X. Not Y. Z." construction (§IV, Tier 1).

**Fix:** Render the voice through action: "Padre Joaquin said. He did not look up. His hands were still on the bread." Or: "Padre Joaquin said, the words barely reaching past the step." Cut "Not the pastoral voice. Not the homily cadence. A smaller voice." The format rules explicitly ban this construction.

### F11 — Named Emotion / Abstract Telling

**Location:** Line 113
**Type:** Named abstract interiority (Tier 2)
**Text:**
> "The blessing was the same. The body was different. The difference was the point and the problem and the thing I would carry."

**Problem:** "The thing I would carry" is named interiority — the narrator tells the reader this is significant and will stay with him. The triadic structure ("the point and the problem and the thing I would carry") is the format rules' Tier 1 triadic parallel ban. The scene has already rendered the contrast: same blessing, different body, the priest moving down the row. The reader registers the dissonance. The narrator doesn't need to label it.

**Fix:** Cut the final sentence. End on "The body was different." The period is enough.

### F12 — Retrospective Over-Explanation (Ending Zone)

**Location:** Lines 167-177 (final 15%)
**Type:** Telling-not-rendering / authorial intrusion (Tier 1)
**Text:**
> "The boy would walk into it because the boy did not know the door had closed. The boy did not know the quick victory was dead." (lines 167-168)
> "The boy is wrong. The boy is twenty-two. The boy is in Madrid. The sounds will not stop. The sounds will go on for three more years." (line 175)
> "But the boy does not know that." (line 177)

**Problem:** The ending coda (lines 163-177) is the chapter's weakest section. The narrator repeats "The boy" thirteen times in the final fifteen lines, each iteration explaining what the boy doesn't know. Line 175 is the worst offender: "The sounds will go on for three more years" is the narrator asserting historical knowledge the character cannot have — it is dramatic irony explained rather than rendered. "The boy is wrong" is authorial judgment. The format rules (§IV, Tier 1) ban meta-address: "The narrator must never step back to explain the novel's own structural choices." The ending is the narrator explaining the novel's own tragic architecture.

**Fix:** The retrospective frame ("I am at the table") is established and works. But the final coda should render, not explain. The boy holds the crucifix. The boy prays. The boy sleeps. These are concrete. Stop there, or let the concrete details accumulate without the narrator's interpretive overlay. Cut "The boy is wrong," "The sounds will not stop. The sounds will go on for three more years," and "But the boy does not know that." The image of the boy sleeping in the dark with the crucifix in his hand while the artillery pulses is already the ending. The narrator's explanation diminishes it.

### F13 — Hedge / Avoided Specificity

**Location:** Lines 49-50
**Type:** Hedge (Tier 1)
**Text:**
> "something that was not quite fear and not quite admiration but lived in the space between"

**Problem:** "Something between X and Y" is explicitly banned in the format rules (§IV, Tier 1). "Not quite fear and not quite admiration" is a hedge that avoids specificity. The narrator tells the reader an emotion exists between two poles without rendering what it actually is.

**Fix:** Make it specific. What did the men in Pamplona actually do when they talked about the Army of Africa? "In Pamplona the men talked about them over wine and lowered their voices, the way men lower their voices when they talk about things they have not seen but believe." Render the behavior, not the abstract emotional space.

### F14 — Triadic Parallel / Pattern Repetition

**Location:** Lines 164-165
**Type:** Triadic parallel (Tier 1/3)
**Text:**
> "I held the crucifix. The artillery pulsed. The city breathed. The dead lay in their row. The priest sat on the step. The boy lay in the dark. The hands held the wood. The hands were steady."

**Problem:** This is a catalog of static images — each sentence a snapshot, none advancing. The format rules (§IV, Tier 1) flag triadic parallel as default rhythm. Here the pattern repeats for eight sentences, each following the same Subject-Verb-Object structure. The prose becomes a list.

**Fix:** Vary the sentence structure. Break the pattern with one longer sentence or one fragment. Or cut the catalog to three images and let the reader supply the rest. Less is more in a closing image.

## Verdict: CONDITIONAL PASS

**Strengths:**
- The assault sequence (lines 69-84) is exceptional — rendered through sound, vibration, physical sensation. No named emotions, no telling. The body learns through the ears. This is the prose at its best.
- The Army of Africa passage (lines 47-57) renders through observable detail — the way they walk, hold rifles, look at buildings. Physical specificity drives the characterization.
- The dead-blessing scene (lines 101-121) is largely earned. The repetition of ritual gesture carries the weight without narrator commentary — until the narrator explains it (F8, F9, F11).
- Dialogue is sparse and effective. "Artillery." / "I know." / "Big." — Tomás's voice is distinct and rendered through action, not description.
- The retrospective frame ("I am at the table") is a legitimate structural choice and works when it renders rather than explains.

**Weaknesses:**
- The final 30% of the chapter tips into over-explanation. Lines 93-96 (the door metaphor), lines 128-129 (Hijo explanation), and lines 167-177 (the boy coda) all explain what the rendered scene already communicates.
- The "Not X. Not Y. Z." construction appears at line 145 despite being banned.
- "Something between X and Y" appears at line 50 despite being banned.
- Three instances of omniscience leak (narrator asserting what Tomás saw/thought).
- The ending coda's "The boy is wrong" and "the sounds will go on for three more years" are the chapter's most damaging violations — the narrator asserting historical knowledge and passing judgment on the character.

**Required revision:** Cut or rework findings F1, F3, F4, F10, F12. The chapter's core prose is strong. The problem is the narrator's lack of trust in the rendered scene.
