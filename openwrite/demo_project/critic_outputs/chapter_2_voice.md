## 1. Register Identification & Coherence

### Martín Javier Echeverría (Narrator / Remembered Self)

**Register 1: The Catechism** — Present and correctly deployed. The boy's declarative certainty surfaces when speaking about the cause:

> "We fight for the king. For the fueros. For Christ the King."
> — Line 175, dialogue, Catechism register. Three prepositional phrases, no hedging, the syntax of taught answers. Correct.

> "this is what the crusade looks like. This is the land we are saving."
> — Line 19, interior thought, Catechism register. The repeated "This is" structure — declarative certainty applied to landscape. The boy mapping ideology onto geography. Correct.

> "The fueros were the rights of the Basque-Navarrese people. The fueros were the ancient laws that predated Spain. The fueros were the foundation of the Carlist cause."
> — Lines 181, interior thought, Catechism register. Anaphoric repetition of "The fueros were" — the catechism's cadence applied to explanation. This is the boy reciting what he was taught. Correct.

**Register 2: The Son** — Present in the letter-writing scene and the father references:

> "My father doesn't need me to write. He knows."
> — Line 77, dialogue, Son register. Declarative but deferential — the certainty comes from trust in the father's understanding, not from Martín's own authority. Correct.

> "I wrote in my own hand — the handwriting my father taught me, small, precise, slanted to the right."
> — Line 67, narration, Son register. The father's instruction embedded in the physical act. The specificity of "slanted to the right" — the son carrying the father's craft. Correct.

> "*The crucifix is in my pocket.* That meant: I am still what you made me. *The beret is on my head.* That meant: the tradition continues."
> — Lines 157, narration, Son register. The coded language of the Carlist father-son bond. The son knows the father's reading habits and writes accordingly. Correct.

**Register 3: The Soldier** — Present in concrete physical observations:

> "The railcar was third class. Wooden benches, no padding."
> — Line 7, narration, Soldier register. Short, factual, no abstraction. Correct.

> "The air was sweat and wool and the oil from the rifle stocks and the dust that came through the windows in sheets."
> — Line 7, narration, Soldier register. Sensory catalog, concrete nouns, no interpretation. Correct.

**Assessment:** All three Act I registers are present and correctly deployed. The Catechism register dominates early (the boy's certainty), the Son register appears in father-related moments, and the Soldier register grounds physical description. No register confusion detected.

### Tomás Eguía

**Register 1: The Companion** — Correctly deployed in camp and march scenes:

> "It's flat," he said.
> — Line 31, dialogue, Companion register. Two words, factual, not a complaint. Matches profile: "He said it the way he said most things — as a fact, not a complaint." Correct.

> "Rabbits."
> — Line 133, dialogue, Companion register. One word, a grin implied, the deflection of seriousness with humor. Matches profile: "He deflects seriousness with humor." Correct.

> "That one's an officer."
> — Line 139, narration of Tomás's speech, Companion register. Classification, not opinion. Matches profile: "the way he might say that one's a ewe or that one's a ram." Correct.

**Register 3: The Son** — Correctly deployed in the letter-dictation scene:

> "Tell her the food is not so bad. Tell her the woman here cooks well. Tell her I am with good men."
> — Lines 65, dialogue, Son register. The repetition of "Tell her" — the dictated letter, the man who cannot write fluently. Matches profile exactly: "Tell my mother I am well. Tell her the food is not so bad. Tell her I will come home." Correct.

> "Tell her I will come home."
> — Line 69, dialogue, Son register. The narrator's analysis confirms the register: "Tomás said it as a fact." Correct.

> "We fight. We win. We go home."
> — Line 219, dialogue, Companion register (borderline Son). Three blunt declaratives. The war reduced to its simplest logic. Matches profile: "He does not talk about the cause. He talks about food, sleep, women, home." Correct.

**Assessment:** Tomás is the novel's most voice-consistent character in this chapter. Every line of dialogue matches his profile. The Companion register dominates; the Son register appears only in the private letter scene. No violations.

### Teniente Rodrigo Vidal

**Register 1: The Officer** — Correctly deployed on first appearance:

> "Name," he said.
> — Line 111, dialogue, Officer register. One word. Not "What's your name?" — the imperative clipped to its minimum. Matches profile: "Short sentences. Military vocabulary." Correct.

> "Show me your sight picture."
> — Line 119, dialogue, Officer register. Instruction as order, not request. Correct.

> "Low," Vidal said. "You're shooting at the base. Aim at the top third. The bullet drops."
> — Lines 123-124, dialogue, Officer register. "Not criticism. Information." The narrator's interpretation confirms the register: Vidal's speech is functional, not emotional. Matches profile: "He does not waste words because wasted words get men killed." Correct.

> "We'll use you on the forward posts."
> — Line 137, dialogue, Officer register. Decision delivered as statement. No explanation, no praise. Correct.

**Register 2: The Castilian** — Not explicitly present in dialogue, but the narrator renders Vidal's reflective quality through observation:

> "something in his eyes shifted — not warmth, exactly. Recognition. The recognition of one professional for another, even if the other's profession was rabbits."
> — Lines 135-136, narration, Vidal observed. The Castilian register's "recognition" — not warmth, but professional respect. The narrator captures this without putting it in Vidal's mouth. Appropriate for a first appearance. Correct.

**Assessment:** Vidal's introduction is voice-disciplined. His dialogue is limited to Officer register — appropriate for a professional meeting volunteers for the first time. The Castilian register is rendered through the narrator's observation rather than Vidal's own speech, which is the right choice: we see Vidal as Martín sees him, not as he sees himself. No violations.

## 2. Voice Distinctiveness

### Martín vs. Tomás

The distinction is sharp and consistent:

| Element | Martín | Tomás |
|---------|--------|-------|
| Sentence length | Long, reflective | Short, blunt |
| Abstraction | Present (the fueros, the crusade) | Absent (food, sleep, home) |
| Vocabulary | Formal, precise | Colloquial, practical |
| Interiority | Extensive | None (rendered through the narrator) |

Example of clear distinction in the same scene:

> Martín: "The man in the square. He didn't know what the fueros were."
> Tomás: "So?"
> Martín: "So — he's on our side. He's fighting with us. And he doesn't know."
> Tomás: "We fight. We win. We go home."
> — Lines 213-219. Martín questions; Tomás reduces. The Catechism register meets the Companion register. Correct.

### Martín vs. Vidal

The distinction is institutional and tonal:

> Martín's narration of Vidal: "He was counting. He was sorting. He was making the column into a thing that could function."
> Vidal's dialogue: "Name." / "Low." / "We'll use you on the forward posts."
> — Lines 141, 111, 123, 137. Martín observes at length; Vidal speaks in fragments. The narrator's expansiveness contrasts with the character's economy. Correct.

### Martín vs. Padre Joaquín

The distinction is between the observer and the observed:

> Padre Joaquín: "Go with God. The road is His. The march is His. The cause is His."
> Martín's interior thought: "I thought: this is what the crusade looks like."
> — Lines 89, 19. The priest blesses; the boy interprets. The Pastor register's warmth meets the Catechism register's certainty. Correct.

## 3. Narrative Voice Consistency (POV)

### First-Person Retrospective

The POV is consistent throughout: Martín, ~30 years after the war, looking back. The voice spec requires:
- Past tense for remembered events ✓
- Present tense for the act of writing ✓
- The immediacy collapse (body bridging past and present) ✓

**Present-tense writing frame appearances:**

> "I am at the table now. The inkwell is full."
> — Line 5. Grounding location established early. ✓

> "The boy did not know. I am at the table. I am looking at the boy."
> — Line 205. Grounding location mid-chapter. ✓

> "I am at the table. The inkwell is full. The pen is in my hand."
> — Line 233. Grounding location at close. ✓

Three appearances — appropriate frequency for a 239-line chapter. Each anchors the narrator's present before returning to the past.

**Immediacy collapse instances:**

> "I am writing this and I can feel the heat. Not the memory of the heat — the heat itself, stored somewhere in the body's accounting, filed under the skin."
> — Lines 17. Body bridging present writing and past sensation. Heat. ✓

> "I am writing this and I can feel the vibration. The way the floorboards of the hardware store carry the truck's rumble the same way the railcar's planks carried the clatter of the wheels."
> — Lines 5-6. Body bridging present vibration and past vibration. Sound/proprioception. ✓

> "the crucifix under my pillow... the wood was warm from my body"
> — Line 91. Body-object contact bridging time. Touch. ✓

> "The road was under my feet even in sleep — I could feel it, the white dust, the heat."
> — Line 225. Body bridging sleep and memory. Proprioception. ✓

Four instances, each using a different sensory bridge (heat, vibration, touch, proprioception). The voice spec warns: "Never the same bridge twice in a chapter." This is correctly followed. ✓

**Temporal distance — "The boy" / "The man" split:**

> "The boy thought that. The boy at the window."
> — Line 21. ✓

> "The boy admired the craft. The man at this table knows what the craft served."
> — Line 147. ✓

> "The boy thought this."
> — Line 201. ✓

> "The boy did not know. I am at the table. I am looking at the boy."
> — Line 205. ✓

> "The boy marched. He did not know what the land was teaching him."
> — Line 231. ✓

> "He does not know... I am writing this and the boy does not know."
> — Lines 237, 239. ✓

Six instances. All serve the voice spec's technique of the narrator observing his younger self. No violations.

**Writing-as-reckoning frame:**

> "I want to be precise: he thought the brown land was something to be saved."
> — Line 21. The narrator correcting potential romanticization. ✓

> "I want to say it was easy. I want to say the faith settled on me like the dust settled on my boots... It was not easy. It was not natural."
> — Lines 93-94. The narrator admitting the temptation of easy memory and refusing it. ✓

> "I want to say I did not, because in the years since Vidal has become something else... But I admired it."
> — Line 147. The narrator's honesty about his own past admiration. ✓

Three instances, each serving the reckoning function. The narrator does not hide behind hindsight; he uses it to be precise. ✓

## 4. Subtext Quality

### The Fueros Scene (Lines 159-197)

The chapter's strongest subtext moment. The farmer asks "What are the fueros?" and Martín cannot explain them in terms the farmer understands. The scene operates on three levels:

1. **Surface:** A farmer learning a new word.
2. **Martín's understanding:** The fueros are everything; the farmer's not-knowing is a failure.
3. **The narrator's hindsight:** The not-knowing is the beginning of the end — the coalition's breadth is its vulnerability.

> "It was enough for him. It was not enough for me."
> — Line 195. Parallel declaratives. The chiasmus of understanding: the farmer is satisfied; Martín is not. The subtext is that the coalition's cohesion is based on a misunderstanding that will eventually collapse it. ✓

> "I did not know that a coalition that does not share a name does not share a purpose, and that a purpose unnamed is a purpose that can be redefined by whoever holds the power to name it."
> — Line 203. The narrator's retrospective political thesis. This is the most overtly analytical sentence in the chapter. The voice spec warns against "editorializing" — but this passage does not tell the reader what to feel; it tells the reader what the narrator now understands. It is earned by the preceding scene. Borderline but acceptable. ✓

### Tomás's Simplicity as Subtext

> "We fight. We win. We go home."
> — Line 219. Tomás's reduction of the war to three verbs. The subtext is that this simplicity is both his strength (he does not overthink) and his vulnerability (he cannot see the political machinery that will consume him). The narrator's analysis confirms: "The fueros were my question. The going home was his." ✓

### Vidal's Introduction as Institutional Subtext

> "not a beret, the cap, the peaked thing with the insignia, the thing that said Army, not requeté"
> — Lines 101-102. The cap vs. beret distinction as institutional marker. The subtext is rendered through objects: Vidal represents a different order — professional, non-ideological, the state's military rather than the movement's militia. No exposition needed. ✓

## 5. Pattern Repetition (Advisory)

### Colon + List Pattern

The syntactic pattern of [clause]: [list of 3-4 items] appears six times:

> "flattened it, burned the green out, left the brown" — Line 15
> "the green, the hills, the river, the certainty" — Line 237
> "the flat, the brown, the endless" — Line 237
> "the beret, the crucifix, the blessing" — implied in line 107
> "the road, the rifle, the bread, and the certainty" — Line 221
> "its own heat, its own distance, its own people" — Line 21

While each instance serves a different purpose (landscape loss, military supply, Vidal's inventory), the rhythmic pattern is recognizable. This is not a violation — the voice spec permits lists and catalogs — but it is a pattern that could become monotonous if unchecked in subsequent chapters.

**Recommendation:** Track this pattern across chapters 3-5. If it appears 6+ times per chapter consistently, consider varying the list structure (interrupted lists, two-item lists, lists embedded mid-sentence rather than at clause end).

### "I did not know" Refrain

> "I did not know that the meseta was not empty." — Line 21
> "I did not know this then." — Line 93
> "I did not know that the breadth was the danger." — Line 203
> "He does not know that the meseta will become the landscape of the war." — Line 237

Four instances. All serve the narrator's retrospective voice — the man at the table correcting the boy's ignorance. The repetition functions as a refrain, but four times in one chapter is notable. The voice spec does not prohibit refrains, but warns against monotony.

**Recommendation:** This refrain is thematically appropriate for Chapter 2 (the boy's first encounter with the wider world). If it persists into chapters 3-5 at the same frequency, it will become a tic. The voice spec's fragments register ("The beret. The crucifix. The king who did not come.") is the intended late-novel refrain; "I did not know" should not compete with it.

### Polysyndeton ("and...and...and")

> "the valleys, the hillsides, the fields of wheat that turn gold in July but stay green at the edges where the irrigation runs, the poplars along the roads, the oaks on the slopes" — Line 11 (not polysyndeton, but long catalog)
> "the green was gone and the hills were gone and the river was gone and the only thing that remained was the beret on my head and the crucifix in my pocket" — Line 79
> "I held the crucifix in my pocket and the letter to my father was on my lap and I thought" — Line 197

This is a consistent syntactic habit of the narrator's reflective mode. It is not a violation — the voice spec's rhythm section permits "longer sentences that carry the freight of reflection." But the "and...and...and" construction appears in nearly every reflective paragraph. Tracking recommended.

## 6. Voice Spec Risks — Assessment

The voice spec identifies four risks:

1. **Self-pity.** Not present. The narrator observes the boy with precision, not pity. "The boy did not know" is factual, not sentimental. ✓
2. **Indulgence.** Minimal. The immediacy technique serves the scenes (heat, vibration, crucifix touch) rather than displaying itself. ✓
3. **Editorializing.** Borderline at line 203 ("a coalition that does not share a name does not share a purpose"), but earned by the scene and delivered as the narrator's understanding, not as an instruction to the reader. Acceptable. ✓
4. **Monotony of technique.** The colon+list pattern and "I did not know" refrain are the primary concerns. Neither has reached the level of monotony in this chapter, but both should be tracked. Advisory. ✓

## Verdict

**PASS.** The chapter demonstrates disciplined voice execution. The locked spec's core techniques — first-person retrospective, immediacy collapse, grounding location, writing-as-reckoning frame — are all deployed correctly. Character voice registers are distinct and match their profiles. Tomás is the chapter's most consistent voice; Vidal's introduction is disciplined; Padre Joaquín's Pastor register is correctly limited to blessing mode. The advisory findings (pattern repetition) are tracking items for subsequent chapters, not violations of this chapter's voice integrity.
