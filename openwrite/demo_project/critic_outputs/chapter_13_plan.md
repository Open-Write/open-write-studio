# Chapter 13 Plan — "The Chaplain's Confession"

---

## 1. Chapter Number and Title
Chapter 13 — "The Chaplain's Confession"

## 2. Characters Present
- **Martín Javier Echeverría** (POV) — The Fracture
- **Padre Joaquín** — The Doubt (explicit for the first time)

## 3. Outline Summary
January 1939. Barcelona falls. The requeté are in a rear area, waiting. Martín visits Padre Joaquín in the field hospital — the chaplain has collapsed, not from a wound but from exhaustion and something else. They sit together in the quiet. Padre Joaquín speaks — not as a pastor but as a man. "I blessed the bullets. I blessed the men who fired them. I blessed the men who died from them. I am not sure these are the same blessing." He does not weep. He is past weeping. He says: "I told them God was with them. I was not sure God was with anyone." Martín listens. He does not have an answer. He holds the crucifix — the one from his grandfather. He does not know if it means what his father told him it meant. Padre Joaquín says: "Your faith is not gone. It is in a different place. I do not know where." The chapter ends with the news: the Republic is collapsing. The war will be over soon.

## 4. Emotional Palette
**Intimacy** and **Spiritual desolation.** The reader should feel the quiet between two men who have lost the thing they carried — and the tenderness of the loss.

## 5. Key Beats
1. Barcelona falls. The war is ending.
2. Padre Joaquín collapsed — not from a wound, from exhaustion and something else.
3. The confession — "I blessed the bullets. I blessed the men who fired them. I blessed the men who died from them. I am not sure these are the same blessing."
4. "I told them God was with them. I was not sure God was with anyone."
5. Martín holds the crucifix. He does not know if it means what his father told him it meant.
6. "Your faith is not gone. It is in a different place. I do not know where."

## 6. Word Count Target
4,000–5,000 words.

## 7. Body Anchors
Hands (the crucifix, Padre Joaquín's hands), breath (the quiet), eyes (the field hospital).

---

## Notes for the Prose Writer

1. **Padre Joaquín's confession is the chapter's centerpiece.** It must be delivered in fragments, not as a polished speech. The doubt is not articulate — it is halting, unfinished.
2. **The fixed dialogue must land as fragments.** "I blessed the bullets. I blessed the men who fired them. I blessed the men who died from them. I am not sure these are the same blessing." — but rendered as a man finding words, not delivering a thesis.
3. **Martín listens. He does not have an answer.** The silence is the chapter's emotional architecture.
4. **"Your faith is not gone. It is in a different place. I do not know where."** The last thing Padre Joaquín says. It must land as tenderness, not as diagnosis.
5. **All meta-critic constraints apply.**
