# Chapter 15 Plan — "The Altar" (FINALE)

---

## 1. Chapter Number and Title
Chapter 15 — "The Altar"

## 2. Characters Present
- **Martín Javier Echeverría** (POV) — Silence
- **Don Eusebio** — The Silence (final)
- **Amaia** — through letter only
- **Tomás** — through visit to his family

## 3. Outline Summary
April–May 1939. Martín returns to Pamplona. The city celebrates. The requeté march through the streets. The red berets are visible — but they are outnumbered by the Falangist flags. Don Eusebio is in the crowd. He does not cheer. He watches his son march past. Martín visits Tomás's family in Aoiz. He gives Tomás's mother the scapular she sewed. He says: "He was brave. He was my friend." He does not say: "He died for nothing." He goes home. He stands in the dining room. The crucifix is above the table. The red beret is in his hand. Don Eusebio comes in. They stand together. Don Eusebio takes the crucifix from the wall, holds it, and hangs it back. He says nothing. Martín puts the red beret on the hook in the hallway. He goes to his room. He sits on the bed. A letter waits on the pillow — forwarded from the battalion. From Amaia, in exile in France. "The school is closed. The language is banned. You won. I hope the king you fought for was worth it." Martín reads the letter. He folds it. He places it under the crucifix on his bedside table. The final image: the crucifix on the wall. The red beret on the hook. The letter on the table. The silence.

## 4. Emotional Palette
**Silence** and **Weight.** The reader should feel the absence — the space where the crusade used to be. The silence is not peace. It is the space where a century of Carlist faith was.

## 8. The Four Readings
The ending must support all four readings (hopeful, melancholy, metaphysical, the fourth reading from Amaia's letter) without collapsing into any single one. Trust the objects. Do not explain.

## Notes for the Prose Writer

1. **This is the novel's last chapter.** Everything arrives here. The crucifix. The beret. The letter. The silence.
2. **Don Eusebio's silence is load-bearing.** He takes the crucifix, holds it, hangs it back. He says nothing. Render the hands. The weight. The hanging. Do not explain.
3. **Amaia's letter is the ending's most destabilizing element.** "The school is closed. The language is banned. You won. I hope the king you fought for was worth it." Render as a letter being read — the paper, the handwriting, the words arriving one by one.
4. **The final image is objects in a room.** Crucifix on the wall. Beret on the hook. Letter on the table. The silence. Trust the reader to do the work.
5. **No narrator explanation of the ending.** The objects speak. The silence speaks. The reader's interpretation is the reader's work.
6. **All meta-critic constraints apply.** This chapter must be the cleanest in the manuscript.
