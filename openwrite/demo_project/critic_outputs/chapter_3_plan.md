# Chapter 3 Plan — "First Blood"

---

## 1. Chapter Number and Title
Chapter 3 — "First Blood"

## 2. Characters Present
- **Martín Javier Echeverría** (POV) — active registers: The Catechism (cracking), The Soldier (primary for the first time)
- **Tomás Eguía** — active register: The Companion, The Brother (combat)
- **Padre Joaquín** — active register: The Pastor (blessing before battle)
- **Teniente Rodrigo Vidal** — active registers: The Officer, The Realist (background)

## 3. POV Character
Martín Javier Echeverría. First-person retrospective with immediacy technique. The narrator is Martín ~30 years later, writing at the table. The remembered Martín is 22, at the front for the first time, about to kill a man for the first time.

## 4. Narrative Distance
First-person retrospective with immediacy collapse. This chapter demands the closest possible distance — the body's response to killing. The narrator must stay inside the sensory experience: the trigger, the recoil, the man falling, the hands shaking. The retrospective frame should appear minimally — the immediacy collapse should dominate.

## 5. Consciousness Markers
The remembered Martín notices:
- **The body's mechanics.** The trigger pull, the recoil, the smoke, the smell. His consciousness narrows to the physical act.
- **The man who falls.** Not a soldier, not an enemy — a man. Running. Then falling. The specificity of the body.
- **The aftermath.** Hands shaking. Tomás's water. The dirt. The dark. The crucifix in the hand.
- **What he does NOT notice:** Tactics, strategy, the larger battle. His world has shrunk to the rifle, the trigger, the man.

## 6. POV Constraints
- The narrator knows this is the first of many killings. The boy does not know the numbness is coming.
- The remembered Martín's consciousness is reduced to the physical — the body's response before the mind can process.
- The enemy soldier is rendered only through what Martín can see — a man running, then falling. No interiority.

## 7. Outline Summary
September–October 1936. The battalion's first engagement. Not Madrid — a smaller action, a Republican-held village in the advance toward the capital. The requeté go in with the red berets and the prayers. Padre Joaquín says Mass before the attack. Martín kills a man for the first time. The man is running. Martín fires. The man falls. The chapter does not render this as a dramatic revelation — it renders it as a fact. Martín's hands shake afterward. Tomás gives him water. They dig in. That night, Martín holds the crucifix and prays. The prayer is not articulate. It is the body's prayer — the hands, the breath, the dark. The chapter ends with the battalion receiving news: they are being moved to the Madrid front. The war is about to become real.

## 8. State Changes
- **Before:** Martín is a soldier who has not yet killed. His conviction is intact. His body has not yet been tested.
- **After:** Martín has killed a man. His hands have shaken. The body has responded. The numbness has not yet arrived — that comes later — but the shaking has begun and will stop.
- **Padre Joaquín:** Has blessed the attack. Has said Mass before men went to kill. The blessing is still certain, but it has been used for something new.

## 11. Reader-Belief Phase
**Misdirection active.** The reader still believes this is a war story. The first kill is a war-story beat — expected, dramatic, earned. The reader does not yet see it as the first step in the numbness that will define Martín's arc.

## 12. Reader-State Guidance
- **Must convey:** The physical reality of killing. The body's response. The gap between the crusade's rhetoric and the man falling.
- **Must NOT convey:** That this is the beginning of numbness. The narrator knows; the boy does not. Let the reader feel the shaking without naming its future absence.
- **Can plant (subtly):** The shaking will stop. The body will learn. But this is a seed, not a signal.

## 13. Emotional Palette
**Terror** and **Numbness.** The reader should feel the body's response to killing — and the gap between the crusade's rhetoric and the physical reality of a man falling. Terror is primary (the body's honest response). Numbness is secondary (the faintest edge — the shaking will stop, and the stopping will be worse).

## 14. Chapter Objectives
1. Render Martín's first kill as a physical fact, not a dramatic revelation.
2. Show the body's response — the shaking, the water, the dirt, the dark.
3. Establish Padre Joaquín's blessing before battle — the spiritual framework that will later crack.
4. Plant the numbness seed — the shaking will stop.
5. End Act One with the news: Madrid. The war is about to become real.

## 15. Key Beats
1. **The Mass before battle** (opening) — Padre Joaquín says Mass. The requeté kneel. The red berets are on the ground beside them. The blessing. The prayers. The conviction is at its peak.
2. **The advance** — The battalion moves toward the Republican-held village. The terrain. The heat. The waiting. The body's anticipation.
3. **The kill** — A man is running. Martín fires. The man falls. Rendered as a fact: trigger, recoil, smoke, the man's body changing direction, the ground receiving him. No drama. No revelation. A fact.
4. **The shaking** — Martín's hands afterward. The rifle on his knees. Tomás sits beside him. Water. The cigarette. "The first one is the worst." "That was the first one." "I know."
5. **The night prayer** — Martín holds the crucifix in the dark. The prayer is not articulate. The body praying. The hands, the breath, the dark.
6. **The news** — They are being moved to the Madrid front. The war's scale is about to change.
7. **The narrator's bridge** — The boy does not know the shaking will stop. The numbness will come. The king will not come.

## 19. Knowledge Deltas

### Martín
- **Knew before:** The rifle, the march, the coalition, the crucifix, the beret.
- **Learns:** What it feels like to kill a man. The body's shaking. The gap between rhetoric and reality.
- **Still doesn't know:** That the shaking will stop. That the numbness will come. That this is the first of many.

### Tomás
- **Knew before:** Practical soldiering, the march.
- **Learns:** The same — but Tomás has already processed this. His "The first one is the worst" suggests prior knowledge or prior readiness.
- **Still doesn't know:** That Martín is shaken by more than the fear. Martín is shaken by the gap between the crusade and the kill.

### Padre Joaquín
- **Knew before:** Carlist theology, pastoral ministry, the blessing.
- **Learns:** That blessing men before they kill is different from blessing men before they march.
- **Still doesn't know:** That this difference will grow into doubt.

## 20. Character Architecture Depth

### Martín (POV)
- **Motivation (scene-level):** To do what the requeté does. To fight. To be worthy of the crucifix and the beret.
- **Need (arc-level):** To discover whether faith can survive the act of killing.
- **Blind spot:** He believes the kill will feel like something — a revelation, a weight, a change. It does not. It feels like nothing. The nothing is the wound.
- **Contradiction:** He is a man of faith who has just killed a man, and the faith and the killing coexist without resolution. The body prays at night. The hands that held the rifle hold the crucifix.
- **Interiority access:** Extreme close-up on the body. The trigger, the recoil, the shaking, the crucifix. The narrator stays inside the boy's body.

### Tomás
- **Motivation (scene-level):** To be beside Martín. To give water. To share the cigarette. To normalize.
- **Need (arc-level):** To survive with his humanity intact.
- **Blind spot:** He does not see that Martín's shaking is not just fear — it is the beginning of the gap between faith and action.
- **Contradiction:** He is the most human character and the one least troubled by what they are doing.
- **Interiority access:** Through Martín's observation — the body, the voice, the cigarette.

### Padre Joaquín
- **Motivation (scene-level):** To bless the men. To give the attack spiritual meaning.
- **Need (arc-level):** To maintain the theological framework that makes the crusade just.
- **Blind spot:** He does not yet see that blessing men before they kill is different from blessing men before they march.
- **Contradiction:** He is the Church's voice and will become its conscience.
- **Interiority access:** Through Martín's observation — the blessing, the hands, the voice.
