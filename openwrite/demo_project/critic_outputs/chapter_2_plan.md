# Chapter 2 Plan — "The March South"

---

## 1. Chapter Number and Title
Chapter 2 — "The March South"

## 2. Characters Present
- **Martín Javier Echeverría** (POV) — active registers: The Catechism (primary, beginning to crack), The Soldier (emerging)
- **Tomás Eguía** — active register: The Companion
- **Teniente Rodrigo Vidal** — active registers: The Officer (primary), The Castilian (secondary)
- **Padre Joaquín** — active register: The Pastor (background)
- **Don Eusebio** — present through letters only

## 3. POV Character
Martín Javier Echeverría. First-person retrospective with immediacy technique. The narrator is Martín ~30 years after the war, writing at his father's table in the hardware store. The remembered Martín is 22, marching south, still certain but beginning to see the edges of the wider world.

## 4. Narrative Distance
First-person retrospective with immediacy collapse. The narrator oscillates between the hardware store table and the march south through Castile. The distance between the two is beginning to matter — the narrator knows what the coalition became, the boy is only beginning to sense it.

## 5. Consciousness Markers
The remembered Martín notices:
- **Landscape.** The meseta — flat, brown, endless — is alien to a man from green valleys. The physical terrain registers as wrong, unfamiliar, too open. His Navarrese eye reads the land as absence: no hills, no trees, no rivers.
- **People.** The Castilian villagers are friendly but foreign. Their Spanish sounds different. Their concerns are different. Martín notices what they do NOT know (the fueros) as much as what they do.
- **Military organization.** Vidal's efficiency registers as something Martín has not seen before — professional, systematic, non-ideological. The hardware-store boy recognizes competence in a different register.
- **What he does NOT notice yet:** The political implications of the coalition's breadth. He senses the difference but cannot name it.

## 6. POV Constraints
- The narrator knows the coalition will fracture, the fueros will be abolished, Vidal will survive the war.
- The remembered Martín knows only what a 22-year-old Carlist from Pamplona knows: the march, the landscape, the men around him.
- Vidal's interiority is visible only through behavior, voice, and what Martín can read.
- The Castilian villagers are background — their interiority is not accessed.

## 7. Outline Summary
July–August 1936. The requeté battalion moves south through Castile by train and on foot. Martín sees the meseta for the first time — flat, brown, endless. The Castilian landscape is alien to a man from the green valleys of Navarre. He meets Teniente Vidal, the professional Army officer assigned to command the regular soldiers in the battalion. Vidal is efficient, unideological, and respected. He organizes the requeté's raw courage into something militarily functional. Martín is billeted with Tomás and a dozen other volunteers in a Castilian village. The villagers are friendly — they are Nationalist, Catholic, conservative. But they are not Carlist. They do not know what the fueros are. Martín begins to understand that the crusade means different things to different people. Tomás is unconcerned. "We fight. We win. We go home." Martín writes to his father. The letter is short: "We are well. The men are ready."

## 8. State Changes
- **Before:** Martín knows only Pamplona, the Carlist world, the requeté as a local force.
- **After:** Martín has seen the meseta, met a professional Army officer, encountered non-Carlist Spaniards, and begun to sense the breadth of the coalition.
- **Vidal:** Introduced. Professional, competent, non-ideological. The ground beneath the ideology.
- **Tomás:** Deepened. His pragmatism is established as a counterweight to Martín's conviction.
- **Don Eusebio:** Through his letters — the first letter is short, factual.

## 9. Callbacks Landing
None — this is Chapter 2. Seeds only.

## 10. New Callbacks Seeded
1. **The meseta** — the flat, brown, endless landscape as moral mirror. The land is the coalition: wide, featureless, unified only in appearance. (Payoff: recurring motif through Acts I-II)
2. **Vidal's competence** — the proof that courage exists without faith. (Payoff: through Acts I-IV, Vidal's arc)
3. **Don Eusebio's letters** — short, factual, the Carlist content between the lines. (Payoff: letters grow bitter in Ch5, Ch9, Ch11)
4. **The Castilian village** — the first encounter with non-Carlist Spain. (Payoff: the breadth of the coalition, recurring through Acts I-II)
5. **"We fight. We win. We go home."** — Tomás's pragmatism. (Payoff: Ch12 — he does not go home)

## 11. Reader-Belief Phase
**Misdirection active.** The reader still believes this is a war story about a young Carlist's crusade. The chapter introduces the breadth of the Nationalist coalition, but the reader reads this as world-building, not as threat. The unease is planted but not yet foregrounded.

## 12. Reader-State Guidance
- **Must convey:** The meseta as physical and moral landscape. The alien quality of non-Carlist Spain. Vidal's competence as a quiet challenge to Martín's worldview.
- **Must NOT convey:** That the coalition will fracture. That the fueros will be abolished. The reader should feel the breadth of the coalition as interesting, not threatening.
- **Can plant (subtly):** The villagers' ignorance of the fueros. Tomás's pragmatism as both strength and vulnerability.

## 13. Emotional Palette
**Discovery** and **Unease.** The reader should feel the breadth of the Nationalist coalition — and the first hint that the Carlist cause is one voice among many, not the voice. Discovery is primary (new landscape, new people, new military reality). Unease is secondary (the fueros are unknown here, the crusade means different things).

## 14. Chapter Objectives
1. Introduce Vidal as the professional soldier — competent, non-ideological, the ground beneath the ideology.
2. Render the meseta as physical and moral landscape — alien, featureless, the opposite of Navarre.
3. Show the Carlist cause as one voice among many in the Nationalist coalition.
4. Deepen Tomás through his pragmatism and his relationship with Martín.
5. Establish Don Eusebio's letters as a thread (short, factual, the Carlist content between the lines).

## 15. Key Beats
1. **The train south** (opening) — The battalion on a train, moving through landscape Martín has never seen. The meseta opens. The green valleys are gone.
2. **The meseta** — The flat, brown, endless land. The narrator's bridge: "the boy is seeing the land that will eat the armies."
3. **Vidal's introduction** — The professional officer meets the requeté. His competence, his clipped voice, his non-ideological efficiency. Martín recognizes something he has not seen before.
4. **The Castilian village** — Billeted with Tomás and others. The villagers are friendly, Catholic, conservative. But they do not know the fueros. The conversation where Martín realizes the crusade means something different here.
5. **Tomás's pragmatism** — "We fight. We win. We go home." The ease. The bread. The counterweight.
6. **Don Eusebio's letter** — Short. Factual. "We are well. The men are ready." The Carlist content between the lines.
7. **The narrator's bridge** — The boy does not know. The meseta. The coalition. The cracks that will come.

## 16. Timeline Entry
- **Date:** July–August 1936 (approximately July 19 – August 15)
- **Events:** Battalion travels south by train and on foot through Castile. Billeted in a Castilian village. Meets Vidal. First encounter with non-Carlist Spain.
- **Historical context:** The advance toward Madrid is beginning. The Nationalist coalition is forming. The Army of Africa is advancing from the south.
- **Carlist calendar:** The feast of Santiago (July 25) falls during the march. This should be referenced.

## 19. Knowledge Deltas

### Martín
- **Knew before:** Pamplona, the Carlist world, the requeté, the crucifix, the beret.
- **Learns:** The meseta exists. Non-Carlist Spain exists. The fueros are unknown outside Navarre. Professional soldiers exist (Vidal). The coalition is broad.
- **Still doesn't know:** The coalition will fracture. The fueros will be abolished. Franco's consolidation plans.

### Vidal
- **Knew before:** Professional soldiering, the Rif War, the Army's structure.
- **Learns:** The requeté's courage and faith. Martín's name (if they meet).
- **Still doesn't know:** The Carlist political vision in detail. He doesn't need to.

### Tomás
- **Knew before:** His family, the requeté, practical soldiering.
- **Learns:** The meseta. The breadth of the coalition. That the fueros are unknown elsewhere.
- **Still doesn't know:** That this matters. His pragmatism protects him from the implications.

## 20. Character Architecture Depth

### Martín (POV)
- **Motivation (scene-level):** To march south with the requeté. To be a good soldier. To carry the crucifix and the beret.
- **Need (arc-level):** To discover whether faith can survive the corruption of the cause.
- **Blind spot:** He cannot see the coalition as a threat. He reads the villagers' friendliness as solidarity, not as the breadth that will drown the Carlist cause.
- **Contradiction:** He is a man of a specific tradition marching into a world that does not know that tradition. The specificity that gives his faith its beauty also gives it its vulnerability.
- **Interiority access:** First-person retrospective with immediacy collapse. The narrator's present-tense awareness bridges to the remembered self's sensory experience. Interiority arrives through the body (eyes on the landscape, the heat, the unfamiliar food) and through the narrator's dramatic irony.

### Vidal
- **Motivation (scene-level):** To organize the requeté into something militarily functional. To do his job.
- **Need (arc-level):** To survive the war. To serve competently regardless of ideology.
- **Blind spot:** He does not see that his competence is itself a challenge to the requeté's worldview. He does not care about the fueros. He cares about terrain, logistics, fire discipline.
- **Contradiction:** He is the best soldier in the battalion and he believes in nothing the requeté believe in. His competence is a rebuke to ideology without being a statement about it.
- **Interiority access:** Through Martín's observation only — the voice, the hands, the efficiency, the dry humor.

### Tomás
- **Motivation (scene-level):** To march, to eat, to sleep, to be beside Martín. To go home.
- **Need (arc-level):** To survive with his humanity intact.
- **Blind spot:** He does not think about the coalition or the fueros. The "why" is not his question.
- **Contradiction:** He is the novel's most human character and the one least equipped to understand what the war will become.
- **Interiority access:** Through Martín's observation — the body, the voice, the humor.
