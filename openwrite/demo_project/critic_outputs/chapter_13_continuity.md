## Blocking Findings

### B1. Factual Error — Padre Joaquín Named in Prior Chapters (Line 7)

**Location:** Line 7
**Text:** "I have not written his name since I began this. Eleven chapters."

**Problem:** Padre Joaquín is named extensively across the manuscript prior to Chapter 13:

| Chapter | Mentions of "Joaquín" |
|---------|----------------------|
| Ch1 | 1 (blessing at the Plaza del Castillo) |
| Ch2 | 5 |
| Ch3 | 5 |
| Ch4 | 8 |
| Ch5 | 2 |
| Ch6 | 10 (confession scene, blessing the dead) |
| Ch7 | 10 |
| Ch8 | 6 |
| Ch9 | 5 (blessing the stump, last rites) |
| Ch10 | 8 |
| Ch11 | 0 |
| Ch12 | 0 |

The narrator's claim that he has not written Padre Joaquín's name in "eleven chapters" is factually false. The name appears in chapters 1–10 — ten consecutive chapters, not eleven. The count "eleven" is wrong in both directions: it overstates the gap (Ch11–12 are only two chapters without the name) and miscounts the chapters that contain it.

**Secondary issue:** The narrator's catalog — "The red beret. The march south. Jarama. The unification. The northern campaign. Guernica. Teruel. The ridge. Tomás." — lists "The red beret. The march south." as two separate items. They are one chapter (Ch1: "The Red Beret"). The catalog conflates chapter titles with narrative events and miscounts the total.

**Fix:** Either:
- (a) Remove the claim entirely and let the two-chapter absence (Ch11–12) speak for itself, or
- (b) Rewrite to reflect the actual gap: "I have not written his name in two chapters. Before that, his name was in every chapter since the beginning." This preserves the emotional weight without the factual error.

**Severity:** BLOCKING — a reader who has been following the manuscript will notice the contradiction immediately.

## Advisory Findings

### A1. Scapular Discontinuity (Minor)

The scapular was placed in Martín's pocket alongside the crucifix in Ch12 (line 147: "I put it in my pocket. The pocket with the crucifix. Wood and wool."). In Ch12's post-war frame, Martín gives it to Tomás's mother. In Ch13, the crucifix is held repeatedly but the scapular is not mentioned.

This is not a blocking error — the chapter focuses on the crucifix and Padre Joaquín, and the scapular's absence is appropriate. However, the two objects shared a pocket in Ch12. A brief mention (even "Wood and wool. One I still carry. One I returned.") would tighten the hand-off.

**Severity:** Advisory.

### A2. "The Same" Anaphora — 8 Instances

Ch13 contains 8 instances of "The same" anaphora. The meta-critic limit is 12 per chapter. Ch9 was at 5 (PASS), Ch8 at 22 (FAIL), Ch7 at 27 (FAIL). Ch13 is within limits but the pattern is concentrated in the blessing confession scene (lines 63–75: "The same words. The same God. The same blessing. The same sacrament."). The density in that passage risks the Ch7–Ch8 monorhythm.

**Severity:** Advisory — within limits but monitor.

### A3. Triplet/Polysyndeton Density — 32 Triplet Lines, 20 Polysyndeton Lines

Ch13 has 32 lines with 2+ "and" chains and 20 lines with 3+ "and" chains. The meta-critic target is ≤10 triplets + ≤8 polysyndeton lines per chapter. Ch13 exceeds both by 3x and 2.5x respectively.

The worst passages are the frame-narrator coda (lines 197–214), which uses polysyndeton as its primary rhythmic device: "the road was mud, the sky was clearing, the light was the light of late morning..." and "the body settling into the mattress and the body remembering the ridge and the body remembering the hole in the limestone and the body remembering the weight of Tomás..."

This pattern was flagged as FAIL in Ch9 (41 triplet + 32 polysyndeton lines). Ch13 continues the trend.

**Severity:** Advisory — naturalism critic will flag, but continuity notes the pattern is worsening.

### A4. Frame Break Repetition

Ch13 has 4 frame breaks. The first three use the "inkwell is full" motif:

- Line 5: "The inkwell is full. I filled it this morning..."
- Line 129: "I am at the table. The inkwell is full. The crack in the wood runs from the left edge to just past center."
- Line 159: "I am at the table. The inkwell is full. The pen is in my hand."

The third and fourth breaks (lines 129, 159) are nearly identical. Each frame break should enter through a new sensory detail, per meta-critic note P1 blind spot #3. Lines 129 and 159 repeat the same inventory without variation.

**Severity:** Advisory — meta-critic pattern.

## Chapter-Ending Analysis

The final 15% of Ch13 (lines 183–214) is the frame-narrator coda. It does not collapse into thesis or named emotion — unlike Ch7 and Ch9 endings, which the meta-critic flagged as FAIL. The coda uses physical detail (the crucifix, the inkwell, the pen) and repetition ("I held it the way I hold it now") to close the scene. The final line — "The body does not forget" — is a statement, not a thesis. It is earned by the chapter's content.

However, the coda's polysyndeton density (lines 197–214: 8+ "and" chains in 18 lines) risks the monorhythm that the meta-critic flagged in Ch9's ending. The content is sound; the rhythm is the risk.

**Verdict on ending:** Clean on content. Monitor on rhythm.
