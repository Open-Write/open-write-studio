>
> *Example (for a near-future thriller):*
>
> *The world of THRESHOLD is our world, three years from now. The differences are small but accumulating: climate migration has reshaped American demographics, AI-assisted diagnostics have transformed medicine, and a quiet epidemic of "perceptual anomalies" — people reporting experiences that don't match consensus reality — has been dismissed by mainstream science as stress-induced psychosis. The show is set primarily in Washington, D.C. and the surrounding Virginia/Maryland suburbs, with episodes occasionally taking the team to other locations.*

> [What does the show invent? How far does it stretch real science?]

> ### What Does NOT Exist in This World
> [Explicitly state what is NOT possible in this world. This prevents writers from introducing convenient magic.]

> - *No psychic powers. No telepathy. No precognition. The anomalies are neurological, not supernatural.*

> - *No magic technology. All technology is recognizable and plausible.*

> 2. **[Rule 2]:** [Description]

> *Example:*

> 2. **Institutions are slow, not evil.** The FBI, the government, the scientific establishment — they are bureaucratic and self-preserving, but they are not villainous. They are staffed by competent people who disagree about what to do.
> 3. **The patterns are real.** The show does not tease whether the anomalies are real or imagined. They are real. The question is what they mean, not whether they exist.

## VIII. WORLD BIBLE MAINTENANCE

> This document is **canonical and constraining**. If a writer needs to introduce a location, technology, or institution not described here, they must:
> 1. Draft an amendment to this document
> 2. Have the amendment reviewed for consistency
> 3. Add it to this document before using it in an episode
>
> This prevents canon drift — the slow accumulation of contradictions that destroys audience trust over a multi-season run.
