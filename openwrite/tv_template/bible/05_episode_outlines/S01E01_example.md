## I. EPISODE SUMMARY

Dr. Lena Vasquez, a disgraced forensic psychologist teaching community college, is recruited by FBI Special Agent Marcus Cole to consult on a disturbing case: a 19-year-old college student walked into a Virginia police station and calmly confessed to a murder — but the victim he describes doesn't exist, the location he gives is a vacant lot, and the method he details matches no known case. As Lena and the newly formed Anomalous Behavioral Analysis Unit investigate, they discover that the confession is not a confession at all — it is a prediction. And the pattern in the prediction matches something Lena published in a retracted paper three years ago. Meanwhile, Lena receives a call from her estranged sister Elena, who is in town and wants to meet. Lena does not return the call.

---

## II. A-STORY (CASE OF THE WEEK)

**The anomaly:** A 19-year-old, KEVIN PARK, walks into a Fairfax County police station and confesses to a murder that hasn't happened. He provides specific details — a location, a method, a victim description — that match no known case, no cold case, no missing person. He is calm, cooperative, and utterly certain.

**The investigation:**
- Lena is skeptical but intrigued by the confession's structure — Kevin describes the murder in the past tense, with sensory details no liar would include.
- Jay Park (no relation to Kevin) runs the details against every database. No match.
- Dani Reeves interviews Kevin. He says he "saw it" — not in a dream, not in a vision, but the way you see a car accident happening in front of you. "It already happened. It just hasn't happened here yet."
- Lena recognizes the phrasing. It matches the language in her retracted paper on predictive behavioral modeling.
- Marcus pushes back — this is exactly the kind of case that destroyed Lena's career. Lena pushes harder.
- On Day 3, the murder happens. Exactly as Kevin described. In the exact location he gave — which was a vacant lot when he confessed but is now a construction site where a body is found.

**The resolution:** Kevin is not a murderer. He is a witness to something that hasn't happened yet. The case is closed — the actual murderer is caught through conventional forensics. But Lena now has two data points: her paper, and Kevin. She begins to see the pattern.

**The thematic connection:** The case asks: what do you do with knowledge that shouldn't exist? Kevin's "confession" was dismissed because it didn't fit the framework. Lena's paper was retracted because it didn't fit the framework. The episode argues that the framework is the problem.

---

## III. B-STORY (CHARACTER ARC)

**Character focus:** Lena Vasquez

**The beat:** Lena is teaching Intro to Psych at a community college. She is competent, distant, and unchallenged. When Marcus shows up with the case, she is reluctant — she has spent three years building a life that doesn't require her to see patterns. The case pulls her back in. By the end of the episode, she has agreed to consult "one case at a time."

**The emotional shift:** Lena starts the episode in managed numbness — she has suppressed her pattern recognition to survive. She ends the episode with the numbness cracked open — the pattern is real, and she cannot unsee it.

**Connection to A-story:** Kevin's experience mirrors Lena's. He saw something and was disbelieved. Lena saw something and was professionally destroyed. The episode is about two people who see too much, and the cost of that sight.

---

## IV. C-STORY (SEASON MYTHOLOGY)

**Mythology beat:** The first hint of the pattern. In the background of three scenes — a news ticker, a line of dialogue from a background character, a detail in Kevin's confession — there are elements that don't connect to the case but connect to each other. The audience should not notice on first viewing.

**Information state:** The audience knows that Lena's paper was retracted and that Kevin's confession came true. They do not yet know that these are connected to anything larger.

**Seeds planted:**
- The news ticker mentions a "cluster of perceptual anomalies" in Portland
- Kevin's confession includes a detail about the victim's hands being "wrong" — a detail that will recur in Episode 4
- Lena's apartment has a wall of newspaper clippings she thinks she's hidden

**Seeds paid off:** None (pilot episode).

## V. COLD OPEN

**Duration:** 3 pages
**POV:** Kevin Park
**What happens:** Kevin sits in a holding cell. He is calm. A DETECTIVE enters and reads him his rights. Kevin says, "I know. I already told you. I killed someone on Lot 47 of the Westfield development. Her name is Maria. She has a tattoo of a lark on her left wrist. I used a knife I don't own yet." The detective stares at him. Kevin looks at the wall. "You're going to find her in three days. I'm sorry I couldn't stop it."

**The hook:** The audience doesn't know if Kevin is a killer, a liar, or something else. The calm certainty is the hook.

**Recontextualization:** On first viewing, this reads as a confession. After the episode, it reads as a prediction. After Episode 6, it reads as something else entirely.

## VI. ACT BREAKS

### Act One
**Pages:** 1-12
**What happens:** Lena is introduced in her mundane life. Marcus arrives with the case. Lena resists. We see Kevin's confession in detail.
**Act break hook:** Lena reads Kevin's confession and goes still. She recognizes the language. "Where did you hear this?" Kevin: "I didn't hear it. I saw it."

### Act Two
**Pages:** 13-25
**What happens:** Lena joins the ABU. Jay runs the details — no match. Dani interviews Kevin. Lena digs into her old paper.
**Act break hook:** Jay finds something. Not a match to the confession — a match to Lena's retracted paper. The statistical signature is identical.

### Act Three
**Pages:** 26-38
**What happens:** Lena confronts the implications. Marcus is skeptical but intrigued. Elena calls — Lena ignores it. Lena visits the vacant lot. It's still vacant.
**Act break hook:** Three days later. A construction worker finds a body on Lot 47. Maria. Lark tattoo. Left wrist.

### Act Four
**Pages:** 39-50
**What happens:** The team scrambles. Kevin's prediction is confirmed. Lena is shaken — her paper predicted this kind of pattern recognition, and the FBI buried it. Marcus calls the Pentagon contact who funded the ABU. The contact is very interested in Lena.
**Act break hook:** Lena goes home. She opens a closet she hasn't opened in years. Inside: a wall of newspaper clippings, connected by red string. She's been tracking the pattern for three years. She thought she'd stopped.

### Act Five
**Pages:** 51-62
**What happens:** Lena presents her wall to the team. The pattern is larger than Kevin — there are twelve other cases across four years, all dismissed, all with the same statistical signature. Marcus makes it official: Lena is on the team. Elena calls again. This time, Lena answers. "I can't do this right now." Elena: "Lena. Mom's dead." Lena hangs up. Sits in the dark office. Outside, the streetlights cast shadows that — if you know how to look — form a pattern.
**Act break hook:** Final image: Lena's hand on the desk, ring finger tapping. Three taps. Pause. Three taps.

## VIII. EMOTIONAL PALETTE (EPISODE LEVEL)

**Primary:** Clinical fascination curdling into dread — the case is interesting until the prediction comes true.
**Secondary:** The ache of professional resurrection — Lena is good at this and hates that she's good at it.
**Tertiary:** Sibling estrangement — Elena's call is the episode's emotional detonation.
