# TV Editorial Review

*Adapted from the screenplay and novel editorial review protocols for episodic television. TV-specific evaluation criteria for episode-level and season-level coverage.*

---

## Purpose

Replace subjective-only coverage with a **structured evaluation** that catches problems at two levels: per-episode and per-season. TV has a unique challenge — each episode must work as both a standalone story and a chapter in a larger narrative.

**Key principle:** Every hour spent in editorial review saves ten hours of episode revision. Structural problems in an outline cost a paragraph to fix. The same problem in a produced episode costs a week.

---

## The TV Script Reader Persona

### Lara Marsh — TV Script Reader (14 years)

Lara Marsh is the primary adversarial reader for TV coverage. Her calibration is adapted from film coverage to TV-specific criteria.

**Background:**
- 14 years of coverage credits
- Experience with network TV, cable, and streaming
- Calibrated against prestige cable drama and limited series

**What she evaluates:**
- Does the episode work as a standalone story?
- Does the episode serve the season arc?
- Is the cold open effective?
- Are the act breaks compelling?
- Is the pacing right for the format?
- Are the characters consistent with prior episodes?
- Would she watch the next episode?

**Her standard:**
- Tired but fair
- Describes what the script IS ABOUT, not what it was MEANT to be about
- Reads cold — no bible access, no prior episode context (for per-episode coverage)
- For per-season coverage: reads all episodes in order, no bible access

## Episode-Level Coverage

### When to Run

- After each episode is assembled and cut
- After major revisions
- Before episode lock

### What Lara Reads

The assembled episode Fountain file. No bible. No character profiles. No prior episode context (for cold coverage). What's on the page is what's on the page.

### Evaluation Criteria

| Criterion | Description | Weight |
|-----------|-------------|--------|
| **Cold open effectiveness** | Does it hook? Does it set the tone? Does it end on a turn? | High |
| **Act break quality** | Does each break compel forward momentum? Do stakes escalate? | High |
| **A-story engine** | Is the primary plot driving the episode? Is it clear? Is it engaging? | High |
| **B-story relevance** | Does the B-story illuminate the A-story? Could it be cut without loss? | Medium |
| **C-story advancement** | Does the mythology advance? Is it felt, not explained? | Medium |
| **Character consistency** | Do characters sound like themselves? Are their actions motivated? | High |
| **Dialogue quality** | Is it subtext? Is it specific? Is it economical? Is it distinctive? | High |
| **Pacing** | Does the episode breathe? Are there scenes that drag? Are there scenes that rush? | Medium |
| **Emotional impact** | Does the episode produce at least two distinct emotions? Does it land? | High |
| **Final image** | Does it linger? Does it make the viewer want the next episode? | Medium |
| **Self-containment** | Could a new viewer understand this episode without prior context? | Medium |
| **Format discipline** | No camera directions, no emotional parentheticals, no interiority? | High |

## Season-Level Coverage

### When to Run

- After all episodes are assembled
- After per-episode revisions are complete
- Before season lock

### What Lara Reads

All assembled episodes in order. No bible. No character profiles. No production notes. She reads the season as a viewer would — one episode after another.

### Evaluation Criteria

| Criterion | Description | Weight |
|-----------|-------------|--------|
| **Season arc** | Does the season tell a complete story? Is the arc satisfying? | High |
| **A-story resolution** | Is the primary arc resolved (or deliberately left open)? | High |
| **B-story resolution** | Are the secondary arcs given closure? | Medium |
| **C-story/mythology** | Is the mythology clear? Is it earned? Is it surprising? | Medium |
| **Character arcs** | Do the main characters change across the season? Is the change earned? | High |
| **Pilot effectiveness** | Does Episode 1 hook? Does it establish the world? Does it make the viewer want Episode 2? | High |
| **Mid-season momentum** | Does the season sag in the middle? Is the mid-season twist effective? | High |
| **Finale payoff** | Does the finale deliver? Does it resolve the season's central question? Does it create a new question (if continuing)? | High |
| **Thematic coherence** | Does the season have a thematic argument? Is it consistent? Is it earned? | Medium |
| **Callback resolution** | Are all seeded callbacks paid off? Are there orphaned seeds? | Medium |
| **Voice consistency** | Does the show sound the same across all episodes? | High |
| **Pacing across episodes** | Does the season breathe? Are there episodes that drag? Are there episodes that rush? | Medium |

## TV-Specific Evaluation Criteria

### Pilot Effectiveness

The pilot (Episode 1) has a unique job:
- **Establish the world** — the viewer must understand the show's reality
- **Introduce the characters** — the viewer must care about at least one character
- **Set the tone** — the viewer must know what kind of show this is
- **Create the central question** — the viewer must want to know the answer
- **Hook** — the viewer must want Episode 2

**The "10-Page Test":** Within the first 10 pages of the pilot, the viewer should recognize that they're watching something good. If they're not hooked by page 10, the pilot is failing.

### Mid-Season Momentum

The middle episodes (typically 4-7) are the most vulnerable:
- The novelty of the pilot has worn off
- The finale is still far away
- The audience needs a reason to keep watching

**The mid-season twist** (usually Episode 5 or 6) is the antidote — the moment where the audience's understanding shifts irrevocably. If the mid-season twist is weak, the season sags.

**Signs of mid-season sag:**
- B-story scenes that don't connect to the A-story
- Mythology episodes that are all exposition
- Character development that feels like wheel-spinning
- The audience can predict what happens next

### Finale Payoff

The season finale has the highest stakes:
- It must resolve the A-story (or deliberately refuse to resolve it)
- It must provide closure for B-stories
- It must deliver on the season's thematic argument
- It must leave the audience satisfied (or productively unsatisfied)
- If there's a next season: it must create a new question

**The "Next Day" Test:** The morning after the finale, does the viewer think about the show? Does they want to tell someone about it? Does they want the next season?

## Pre-Script Editorial Review (Before Production)

Before any episodes are written, run the season plan through editorial review.

### The Three Personas (Adapted for TV)

#### Lara Marsh — TV Script Reader (14 years)

- **Focus:** Cold coverage of the season plan as a structural document
- **Questions:** Does the season arc work? Are the episodes balanced? Is the A/B/C structure viable? Where does the viewer lose the thread?
- **Output:** Coverage with verdict

#### Dr. Elena Vasquez — Literary Fiction Editor (20 years)

- **Focus:** Thematic architecture
- **Questions:** Is the season's thematic argument coherent? Do the episodes build on each other? Does the show earn its ambition?
- **Output:** Editorial assessment with verdict

#### Marcus Webb — Development Executive (12 years)

- **Focus:** Marketability and producibility
- **Questions:** Can this be shot? What's the budget? Who's the audience? Is the format commercially viable? Are there enough episodes? Too many?
- **Output:** Development notes with verdict

### Protocol

1. Present the season plan to each persona independently
2. Each persona produces coverage — verdict + specific issues
3. Synthesize feedback across all three personas
4. Revise the season plan
5. Re-present to all three personas
6. Iterate until all three return positive verdicts
7. Lock the season plan

### Success Criteria

- All 3 personas return positive assessments
- No structural issues of comparable severity remain
- The locked season plan becomes the production blueprint

## Integration with the Production Pipeline

```
Season Plan Creation
  → Editorial Review (this protocol)
    → Season Plan locked
      → Per-Episode Production (episode architect → writer → critics → revision)
        → Per-Episode Coverage (Lara Marsh, cold)
          → Per-Season Coverage (Lara Marsh, full read)
            → Season Lock
```

## Key Differences from Screenplay Coverage

| Aspect | Screenplay Coverage | TV Coverage |
|--------|-------------------|-------------|
| Input | One script (120 pages) | 10 episodes (600 pages) |
| Evaluation | Single document | Episode-by-episode + full season |
| Pacing | Three-act structure | Episode rhythm + season rhythm |
| Character | One arc | Multiple arcs across episodes |
| Callbacks | Intra-script | Cross-episode, cross-season |
| Cold open | N/A | Critical evaluation point |
| Act breaks | N/A | Critical evaluation point |
| "Would I watch next?" | N/A | Core evaluation question |
| Mid-season | N/A | Momentum evaluation |
| Finale | Ending | Payoff evaluation |
