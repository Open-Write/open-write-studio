# Dual-Voice Guidance

*How to manage two POV voices in one work so they feel like the same author.*

---

## The Principle

Two voices, one authorial sensibility. The reader of the assembled screenplay should perceive a single intelligence rendering two different consciousnesses, the way Cusk's *Outline* trilogy and *Second Place* are unmistakably the same author in different registers.

---

## The "One Writer or Two" Test

Write 3 consecutive scenes — voice A, voice B, voice A — and have Lara read them cold. The question:

> "Does this feel like one writer or two?"

If Lara can tell the tracks were written by different hands (or different models in different modes), the voices haven't been unified yet.

### Cross-Track Test Example

The test was run with three scenes:
1. **Mira in the Lab** (Human) — 45 lines. Mira alone at night, finding a pattern in anomaly data.
2. **Solae Watches** (Velai) — 37 lines. Solae on the deep substrate, receiving an anomalous signal.
3. **Theo Wakes Up** (Human) — 31 lines. Theo at night, standing in the hallway.

**Lara Marsh's verdict: "One Writer. This reads as a single authorial sensibility operating across two registers. The question is not close."**

---

## What to Look For

### Sentence Rhythm
Do both voices use similar cadence patterns? If voice A uses short declarative sentences and voice B uses flowing periodic sentences, the reader will feel the seam.

### Detail Density
Do both voices notice similar amounts of physical detail? If track A renders three sensory details per beat and track B renders seven, the texture shifts when you cut between tracks.

### Emotional Register
Do both voices handle emotion with similar restraint? If track A lets silence do the work and track B names every feeling, the emotional contract changes with each track switch.

### Action Line Discipline
Do both voices obey the same formatting rules? Same parenthetical usage. Same body-anchor conventions. Same line length targets.

### Structural Rhyming
Do both voices build parallel emotional architectures? In one validated cross-track test, all three scenes featured reaching-toward-connection-then-pulling-back. This is worldview, not technique.

### Shared Sound Design
Do both voices use sound the same way? In one validated test: server hum, vent-hum, house hum across all three worlds. Sound never commented on, present as environment.

---

## What to Adjust

| Problem | Fix |
|---------|-----|
| Voice B too lyrical vs. voice A spareness | Tighten voice B — cut decorative language |
| Voice B too clinical vs. voice A warmth | Add sensory detail to voice B — body anchors, temperature, texture |
| Sentence rhythms differ | Identify the dominant rhythm and adjust the weaker voice to match |
| Detail density mismatch | Count sensory details per beat in 3 scenes from each track. Normalize. |
| Emotional register mismatch | Identify which voice's emotional approach is stronger. Adjust the other to match. |
| No structural rhyming | Identify the shared emotional pattern and ensure both tracks express it |

**Rule:** Always adjust the weaker voice, never the locked primary voice.

## The Body-Anchor Technique

The body-anchor is the bridge between voices. Both voices track physical behavior — hands, limbs, posture. The reader's attention follows bodies across transitions, not worlds.

**What they share:** Both are physical. Both are specific. Both are silent in the right way. The body-anchor grounds abstract emotion in physical sensation, and that grounding works regardless of whether the body is human or alien.

### How to Use Body-Anchors as Bridge

1. **At track transitions:** End a scene with a body action. Begin the next scene with a body action. The reader's attention follows the bodies across the transition.
2. **At emotional peaks:** When emotion runs high in both tracks, anchor it in the body.
3. **At convergence:** When the two tracks approach the same crisis, use parallel body-anchors.

## POV-Specific Voice Rules

### Track A (Primary Voice)

- Characters speak only when they must. Silence carries meaning.
- Action lines render the gaps — what characters don't say, don't do, don't acknowledge.
- White space is structural.
- Body-anchors: hands, eyes, breath, spine, jaw.

### Shared Rules

- No camera directions in either track.
- No interiority in action lines.
- No emotional parentheticals.
- Both tracks use the same sentence-level discipline (not the same sentences, but the same standards).
- Both tracks earn their details with equal precision.

## Application in Production

1. **Voice selection:** Test each voice independently using [`skills/voice_experiment_protocol.md`](voice_experiment_protocol.md)
2. **Lock each voice:** Produce a locked spec for each
3. **Cross-track test:** Write 3 consecutive scenes in alternating voices. Have Lara read cold.
4. **If "two writers":** Adjust the weaker voice. Never the locked primary voice.
5. **Iterate:** Run the cross-track test again. Repeat until "One Writer."
6. **Proceed to full generation:** Only after the cross-track test passes.

---

## Key Insight

> "Two voices can share authorial DNA without collapsing into one register. The voices are distinct. But the body-anchor discipline, the shared silence, and the structural rhyming prove a single authorial intelligence behind both tracks."
