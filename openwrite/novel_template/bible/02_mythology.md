## II. THE WORLD AND ITS MYTHOLOGY

**The world.** [Describe the setting. Is it our world? A secondary world? Near-future? Far-future? What makes it distinct? Ground the reader in a reality they recognize, then show what's different.]

**The central speculative element.** [What is the key science-fiction, fantasy, or speculative conceit? Describe it in concrete terms. What is it? Where did it come from? What does it want? The more specific, the better.]

**Their abilities (kept restrained for narrative discipline).** [What can the speculative element do? List specific abilities with their costs and limitations. Restraint is essential — every ability must have a price.]

**The stakes.** [What happens if the protagonist fails? What happens if they succeed? What is the cost of each outcome?]

**The faction/force dynamics.** [Who are the opposing forces? What do they each want? Why can't they agree? Both sides should have a legitimate point — the conflict comes from incompatible goods, not good vs. evil.]

**The institutional context.** [What organizations, governments, or power structures exist in this world? How do they relate to the protagonist and the central conflict?]

**The world outside the main setting.** [What does the broader world look like? This grounds the story in a reality the reader recognizes. What is the backdrop of news, politics, and culture against which the story unfolds?]
